## Messaging extraction (verbatim)

**STRATEGIC LAYER**
1.   Angle: "My neighbor said this tallow made her look 10 years younger." - Headline-led neighbor testimony: an existing tallow user switches to F-Balm, reports a better feel, and reveals she is perceived roughly ten years younger.
2.   Market Awareness Stage: "Beef tallow? No way! Like I use, that's what I use." - Solution-aware moving toward Product-aware. The category is known and used; the ad converts the proof subject from another brand to F-Balm.
3.   Sophistication Stage: "bleached or deodorized or from even from China" - Stage 4. Differentiation uses direct competitor switching, texture, sourcing, non-bleached processing, nutrient preservation, review volume, and anti-hype qualification.
4.   Tone of Voice: "no guarantees you might look 10 years younger" - Founder as casual neighbor-storyteller in a conversational, humorous, hedged, self-interrupting, anti-hype register.

**AVATAR LAYER**
5.   Primary Avatar: "I don't want to do Botox or anything unnatural" - Midlife women seeking natural younger-looking results and a simpler routine instead of Botox.
6.   Sub-Avatar: "I'm gonna give away the other brand" - The creative directly serves multiple segments:
7.   "I'm a vegetarian but I use it" - Vegetarians willing to use effective animal-derived skincare.
8.   "I've ditched all my other products" - Routine minimalists.
9.   "Yours isn't greasy" - Existing tallow users dissatisfied with texture.
10.                Age (explicit + implicit): "I'm actually closer to 60 than like 50." - Explicitly late 50s, first perceived as 50; the headline claims a ten-year-younger appearance.
11.                Gender (explicit + implicit): "my attractive 50-year-old neighbor" - The proof subject is a woman through she/her language; Botox and facial-use cues further position the creative toward women.
12.                Daily Occurrences: "I put it on my face before bed." - Nighttime facial application is the named routine moment.
13.                Habits / Lifestyle Callouts: "I'm a vegetarian" - The avatar is vegetarian, uses tallow despite that identity, had multiple products, used a competing tallow brand, and refuses Botox.

**PSYCHOLOGY LAYER**
14.                Pain Points - Physical: "Yours isn't greasy" - Competing-product greasiness is the only specific physical or sensory pain named.
15.                Pain Points - Emotional: Not addressed.
16.                Pain Points - Social / Relational: "you might get compliments" - The desired social outcome is appearance compliments and being perceived younger.
17.                Pain Points - Identity: "I don't want to do Botox or anything unnatural" - The avatar wants visible improvement without violating a natural-aging identity.
18.                False Beliefs: "This is even better." - The narrative challenges the belief that all tallow brands perform similarly or that an existing tallow routine cannot be improved.
19.                Felt Solutions Tried: "I've ditched all my other products" - Prior solutions include multiple skincare products, a competing tallow brand, and Botox considered but rejected.
20.                "I'm gonna give away the other brand" - Competing tallow balm.
21.                "I don't want to do Botox" - Rejected Botox.
22.                New Mechanism Introduced: "keeps the nutrients, delivers the benefits to your skin" - Premium rancher sourcing and avoidance of bleaching or deodorizing preserve the nutrients that deliver skin benefits.

**PROMISE & OFFER LAYER**
23.                Core Promise: "made her look 10 years younger" - A hedged ten-year-younger appearance, better product feel, and possible compliments.
24.                Results Timeline: "I see her two weeks later." - Two weeks, delivered through the neighbor's comparative experience rather than as a formal guarantee.
25.                Proof Elements: "There's a ton ton of reviews on this stuff" - Proof includes the neighbor story, competitor switch, texture comparison, age reveal, review volume, in-house production, premium sourcing, and results claim.
26.                Offer Structure (price, discount, bundle, guarantee, urgency): "If you don't like it, money back guarantee." - Money-back guarantee and no-downside framing. Price, discount, bundle, subscription, bonus, scarcity, urgency, and guarantee window are Not addressed.
27.                CTA Copy + Positioning: "give it a try" - Soft trial CTA at the close, supported immediately by the guarantee and no-downside positioning; the supplied script ends mid-closing sentence.

# Winner: Weird Botox hack / hot 50-year-old neighbor

Source: "Top Ads All Time" client doc, filed into the vault 2026-08-28. Proven top performer for The Tallowed Truth / F-Balm; messaging extraction was already done in the source document and is preserved verbatim below.

## Script (verbatim)

Okay, my 50-year-old neighbor who is pretty hot, uh, and I say that respectfully, she's married, but she's attractive. Got chatting to her, she's asking what I do. I was telling her about beef tallow and we make beef tallow products and she was like, “No way, I use beef tallow and I don't use,” she's like, “I used to use all this expensive stuff,” she's like, “I used to do the Botox,” and, uh, she's like, “Now I just use this beef tallow and I love it.” And I was like, “No way, that's awesome.” And it's kind of cool because like people make jokes about, oh, like, “It replaces this” or “It replaces that.” But these are the things that we hear from people. When I first started making the F-Balm, I thought it was going to be more geared for dudes and I was like, “Oh, I want to make clean stuff that doesn't disrupt hormones and like tank your testosterone and all this stuff.” And while men do use this, it's largely women, you know, 30, 40, 50, 60, even 70 who use it on their face and they love it and it's a simple product that can replace a lot of, you know, more expensive or more traditional like facial or anti-aging creams or serums that are kind of just, um, you know, mixed up from a factory or whatever. So we make this stuff, premium beef tallow, women love it. If you want to try it, if you want to ditch your Botox and you want to be a hot 50-year-old, uh, no downsides to trying this. If you don't like it, money-back guarantee. But I think you will and you can try it below. Uh, money-back guarantee. I will see you there.

## Messaging extraction (verbatim)

══════════════════════════════ MESSAGING EXTRACTION ══════════════════════════════
STRATEGIC LAYER
● Angle: Category replacement via unsolicited stranger endorsement — an attractive 50-year-old neighbour independently reveals she abandoned Botox and expensive skincare for beef tallow. Anchored in "I used to do the Botox... Now I just use this beef tallow and I love it."
● Market Awareness Stage: Solution-aware. The neighbour already knows the category and uses it: "No way, I use beef tallow." The creative is not teaching what tallow is — it's demonstrating what tallow displaced. Reinforced by the replacement framing: "a simple product that can replace a lot of, you know, more expensive or more traditional like facial or anti-aging creams or serums."
● Sophistication Stage: Stage 3–4. Differentiates on positioning against the incumbent category rather than a new mechanism: "kind of just, um, you know, mixed up from a factory or whatever" versus "premium beef tallow." No new biological mechanism is introduced.
● Tone of Voice: Speaker archetype — founder as unfiltered peer, narrating a real encounter. Register — rambling, self-correcting, slightly transgressive, with an in-built social hedge: "who is pretty hot, uh, and I say that respectfully, she's married, but she's attractive." Heavy filler and mid-sentence pivots throughout; conspicuously unscripted.
AVATAR LAYER
● Primary Avatar: Women using expensive skincare and/or cosmetic injectables who want a simpler natural replacement — "largely women... who use it on their face and they love it."
● Sub-Avatar 1: Botox users looking to stop — "if you want to ditch your Botox." ● Sub-Avatar 2: Women spending on premium skincare — "I used to use all this expensive stuff." ● Sub-Avatar 3: Men concerned with hormonal exposure in personal care — "I thought it was going to be more geared for dudes and I was like, 'Oh, I want to make clean stuff that doesn't disrupt hormones and like tank your testosterone.'" Explicitly de-prioritised: "while men do use this, it's largely women."
● Age (explicit + implicit): Explicit — "my 50-year-old neighbor"; the full stated range "30, 40, 50, 60, even 70"; and the aspiration "you want to be a hot 50-year-old." Implicit — Botox history and "anti-aging creams or serums" signal an established, long-standing routine.
● Gender (explicit + implicit): Explicit — "it's largely women." Implicit — Botox, facial serums and anti-aging creams are gender-coded categories; the male sub-avatar is named only to be set aside.
● Daily Occurrences: "use it on their face." No other day-in-the-life moments named.
● Habits / Lifestyle Callouts: Previously used Botox ("I used to do the Botox"); previously bought premium skincare ("all this expensive stuff"); now uses a single product ("Now I just use this beef tallow").
PSYCHOLOGY LAYER
● Pain Points — Physical: Not addressed.
● Pain Points — Emotional: Not addressed.
● Pain Points — Social / Relational: Not addressed directly; the inverse is the aspiration — being the attractive 50-year-old neighbour people notice.
● Pain Points — Identity: Implied through the Botox exit — "if you want to ditch your Botox" positions injectables as something to be free of rather than something to acquire.
● False Beliefs: That results require expense or injectables — reframed by "I used to use all this expensive stuff... Now I just use this beef tallow and I love it." Secondarily, that a skincare shelf requires many products — reframed by "a simple product that can replace a lot of."
● Felt Solutions Tried: Botox ("I used to do the Botox"); expensive skincare ("all this expensive stuff"); "anti-aging creams or serums."
● New Mechanism Introduced: Not addressed. No biological or causal mechanism is offered — the differentiator is sourcing and simplicity ("premium beef tallow," "clean stuff that doesn't disrupt hormones"), not an explanation of how it works.
PROMISE & OFFER LAYER
● Core Promise: Aspirational and jokingly framed — "if you want to ditch your Botox and you want to be a hot 50-year-old." Softened by the risk framing rather than a stated outcome: "no downsides to trying this."
● Results Timeline: Not addressed.
● Proof Elements: Unsolicited neighbour endorsement; the Botox-abandonment detail; category-wide adoption claim ("it's largely women, you know, 30, 40, 50, 60, even 70 who use it on their face and they love it"); recurring-feedback claim ("these are the things that we hear from people"); sourcing credential ("premium beef tallow"); founder-manufacturer credential ("we make beef tallow products" / "we make this stuff").
● Offer Structure: Money-back guarantee only, stated twice — "If you don't like it, money-back guarantee" and "Uh, money-back guarantee." Risk-reversal framing: "no downsides to trying this." No price, discount, bundle, subscription, bonus, scarcity or urgency mechanic.
● CTA Copy + Positioning: "If you want to try it... you can try it below... I will see you there." Positioned as low-commitment trial with a soft confidence signal ("But I think you will"), landing at the very end alongside the guarantee.

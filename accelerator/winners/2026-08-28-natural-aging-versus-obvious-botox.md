# Winner: Natural aging versus obvious Botox

Source: "Top Ads All Time" client doc, filed into the vault 2026-08-28. Proven top performer for The Tallowed Truth / F-Balm; messaging extraction was already done in the source document and is preserved verbatim below.

So I was standing beside this woman at a concert, we were on like the floor and she was maybe, it was hard to tell, but 45 years old, mid forties, but she clearly had, you know, the Botox, uh, the lip fillers, and she was kind of asking about what I do and I was saying I use, I use beef, or I make beef tallow products for skincare. My mom's a great example, like she just looks, she's not trying to stay young, but she looks healthy and vibrant, and that is a much more attractive quality than the people who are putting the Botox into their face. And that's what the F-Balm helps you do, and that's why there's like thousands of women who have tried this, and they say they're ditching other products and they're trying this instead. It's a natural aging, it's a natural glow, it's a natural rejuvenation. So we've got thousands of five-star reviews on this at this point. The F-Balm, premium stuff that we make in-house. I started this brand, um, natural ingredients. You can read the reviews and you can check it out.

## Messaging extraction (verbatim)

**STRATEGIC LAYER**
1.   Angle: "women can age gracefully" - Natural, healthy, vibrant aging is positioned as more attractive than obvious Botox and fillers.
2.   Market Awareness Stage: "I don't think that works as well as my Botox" - Solution-aware. The avatar knows both Botox and tallow but doubts the natural solution can match the incumbent.
3.   Sophistication Stage: "a natural aging, it's a natural glow, it's a natural rejuvenation" - Stage 4. Differentiation comes from a counter-category aesthetic and identity - natural movement and vitality instead of frozen, obvious intervention - supported by reviews and replacement stories.
4.   Tone of Voice: "I think we all know what I'm talking about. It's too obvious." - Founder as blunt observer / cultural commentator in a conversational, judgmental, provocative, anecdotal register.

**AVATAR LAYER**
5.   Primary Avatar: "women can age gracefully" - Midlife women who want to look healthy and vibrant without an obvious injected or filled appearance.
6.   Sub-Avatar: "I'm always gonna need my Botox" - The creative targets committed Botox and filler users, plus women trying hard to stay young and women replacing other products.
7.   "she clearly had, you know, the Botox, uh, the lip fillers" - Women using injectables and fillers.
8.   "they're ditching other products" - Routine switchers seeking a natural option.
9.   Age (explicit + implicit): "45 years old, mid forties" - Explicitly mid-40s, with the broader contrast built around aging and trying to stay young.
10.                Gender (explicit + implicit): "women can age gracefully" - Women are the explicit target and proof group; the founder's mother is the positive model.
11.                Daily Occurrences: Not addressed.
12.                Habits / Lifestyle Callouts: "I'm always gonna need my Botox" - The avatar uses Botox and lip fillers, evaluates tallow skeptically, and may be replacing other skincare products.

**PSYCHOLOGY LAYER**
13.                Pain Points - Physical: "her face actually changed when she started to smile" - The physical downside is an obvious, tight, altered appearance with unnatural facial movement.
14.                Pain Points - Emotional: "people trying so hard to look younger" - The emotional pain is effortful anxiety about staying young and overcorrecting aging.
15.                Pain Points - Social / Relational: "she was, she was, would have been attractive, but it was too obvious"- The social cost is being perceived as less attractive because cosmetic work looks obvious.
16.                Pain Points - Identity: "she's not trying to stay young, but she looks healthy and vibrant" - The identity conflict is chasing youth versus accepting one's age while remaining healthy, vibrant, and attractive.
17.                False Beliefs: "I don't think that works as well as my Botox" - The ad challenges the belief that Botox is necessary or that matching its obvious effect is the right success criterion.
18.                Felt Solutions Tried: "the Botox, uh, the lip fillers" - Botox, lip fillers, and other unnamed skincare products are explicitly acknowledged.
19.                New Mechanism Introduced: "a natural glow, it's a natural rejuvenation" - The reframe is not frozen youth but healthy-looking natural rejuvenation; a biological mechanism is Not addressed.

**PROMISE & OFFER LAYER**
20.                Core Promise: "age gracefully" - A healthy, vibrant, naturally attractive appearance without obvious Botox or fillers.
21.                Results Timeline: Not addressed.
22.                Proof Elements: "thousands of five-star reviews" - Proof includes the concert contrast, the founder's mother as a model, thousands of women replacing products, review volume, founder identity, in-house production, and natural ingredients.
23.                Offer Structure (price, discount, bundle, guarantee, urgency): Not addressed.
24.                CTA Copy + Positioning: "You can read the reviews and you can check it out." - A soft review-led exploration CTA at the close; no explicit buy-now language or offer mechanic is stated.

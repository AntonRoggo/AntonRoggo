## Messaging extraction (verbatim)

**STRATEGIC LAYER**
1.   Angle: "it's not a scam, like it's legit" - Legitimacy proof for online skeptics: a recognizable founder, real-world encounter, in-house manufacturing, fast fulfillment, reviews, quality contrast, and guarantee make the brand trustworthy.
2.   Market Awareness Stage: "I've been... I've been wanting to try it." - Product-aware. The woman recognizes the ads and founder and wants the product, but online-purchase skepticism prevents conversion.
3.   Sophistication Stage: "This is the triple-purified beef tallow that we use in our formula." - Stage 4. The creative stacks real-person proof, spontaneous sampling, operations, delivery speed, review scale, use-case breadth, purification, sourcing contrast, premium price defense, and guarantee.
4.   Tone of Voice: "we have a real business" - Founder as reassuring peer and transparent insider in a spontaneous, repetitive, candid, proof-heavy register.

**AVATAR LAYER**
5.   Primary Avatar: "I don't buy that much stuff, like, online" - Older, skeptical online shoppers who have seen the ads and want F-Balm but question legitimacy or product value.
6.   Sub-Avatar: "you've tried all these serums and things to look younger" - The creative serves multiple narrower need states:
7.   "dry hands" - People with dry hands.
8.   "stretch marks, on tattoos" - Users targeting stretch marks or tattoo appearance.
9.   "scars, eczema" - People with scars or eczema.
10.                "under eye" - Under-eye and younger-looking facial shoppers.
11.                Age (explicit + implicit): "Maybe she was like 60." - The encountered prospect is estimated at around 60; looking younger and under-eye concerns reinforce an older adult target.
12.                Gender (explicit + implicit): "I saw this woman" - The prospect is explicitly a woman; the broader review use cases are presented without gender restriction.
13.                Daily Occurrences: "Use this on your face. It goes on really well like if your skin is a little bit moist" - Facial application to slightly moist skin is instructed; dry hands and other body uses are also named.
14.                Habits / Lifestyle Callouts: "I don't shop a lot." - The avatar rarely buys online, sees repeated ads, researches reviews, and has tried serums for a younger look.

**PSYCHOLOGY LAYER**
15.                Pain Points - Physical: "softer skin" - The physical stack includes lack of softness, irritation, stretch marks, faded tattoos, under-eye concerns, scars, eczema, and visible aging.
16.                "great for irritation" - Skin irritation.
17.                "stretch marks" - Stretch marks.
18.                "bringing back the color in their tattoos" - Faded-looking tattoos.
19.                "scars, eczema" - Scars and eczema.
20.                Pain Points - Emotional: "but you're skeptical" - Online purchasing distrust, scam concern, uncertainty about delivery, and skepticism after trying other products are the emotional barriers.
21.                Pain Points - Social / Relational: Not addressed.
22.                Pain Points - Identity: "I don't shop a lot." - The avatar sees herself as a cautious, non-impulsive shopper rather than an easy online buyer.
23.                False Beliefs: "we're not a scam" - The ad corrects the beliefs that the brand may be illegitimate, online delivery may be unreliable, and cheap imported tallow is comparable.
24.                Felt Solutions Tried: "you've tried all these serums and things to look younger" - Serums and other younger-looking skincare products are explicitly acknowledged as prior attempts.
25.                New Mechanism Introduced: "triple-purified beef tallow" - Triple purification, in-house manufacture, premium ingredients, and rejection of bleached imported tallow explain the claimed quality difference.

**PROMISE & OFFER LAYER**
26.                Core Promise: "rejuvenating their skin" - Softer, rejuvenated skin with broad benefits across irritation, marks, tattoos, under eyes, scars, eczema, and visible aging.
27.                Results Timeline: Not addressed.
28.                Proof Elements: "over 10,000 five-star reviews now" - Proof includes the chance encounter, spontaneous free sampling, real founder, in-house production, warehouses, three-to-five-day delivery, review scale, use-case testimonials, ingredient spend, and review-reading.
29.                "we actually do make this stuff" - Manufacturing legitimacy.
30.                "within like three to five days for most people" - Delivery proof, not a results timeline.
31.                "the results speak for themselves" - Asserted proof.
32.                Offer Structure (price, discount, bundle, guarantee, urgency): "If you don't like it, we'll literally just give you your money back." - Money-back guarantee, missing-out language, and premium-price positioning versus a $12 Amazon balm. Exact price, discount, bundle, subscription, bonus, scarcity, urgency, and guarantee window are Not addressed.
33.                CTA Copy + Positioning: "F-Balm, click the link below" - Direct CTA for skeptical prospects, placed after legitimacy proof, reviews, premium-value defense, and the money-back guarantee.

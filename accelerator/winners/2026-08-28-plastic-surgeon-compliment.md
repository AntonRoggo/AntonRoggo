## Messaging extraction (verbatim)

**STRATEGIC LAYER**
1.   Angle: "I'm getting compliments from a plastic surgeon" - Borrowed expert validation: a 70-year-old customer's skin earns a compliment from a specialist in improving faces.
2.   Market Awareness Stage: "This is the product she was talking about." - Product-aware. The testimonial and F-Balm are the center; category education is minimal and the ad moves quickly to trial.
3.   Sophistication Stage: "this guy's specialty is literally making people's faces look better" - Stage 4. Differentiation relies on unusual authority-adjacent proof, customer email, small-batch production, processing quality, reviews, and anti-surgery contrast.
4.   Tone of Voice: "All jokes aside people are loving this stuff." - Founder as excited storyteller / proof curator in a playful, conversational, confident, lightly irreverent register.

**AVATAR LAYER**
5.   Primary Avatar: "if you want to avoid the plastic surgery" - Older skincare users seeking younger-looking results and compliments without plastic surgery.
6.   Sub-Avatar: "This woman was 70" - The narrower segment is women around 70, including those managing scarring and already consulting plastic surgeons.
7.   "she was in there for some scarring" - People concerned with facial scarring.
8.   Age (explicit + implicit): "This woman was 70" - Explicitly 70, with younger-looking results positioned as the desired outcome.
9.   Gender (explicit + implicit): "This woman was 70" - The proof subject is explicitly female; the surgeon's wife is also a tallow user.
10.                Daily Occurrences: "she use beef tallow on her face" - Facial application is named without a specific time of day.
11.                Habits / Lifestyle Callouts: "she was in there for some scarring talking to a plastic surgeon" - The avatar seeks professional input about scarring and already applies beef tallow to her face.

**PSYCHOLOGY LAYER**
12.                Pain Points - Physical: "some scarring" - Scarring and visible aging are the named physical concerns.
13.                "look younger" - Visible aging.
14.                Pain Points - Emotional: Not addressed.
15.                Pain Points - Social / Relational: "getting compliments from a plastic surgeon" - The desired relational payoff is expert-level validation of the customer's appearance.
16.                Pain Points - Identity: "don't get the plastic surgery" - The identity tension is wanting improved looks without becoming a plastic-surgery patient.
17.                False Beliefs: "don't get the plastic surgery, try some of the F-Balm" - The ad challenges the assumption that optimizing facial appearance requires surgery.
18.                Felt Solutions Tried: "talking to a plastic surgeon" - Professional consultation for scarring is directly named; an actual surgery is not stated.
19.                New Mechanism Introduced: "it's not bleached or deodorized" - The closest mechanism is high-quality, small-batch tallow preserved without bleaching or deodorizing; no biological explanation is provided.

**PROMISE & OFFER LAYER**
20.                Core Promise: "look younger feel better" - A younger-looking, better-feeling appearance that can attract compliments while avoiding surgery.
21.                Results Timeline: Not addressed.
22.                Proof Elements: "feedback from a plastic surgeon from one of our customers" - Proof includes a 70-year-old customer email, a plastic surgeon's reaction and family tallow use, in-house small-batch production, processing claims, and reviews.
23.                Offer Structure (price, discount, bundle, guarantee, urgency): Not addressed.
24.                CTA Copy + Positioning: "give this a try read some of the reviews" - Direct trial CTA positioned as a non-surgical route to compliments, followed by review-reading and the final link instruction.

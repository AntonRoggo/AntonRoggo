## 1. What 002 adds to the spine

- **A second wound on top of the first.** The catch is the same silent Tuesday as 001. Then a new act: the families take her side without being asked, the couple sends a message through her mother, the message calls her selfish and offers to name a daughter after her, and "she's pregnant with a boy." That one line is the best-written beat in either story: the insult, the lie, and the punchline in five words. It also gives the story a clock (the wedding in two months) that 001 does not have.
- **The mirror does not concede.** "I almost believed them. Maybe I was the bitter one." Almost. Where 001 says "I got why he did it," 002 stops one step short, and the guide finishes it: "Nothing is wrong with you. You disappeared. The heartbreak buried you. And we're going to dig you out." That is the operator's zero-accountability rule in a winning ad, two beats after the mirror.
- **Cortisol instead of estrogen.** "Months of cortisol. Every glass of wine to fall asleep. Every feeling you swallowed to keep it together." The clog cause is the wound itself. The herb jobs are re-tied to it: "the sludge the stress packed in," "the circulation the heartbreak shut down."
- **The sludge gets renamed by cause.** "You don't have heartbreak weight. You have stress sludge." (001: menopause weight, menopause sludge.) The slot is [cause] weight, [cause] sludge.
- **The promise markers match the progression.** "By week one your face will deflate. By week three your jawline is back." Week one is the face, week three is the jawline. 001 had a mismatch here (week two promised, week one delivered).
- **The wedding is a beat she skips.** Week six is their wedding day; she is at the lake with Caleb, phone off, and reports it secondhand: a hundred paid for, twelve showed, most for the food. The payoff moves to the fall festival two weeks later. Reporting the wedding instead of attending it is what keeps her from looking like she went to gloat.
- **The betrayer is the one who compliments.** At 001 the ex-husband says "you look incredible." At 002 the bride does, by the lemonade stand, with nobody talking to them. Same line, worse for the person saying it.
- **The circle is a peer, not a child.** The roommate, "we're the same age and you're glowing." The witness slot scales with the lead's age.
- **The truth adds "consequences."** "I never told a single person to stay home. Everyone who knew them chose for themselves. That's not revenge. That's consequences." It answers the hook's accusation and keeps her clean.
- **The enemy in the close scales with age.** 001: Ozempic and hormone therapy. 002: Ozempic and another self-care routine.

## 3. Timeline, checked

- 28 now, best friend since 12, "best friend of sixteen years." Holds.
- The catch on a Tuesday. "Wine every night since," "fifteen pounds since that Tuesday," "tried for months," "looked at myself for the first time in months." So months pass before the offer. The baby's sex is known, which needs the pregnancy at four to five months, so she was pregnant at or before the catch. Holds, and it makes the betrayal older than the Tuesday, which is worse, which is right.
- The wedding was "two months away" when the offer came. Aunt arrives that week, bottle on day three, six weeks, week four is "two weeks away," week six is the wedding. Holds exactly.
- Fall festival two weeks after. Roommate a month after that. Holds.

**Caught:**
1. **The number.** "Fifteen pounds since that Tuesday," then "down twelve" at week four and "down sixteen" at week five. She loses one more than she gained. Either she was carrying some before the Tuesday (say so in the mirror) or week five is "down fourteen." A viewer counting will notice.
2. **The dose.** "Two drops under your tongue every morning." A fourth dose across the bank (one full dropper; half and half; one to two droppers on Amazon; now two drops). The operator has to pick one.
3. **"They get drained. Every single week"** against a daily dose. Same slip as 001.
4. **"Brazilian women have known three of them for a hundred years."** Same origin slip as 001. The guide's clinic credential is what holds.

## 4. What to watch when reusing

- Hook 1 gives away the offer, Hook 3 gives away the ending ("made sure I got the last word"), Hook 2 gives away nothing but the betrayal and the aunt. The operator says Hook 2 won. Worth remembering when writing hooks for this spine: the version that withholds beat the versions that told.
- The town is a character. "Small town in the South," "her family less than a mile away," "everyone knew why," "half the town turned to look." Every public beat needs the town for the payoff to land. If the setting moves to a city, the "twelve showed up" beat and the festival lose their teeth.
- Names: Renata, Megan, Caleb. No collisions with 001 (Renee, Yolanda, Marcus) or the cookout script (Renee, Marcus, Tanya). See the naming register note in [[_story-library]].

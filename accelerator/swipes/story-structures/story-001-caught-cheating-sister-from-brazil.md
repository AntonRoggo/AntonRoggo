## 1. Hook formula

All three hooks share one shape: **[the wound in one line] + [two ages] + "The only reason I [didn't crumble / am not still broken / was wrong] was what my sister flew in from Brazil to show me."**

The last line is the engine. It names a specific person, a specific place, and a mysterious thing, and promises all three will pay off. V1 is the wound itself. V2 is seeing the other woman. V3 is the fear after (die alone, no man would want me). Same HOLD for all three.

## 5. What to watch when reusing

- **Beat 3, the concession.** "I got why he did it. I didn't recognize the woman in the mirror either." This is the operator's most successful story, and it contains the line the operator later ruled out for the Walmart script ("he wasn't wrong," zero accountability, the man is at fault). Both are on the record. The library's job is to note it, not resolve it: the winner concedes at the mirror and takes it back in beat 17 ("he was a coward, not right"). The Walmart script never concedes. Test both, do not blend.
- **Beat 9, "every single week"** against a daily dropper. Small logic slip in the source. Later scripts say "every single day."
- **Beat 12, the origin claim.** "Afro-Brazilian women have used three of them for a hundred years." The four herbs are North American and European. Later scripts moved the credential to a person ("my auntie in Kingston, forty years"). Either way, the guide's own credential (clinic-trained) is the one that holds.
- **Names collide across the account.** Here the narrator is Renee and the good date is Marcus. In the cookout script, Renee is the friend and Marcus is the bad husband. If both run in the same account, a repeat viewer notices. Worth a naming register.
- **Three payoffs in one scene** (the ex, the stranger, the ex again) plus the daughter circle. That is the longest back half in the bank. When cutting for length, the stranger at the sink is the conversion beat and the daughter is the proof beat; the ex's second line is the one to lose first.

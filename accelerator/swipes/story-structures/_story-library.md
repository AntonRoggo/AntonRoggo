# Story structure library

Started 2026-09-23. A reference bank of whole-story spines from winning ads, any brand, any product. The point is not the ad, it is the skeleton: what each beat does, in what order, and how long the product's timeline is. When a new story has a good lead and a broken back half, or a payoff that only works for a one-day product, we pull the missing acts from here and re-time them for the product we are writing.

This sits above `clients/lymphoria/structures/` (which holds mechanism-delivery blocks: the locker room, the coworker relay, the aunt on the phone). A story spine says where the mechanism goes and what surrounds it. The structures say what the mechanism sounds like.

How to use it: [[story-completion]] in workflows/.

## Taxonomy (tag every story with all seven)

1. **Wound.** What happened to the lead. Caught cheating. Left for younger. Called out in public. Couldn't protect her. Ran into the ex. Invisible to a spouse. Rejected on a date.
2. **Lead.** Who is telling it. Sex, age, life stage (menopause, divorce, the grind), what the body shows.
3. **Guide.** Who knows the secret and why they are allowed to. Family with a foreign credential who travels in. A peer who was worse. A coworker relaying a relative. A practitioner. A stranger.
4. **Mechanism delivery.** How the science gets said. Kitchen counter monologue. Locker room dialogue. Truck ride relay. Hands on the body. Phone call.
5. **Timeline class.** How long the product takes to show. INSTANT (same day, cosmetics, foundation, a mask). SHORT (days to two weeks). LONG (six to twelve weeks, weekly milestones). Lymphoria is LONG: first week a feeling, week two a belt notch and a witness, week six visible, week twelve a number.
6. **Payoff.** The scene that proves it. Public re-encounter with the one who did the wound. A new person's attention. A stranger asks. A child notices. The same room, revisited.
7. **Close.** How it sells. Direct address to the viewer, the "maybe you" list, the enemy (Ozempic, HRT, the doctor), the herbs, scarcity, guarantee, the last line.

## Timeline rule

A story's beats move between products. Its timeline does not. An INSTANT story can put the payoff the same night. A LONG story cannot, and if it tries, the viewer stops believing at the first "the next morning." When a lead is borrowed from an INSTANT story for Lymphoria, the middle must be rebuilt with weekly milestones and witnesses (checklist 9 in [[story-ad-logic]]), and the payoff moves to week six or later.

## 3. What must never change
- The comments land on the witness, never on the lead. The lead is oblivious or in denial at the hooks.
- The hard talk fails first. The ordinary fixes are named and brushed off before the real stake is said.
- The mechanism comes from a peer's family, not from the witness. The witness only relays it, and keeps it to six lines.
- The clog cause names the lead's habit and then absolves it: "still drinks on weekends," "didn't stop having beers." The habit stays. The waste goes.
- "Just try it. For me." is the close of the relay. The lead's answer is the same three words both times: "...alright. I hear you." / "...alright. I'll try it."
- The same event, the same strangers, no laughing. Nobody fails to recognize him.
- The lead narrates the CTA and gives the witness the credit: "he was right."

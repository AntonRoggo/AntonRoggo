## 1. Hook formula

**[The insult in one concrete object] + [the reason she gave] + [who she is marrying] + [the clock].** "My sister ordered my bridesmaid dress two sizes up from hers. Didn't want me squeezed in on her big day. She's marrying my ex-husband. And I had ten weeks." Four lines, and the last one is a deadline, not a mystery. This is the only hook in the bank that does not promise a person or a place; it promises a date.

## 2. What 004 does that 001 and 002 do not

- **The wound is in the past.** No silent catch. "Ten months holding it down." The story opens on the aftermath and the new insult, not the discovery.
- **Social pressure is a character.** Mama: "If you don't show up smiling, baby, you're the scandal, not them." Then the yes: "Because no means I'm bitter. Means I'm still a mess. Means every Sunday dinner they whisper my name." Nobody in 001 or 002 is trapped by what people will say. Here it is the reason she is standing in the dress at all.
- **The insult is an object with a number.** Two sizes up. "Keeping me the big sister in every photo on that wall." The dress is the scale for the whole story: the seamstress takes it in at week three and again at week five, and "the dress they took in twice" replaces every pound count. There are no numbers on the narrator.
- **Panic effort, not patient effort.** "So I did what we do when we panic. Ran the gym five days a week like punishment. Keto for twelve days. Dropped five, gained eight. Crying in the Target parking lot by the interstate." Four weeks, not three years, and it makes things worse. "Like even my own body was on her side."
- **No doctor.** The wrong-authority beat is gone. Mama and the scale are the authorities that fail her.
- **The relative does not fly in. She picks up.** "She picked up laughing. Long time, baby." The credential is the shop ("runs a little spot, herbs and oils"), not a clinic. She sends the bottle: "I'm sending you something."
- **The guide is the first to ask.** "It took my aunt an ocean away to ask the one question nobody would say. But how are you, love." The wound is said aloud for the first time on this call ("Say that again"). That is the beat the cortisol rule is built on.
- **The blame lands before the science, and comes back after it.** "Girl, you know they did this to you, right. Not just your heart. Your body." Then the mechanism. Then "They broke your heart and put this weight on you. So here's what we're gonna do. We're taking both back."
- **A refrain.** "Mama said smile / So you know I'ma smile / Just get me down that aisle" at the low point, and "Mama said smile / Oh, I'ma smile / Watch me light up that aisle" at the turn. The same three lines, one word changed. Same trick with "standing beside her in the dress that she picked with my belly tucked inside" and later "in the dress they took in twice with nothing left to hide."
- **The witness is a professional with no stake.** The seamstress. "This dress is too big now. Gotta take it down." Then "Second time, honey. Somebody's gonna steal that crowd." She cannot be accused of flattery.
- **The payoff is one beat.** "He watched me down that aisle at her wedding. One beat too long. And his best man saw it. And Nia saw it too." Nobody says "you look incredible." The stare is measured in time, and two people clock it.
- **The betrayer concedes.** At the reception Nia grabs her hand, tears: "That's how you stand." The bride admits it.
- **The circle is a text with a number.** "Cousin Tasha texting. Down nine pounds. This stuff is real."
- **The close names the occasion, not the wound.** "Maybe you got a date you're dreading. A reunion. A wedding. A funeral." Then the absolution: "Heartbreak stress is real and it's not your fault." Then "Your dress is waiting."

## 5. What to watch when reusing

- The verse form is doing work the prose versions do with stage directions. The refrain and the two mirrored couplets are the structure of the transformation; if this is adapted to prose, those need a scene each or the turn goes flat.
- The register is specific ("I'ma," "ain't know," "baby," "honey," "girl"). It lives in Mama, Marcia, and the seamstress, and in the narrator's own lines. It is the ICP's voice, not a costume, because the operator wrote it. Do not port it to another ICP by swapping the words; rebuild the register.
- No age on the narrator. Fourteen years married and a sister who braided her hair puts her late 30s to mid 40s. If a number is needed, it has to fit "fourteen years."

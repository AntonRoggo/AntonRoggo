# Swipe: Mario Castelli's banked rulebook

Source: The Standards Ladder field guide (Claude Code Accelerator, Day 2). These are MARIO's rules, banked from his real email engine and ad factory. They live here as reference to model from, not as our voice. The full copy rubric below was adopted into [[voice]] on 2026-08-28 at the operator's direction; this note remains the source record. The animation principles and tool ideas are still reference only.

## His copy rubric (email engine)
- One sentence per line. Shatter the paragraph. A wall of grey makes the eye quit before it reads a word.
- Serve the reader, not yourself. A line that admires its own cleverness is dead weight. Swap it for what the reader actually gets.
- Street-smart is the register. Grade for real, not articulate.
- Do not neuter the profanity. It is texture, not vulgarity.
- Lead with the human, not the machine. The mechanism is body proof, never the hook.
- Never leave the WHY vague. Cash every claim out into a concrete reader payoff.
- Kill the canned signpost. No "and here's the magic." Let the line land on its own.
- The punch-cap: the single emotional-payoff word of a beat gets caps AND underline.

## His animation principles (ad factory, AI video)
- Stylize the look, never the biology. Dramatic abundance flows through intact structures. A doctor should not be able to correct the frame.
- Sculpt the emotion, do not label it. The feeling hits in a glance, not after an explanation.
- Make the pain brutally obvious. A 5-year-old glancing for one second should read "this person is hurting."
- The simplest shot that sells the beat beats the clever one the AI keeps botching.
- One reference image per emotion AND posture, or the triumphant hero inherits the defeated guy's face.
- Max intensity per action, not max number of actions. AI video defaults lifeless. Pick one move, push it to the peak.
- Zero machine-made text in frame, ever. Stamp the real CTA on in post.
- Positive motion grammar: describe the motion you want ("shoulders roll, chin lifts, jaw sets"), never suppressor phrases ("barely any movement").

## His tools, as an ideas bank for ours
- Formatter that also scores the output against spec.
- Skim-path extractor: pull every emphasized word in order; if they alone do not sell the pitch, the emphasis is on the wrong words.
- Prompt linter that blocks known-bad patterns before an expensive render.
- CTA overlay applied in post, identical every time.
- Memory-sync linter: flags lessons saved in memory that never made it into the permanent docs.

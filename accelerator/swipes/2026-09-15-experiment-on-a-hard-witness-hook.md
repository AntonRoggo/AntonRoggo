# Swipe: the experiment on a hard witness

Source hook (Organised, proven): "I cooked a meal for my four little brothers who are extremely picky eaters to see if I could trick them into eating one of the world's healthiest foods."

## Copy blocks (P3C2)

| Block | Where it is in the sentence | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **Curiosity** | "to see if I could trick them" | Frames the whole ad as an experiment with an unknown result. The reader has to stay to find out if it worked. |
| **Constraint** | "four little brothers who are extremely picky eaters" | The witness is chosen to make the promise as hard as possible. Picky eaters are the toughest jury for food. Specific number (four), specific relationship (little brothers), so it reads as real. |
| **Promise** | "eating one of the world's healthiest foods" | The outcome, sized big ("world's healthiest") and hedged just enough ("one of") to stay credible. |
| **Proof** | "I cooked a meal" | First person, past tense, already done. The proof of the result is withheld; the proof that the test happened is in the verb. |
| Pain | implicit | Every parent of a picky eater feels it without being told. |
| Conditions | none | The hook doesn't need urgency; the loop carries it. |

## The template

**[I did the thing] for/on [specific witness] who [constraint that makes noticing or accepting hard], to see if [promise, framed as a test].**

**Veil the reveal.** The source never says "liver" in the hook; it says "one of the world's healthiest foods." The promise names a superlative category, the body names the ingredient. Saying the ingredient in the hook (our first draft said "beef fat") spends the surprise the body was built to deliver. The superlative has to carry the quality the reader wants, not a neutral fact. "Healthiest" is what you want food to be. "Oldest" is not what you want skincare to be; "anti-aging" is. Equivalent for tallow, operator's pick: "one of the world's oldest anti-aging secrets." Age attaches to "secret" (pedigree), "anti-aging" carries the desire, "one of" is the hedge.

The constraint is the lever. It's not a property of the product, it's a property of the witness. Two kinds work:
- **The oblivious witness:** hasn't noticed anything in years. If they notice, it must be big.
- **The expert witness:** can't be fooled. If they can't tell, it must be real.

Same sentence works for any product where the result is visible or tasteable by someone else: skincare, food, hair, clothes, teeth, fragrance, posture, energy.

## Variant: reaction in the hook (operator's, 2026-09-15)
"I started using the world's oldest anti-aging secret without telling anyone, and my husband, who hasn't noticed a haircut in 20 years, gave me my first compliment in a really long time."

Same blocks, different order. Proof and Promise move into the hook (the reaction already happened). The only open loop is Curiosity: what is the secret. Trade-off: less suspense, more proof in the first three seconds. Use when the audience is skeptical of results; use the question form when the audience is bored of claims. The witness's trait still does the work, because it makes the compliment hard to earn. Examples in [[2026-09-15-tt-oldest-secret-reaction-hooks]].

## F-Balm base
"I put beef fat on my face every night for two weeks without telling anyone, to see if anybody would notice."
- Curiosity: would anybody notice. Constraint: beef fat (the gross factor) + without telling anyone. Promise: visible change. Proof: two weeks, already done.
- Weakness vs the source: "anyone" is not a specific witness. The 20 variations in [[2026-09-15-tt-two-weeks-hard-witness-hooks]] fix that.

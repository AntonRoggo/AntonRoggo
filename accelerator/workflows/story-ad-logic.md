# Story ad logic: the pass every story script gets before it ships

Source: Ads Arcadia feedback call, 2026-09-18 (Israel and Lee on the TT "person I have to thank" script and on Cosmin's cholesterol movie ad), plus the operator's bracketed notes on the same script. The verdict on the TT draft: "You're very good at finding the right angle. Cadence is good. It's mostly just story logic." This file is the logic pass.

The test behind all of it: the moment a viewer thinks "that wouldn't happen," she stops watching because she cares and starts watching to critique. The Reddit-story comment sections prove it ("How could Sarah be 43 and her son be 20?"). Write so there is nothing for that comment to say.

## 1. Every hook clause answers "for what?"
- "I've tried everything." For what? "A plastic surgeon asked what I use on my face." Because of what? "I have her to thank." For what outcome?
- Fix: attach the problem or the visible result to each clause. "I've tried everything for the lines around my mouth." "She stopped mid-facial, said my skin was in great shape, and asked if I'd had laser done."
- A label is not context. "I'm a beef farmer's daughter" reads like a job title nobody says out loud. Say it the way a person would: "I grew up on a beef farm," "third-generation beef farmer."

## 2. A compliment must be obviously a compliment
- "Who did your laser?" is ambiguous. Laser can mean damage. Same as "what's that shirt?"
- State the positive first, then the question: "she said my skin was in great shape and asked if I'd had laser done."
- The flip version (Israel): the dreaded line, then the turn. "She said the four words any woman fears: 'I can't help you.' ... 'If anything, I should be asking what you're doing.'"

## 3. The hook chooses the body
- A specific hook invites a specific body. A plastic surgeon in the hook makes the viewer expect science from an authority. A friend in the hook makes her expect a kitchen-table story.
- The witness in the hook is the witness the body pays off. The person thanked in the hook is the person who hands over the product in the body. Different hooks that imply different spokespeople need different bodies, or they don't get tested on this one.
- Run the arithmetic on every age and date in hook plus body together. (A 24-year-old granddaughter cannot share a script with a daughter married ten years ago and a four-year-old grandchild.)

## 4. The guide has to be able to know the secret
- "She's into alternative health stuff" is not a reason. Give the friend a credential the viewer accepts: a nurse for thirty years, a lifelong skincare obsessive, married to someone in the field.
- Then check the credential against the research. For TT: dermatologists (7 percent) and estheticians do not recommend tallow; nurses and doctors who use it themselves appear in the reviews.

## 5. Nothing in the setup may contradict the point
- "Best friends for thirty years" plus "she's had the secret for ten" makes the viewer ask why she never told you. Either the friend only recently started (her skin CHANGED), or you haven't seen her in a while, or she introduces you to someone else who knows.
- After writing, read it as the skeptic: what here would someone call bullshit on? It takes three or four passes or a reader who isn't close to it.

## 6. Dramatize the realization, innocently
- "What the heck happened to my skin?" invites the answer "aging." Stage the moment instead, and make the trigger innocent: no one is being cruel, no one is a villain. A small child pointing at an old photo and not recognizing her does more than any insult.
- Skincare runs on shame and the fear of being judged. Let the scene produce the shame; never have a character deliver it.
- Length is the constraint. The full scene is long-form; for a 90-second ad compress to the one exchange that lands.

## 7. Give the problem a tipping point
- "I was doing the same exact routine. Nothing changed. But I did." Then the natural cause (menopause, for TT). The body compensated for years and then stopped; that is why it felt sudden.
- The tipping point is also what sets up the mechanism later, so plant it in the character early.

## 8. Describe, don't assert
- "Her skin had changed so much for the better" is an assertion. "She's three years older than me and the lines around her eyes were softer than mine" is a description. (Extends the 2026-08-29 rule: every beat is a scene, not a summary.)

## 9. Stage the transformation in three steps
- First night: nothing to see (a feeling at most). A few days to a week: maybe, "I thought I was imagining it." Two weeks: visible, and someone else notices.
- This sets the buyer's expectation for her own first week, which protects the refund rate.

## 10. The honest beat must not kill the momentum
- "It won't erase every line. Only surgery will do that" is a boner killer: everything is going well and then it may not work.
- Keep the honesty, turn it upward in the same breath: "It's not perfect, and it's not the miracle other brands swear they are. But look what it did for me." Then the proof.

## 11. Characters act like real people (from the cholesterol-ad review)
- A wife who goads her husband into a three-on-one fight reads as someone who wants him hurt; the viewer stops empathizing with both of them. A real partner de-escalates and he is the one who wants to act.
- Pity for the wrong reason is fatal. The hero can be limited, never a coward.
- The guide speaks in his own register. A mechanic friend doesn't explain nitric oxide; he says "I don't know what's in it, I know it works." Put the science in the mouth that can carry it.
- Never write "whatever you're doing, keep doing it" for a doctor. It is the clearest tell of a lazy script.
- The story has to smell like the product from the first scene. A harassment story reads as testosterone, not cholesterol. Pick a trigger event that belongs to the condition.
- A deadline helps: "come back in 30 days" gives the timeline a spine and the ending a payoff scene.

## 12. Architecture before copy
- "Copy is easy. Real copy lives in the ideation." Decide how each beat logically hands off to the next and what it allows you to say next. When the chain is sound, the writing is transcription plus flair.
- Two people talking runs far slower than 150 words a minute. Budget dialogue scripts accordingly.
- Research habit: the AI-voiced Reddit story videos. Same cadence every time; read the comments for the logic holes people catch.

## 13. Yapper and UGC rules (Israel, Ads Arcadia, 2026-10-09 review of the TT Botox yapper)
Same notes he gave on 2026-09-25, now banked. Apply to every talking-head, yapper, or founder-reply script.
- **Plant a claim in the hook, not just a story.** "If I could go back, I'd never let a needle near my face" is shock with no promise. Blend the payoff in: what this did that the needle couldn't. Pair shock hooks with a visible contrast (before and after) when the footage exists.
- **Price plus regret beats regret alone.** "I paid someone $400 to freeze my face, and it cost me years of regret." A number makes the regret concrete.
- **Two short hooks back to back read as one fragment.** Open one of them up into a longer hook that sets the scene, names the absurdity ("the crazy things we do, all in the name of beauty"), and promises the reveal ("except there is").
- **Rule of three for sensory beats.** "My forehead felt like a sheet of ice. I could barely move my eyebrows. And then I had to go back and do it all again." A third beat that escalates, not a pivot to "here's what nobody tells you."
- **Name the cost of the alternative in the viewer's terms.** "Every three months, just to keep that frozen look. It felt like renting my youth."
- **Every mechanism needs its "why," in one simple line.** "Your skin stops making oil after 40" invites "why?" Give the biological cause plainly (estrogen drops), make it normal ("it happens to every one of us"), and make it personal ("for me it was 41").
- **New concept, explain it.** A term the viewer hasn't heard (sebum, estrogen-deficient skin) gets a one-line plain-English gloss the first time.
- **Explain the delivery, not just the ingredient.** Say what the product does once it's on: soaks in, goes where your own oil used to be, doesn't sit on top.
- **Yappers sound unpolished.** Spitballing, getting it off her chest, not a written speech. Contractions, "like," "honestly," run-on thoughts are fine. Grade 5 still applies.
- **No "No X. No Y. No Z. Just one W."** It reads as AI. Say it the way a person would.
- **Fill the gaps between sections.** If a paragraph could be swapped with another, there's a missing connector. Adding the missing thought costs about ten seconds, not a minute.
- **Tone for 50+:** a little girly, a little nostalgic ("I just wanted my face back"), never forced young. This buyer wants a time capsule, not a costume.
- **Avoid internal contradictions he'll catch:** "I did Botox once" cannot sit next to "I kept going back every few months."

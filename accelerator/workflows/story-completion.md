# Story completion: finishing an unfinished story from the library

Started 2026-09-23. The play for when a story has a strong lead (from any brand, any product) and a back half that is missing, weak, or timed for a product that works faster than ours.

Library: [[_story-library]] in swipes/story-structures/. Logic pass: [[story-ad-logic]]. Mechanism blocks: [[mechanism-and-product-core]].

Related: [[Promotion]] [[standards-ladder]]

---

## 1. Read the lead and write down its promises

Every hook clause is a promise the body has to keep (checklist 1 and 3). List them: who did what to whom, the ages, the place, the mysterious thing, the person who brought it. If the hook names a witness, that witness hands over the product. If it names a place, the payoff returns to it or explains why not.

## 2. Tag the lead with the taxonomy

Wound, lead, guide (if named), delivery (if implied), timeline class of the SOURCE product, payoff (if the hook gives it away), close (usually not in the lead). The timeline class is the one that decides how much rebuilding the back half needs.

## 3. Pick the missing acts from the index

Match on wound first, then lead, then payoff. Pull the beat map from the closest story and mark which beats the lead already covers. The rest is the shopping list. For the mechanism, pick the delivery structure that fits the guide the lead implies: a peer who was worse (locker room), a coworker relaying a relative (truck), family with a credential who travels or calls in (sister from Brazil, aunt in Kingston), a practitioner (Ana's hands).

## 4. Re-time for our product

Lymphoria is LONG. Whatever the source did in a day, we do in six to twelve weeks:
- First night: a feeling at most (sleep, peeing).
- Week one: one small visible thing (face, ring, watch). "I told no one."
- Week two: a belt notch and the first witness who has a reason to notice.
- Week three to five: jawline, skin, the number, the old clothes.
- Week six: visible to a room. The payoff scene can happen here.
- Week twelve: the big number, if there is one.

If the source's payoff is "that night" or "the next morning," it moves. If moving it breaks the hook, the hook changes, not the timeline.

## 5. Build the chain, then write

Beat by beat, in one line each, before any copy: what happens, what it lets you say next. Run it against [[story-ad-logic]] (arithmetic on every age and date, the guide can know, no contradictions, the shame comes from the scene not a character, the honest beat turns upward). Then transcribe.

## 6. Run the pass on the finished script and list what it caught

Every story ships with its catches listed. If it caught nothing, look again.

## 7. File it

The script goes in copy/. If it runs and wins, the spine goes in the library as a new numbered story with tags, beat map, blocks, and the timeline check. Two moves: save the work, promote the lesson.

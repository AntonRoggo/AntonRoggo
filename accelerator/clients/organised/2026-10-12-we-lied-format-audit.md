## 4. Patterns across the three pan winners

### Structure
1. Confession hook that fakes bad news: "We need to publicly apologize... because we lied" / "I probably shouldn't say this, but the real reason..." / "Do not buy X until you hear why the company lied." The lie is always good news (price).
2. "Yes, it's true" stack: 5 to 8 benefits, each confirmed like a skeptic's question. Each one is feature, outcome, enemy ("unlike Teflon", "not 1-2 years like a coated pan").
3. One enemy runs through the whole ad: Teflon / PFAS / forever chemicals.
4. The turn: "Now to the lie. We thought we couldn't drop the price."
5. Reason-why for the discount (pan 2: missed forecast, overstock). Makes the deal believable.
6. Value math: "three times the current price", "buy two pans, free lid, still save $100", "buy it once, buy it for life".
7. Objection as benefit: "The only downside... you might want to ditch all your old pans."
8. Risk reversal: lifetime warranty, 90 days risk-free.
9. Takeaway close: "So if you'd rather miss out on the deal, go ahead. Just don't blame us..."
10. Identity close, near verbatim in 2 of 3: "if you want to own a pan that finally puts your family's health first without sacrificing performance..."
11. Scarcity CTA: "before we're out of stock."
Format variant: pan 3 numbers the benefits 1 to 5.

### Do not transfer unless true
- Flash sale, "ends tonight", "limited time"
- Overstock or missed-forecast reason
- "Before we're out of stock", "sold out four times"
Account warning: hype buyers cancel at about twice the rate. This format is promo-led by design, so test it as a new format and watch the cancel rate, not just CPA.

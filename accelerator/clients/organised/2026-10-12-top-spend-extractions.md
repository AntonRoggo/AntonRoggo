## Ad 1: Kids, "What would happen in 30 days" (VSL, founder voice)

Ad name in account: TBC

### Strategic layer
- Angle: 30-day transformation of a child given back the nutrients modern food stripped out. "This is what would happen to your kids if they started taking Organised for 30 days."
- Awareness: Solution-aware. "7 times more absorbable than the synthetic iron you get in children's pills" / "It completely replaces 4 things that the doc has told me to give my own son."
- Sophistication: Stage 4 into 5. New mechanism (heme iron 7x, colostrum "seals the gut lining") plus identification ("what every generation before ours was getting automatically from real food").
- Tone: founder or insider, older brother ("I've got 4 little brothers", "our community"). Calm explainer, nostalgic and ancestral. No hype.

### Avatar layer
- Primary: parents of kids.
- Sub-avatars: kids always ill ("constant sniffling, which never seems to go away"); already giving pills or a doctor's list; school-age energy and focus ("homework"); taste or refusal worry ("completely tasteless", "completely hides the organs").
- Age: explicit not addressed. Implicit: school-age kids; "40 years", "great-grandparents".
- Gender: explicit neutral ("parents"). Implicit: mum ("One mom in our community", "the first milk that women produce").
- Daily: mid-day crash, homework, sniffling, "ask for every single morning now".
- Habits: children's pills, a doctor's supplement list, raw milk / raw honey / banana, cooking with it.

### Psychology layer
- Physical: dark circles, constant sniffling, mid-day crash, poor sleep (implied), gut (implied), focus (implied).
- Emotional: not addressed. Nearest: "never seems to go away".
- Social: implied by the payoff, "her son's teacher asked if anything different had happened at home".
- Identity: parent who restores what was lost; implied guilt over "what the food industry has taken out of the family diet".
- False beliefs: food still has it ("you don't find in food anymore"); pills work; kids will refuse organs.
- Tried: "synthetic iron you get in children's pills"; "4 things that the doc has told me to give".
- Mechanism: food-industry depletion ("40 years"); heme iron 7x more absorbable; colostrum seals the gut lining; "E12 [B12], heme iron, choline, and folate".

### Promise and offer layer
- Promise: "given back to your child what every generation before ours was getting automatically from real food", as a stacked benefit list.
- Timeline: first few days / "almost immediately" (sleep) / end of week 1 (gut, dark circles, sniffles) / week 3 (energy, focus) / a month.
- Proof: 5 organs named, 8 ingredients, "7 times more absorbable", one mum anecdote, "over 200,000 families... after just 2 years", founder's brothers, clean-label list.
- Offer: no price, discount or urgency. Whisk, bottle, field guide. 100 days, full refund, "or your children don't like it".
- CTA: no explicit CTA. Guarantee is the last beat, then the loop line "This is what would happen."

### Notes
- Fits the account data: 30-day format, kids job, long stacked list, a why for every benefit, founder as speaker without the origin story.
- Rule tensions: "almost immediately" vs "no instant results"; "seals the gut lining" and "undoes 40 years" are strong claims for compliance to check.
- Story logic: "4 little brothers" plus "my own son". Verify.
- Transcript: "E12" is B12.

## Ad 2: Hong Kong, "like for like" (VSL, first-person user as educator)

Ad name in account: TBC

### Strategic layer
- Angle: longevity through organ eating, proven by a culture. "The average person in Hong Kong eats more than a pound of meat a day, and they have the highest life expectancy in the world, but it's not the type of meat that you'd expect."
- Awareness: Unaware. Opens on curiosity, no problem or solution: "What would happen to your health if you ate a pound of meat every day?" Problem only arrives via the testimonial.
- Sophistication: Stage 5. Big idea plus worldview: Hong Kong, TCM "like for like", "peptides that are so similar to our own", "26,000 compounds" vs "about 20".
- Tone: first-person user as educator ("That's why I use Organised"). Curious, documentary-style explainer, contrarian hook. No hype.

### Avatar layer
- Primary: health and longevity-curious adults.
- Sub-avatars: woman with IBS and hormone issues (testimonial); supplement stackers ("collagen, protein, and vitamins all separately"); toddler parents ("2-year-olds' baked beans").
- Age: explicit not addressed. Implicit: menstruating age, toddler parent, longevity-minded.
- Gender: explicit neutral. Implicit: female ("I finally got my period back").
- Daily: "with almost every single meal" (Hong Kong); smoothies, pancakes, soups, baked beans. No pain moments.
- Habits: supplement stacking, "unlimited solutions and supplements", avoiding unusual organs.

### Psychology layer
- Physical: IBS, "hormonal imbalances", lost period, gut and digestion.
- Emotional: "battled... for years", "unlimited solutions", "feels like a miracle", "spending so much money".
- Social: not addressed.
- Identity: implied, "things most people would never dare to try".
- False beliefs: lots of meat is bad for long life; supplements are complete ("only have about 20"); organs taste bad ("milk at the bottom of a cereal bowl").
- Tried: "unlimited solutions and supplements"; "collagen, protein, and vitamins all separately".
- Mechanism: like for like; peptide matching; 26,000 compounds vs 20; freeze-dried to preserve nutrients.

### Promise and offer layer
- Promise: long life and self-healing through whole-food organs ("heal yourselves"); made concrete by "complete game changer... got my period back".
- Timeline: "Just 2 months in" (testimonial only).
- Proof: Hong Kong life expectancy, TCM, "emerging science", 26,000 vs 20, named testimonial (Kaya), ingredients and sweeteners, "Over 100,000 people".
- Offer: no price, bonuses or urgency. "100 days completely risk-free... full refund, no questions asked."
- CTA: "you can try it too for 100 days completely risk-free." Soft trial, last line.

### Notes
- Breaks the account data (no single job or person up front, hormone and gut heavy, long testimonial) yet spends. Need its cost per sale to know if it is efficient or just big.
- Customer count: "100,000 people" here vs "200,000 families" in ad 1 vs "200,000+" on site.
- Banned word: "heal" three times.
- Verify: Hong Kong pound-a-day stat, 26,000 vs 20, Kaya is real and verbatim (not in site reviews pulled).
- Steal: "It tastes just like the milk at the bottom of a cereal bowl." Best taste line seen so far; taste is the top family cancel reason.

## Ad 3: Picky eater, "hide it" (VSL with on-screen lists, peer voice)

Ad name in account: TBC

### Strategic layer
- Angle: stop fighting the plate, hide organs in what the picky eater already eats. "Here's how to hide 5 organs in the chocolate milk that your picky eater already drinks."
- Awareness: Problem-aware. "If that's the whole menu in your house, you're not failing. You're trying to feed a picky eater. The worry is what's missing."
- Sophistication: Stage 3 into 4. New method ("stop fighting the plate and just hide it") plus mechanism ("hemin... absorbs more readily than iron in plants"). No worldview layer.
- Tone: practical peer, no "I". Reassuring, non-judgemental how-to. Includes an anti-promise: "It's not going to turn a picky eater into an adventurous one."

### Avatar layer
- Primary: parent of a picky eater.
- Sub-avatars: school kids always ill ("every cold that goes around the class"); beige diet ("Nuggets, pasta, toast, pizza"); whole family ("coffee", "Anyone over the age of 1").
- Age: explicit "Anyone over the age of 1". Implicit: school age ("lunchbox", "class"). Parent's age not addressed.
- Gender: neutral throughout.
- Daily: lunchbox comes home full, meltdowns before dinner, colds going round the class, chocolate milk, breakfast they already eat.
- Habits: beige menu, fighting the plate, daily chocolate milk.

### Psychology layer
- Physical: dark circles, every cold, meltdowns before dinner, missing nutrients (implied).
- Emotional: "The worry is what's missing", fighting the plate.
- Social: "the lunchbox that comes home full", "every cold that goes around the class".
- Identity: failing parent fear, "you're not failing".
- False beliefs: the fix is getting them to eat better; plant iron is enough; familiar foods fill the gaps.
- Tried: "fighting the plate"; "the foods that used to fill the gaps".
- Mechanism: organ nutrient density (B12, folate, vitamin A, copper); hemin from spleen vs plant iron; hide it in milk, pancakes, sauce.

### Promise and offer layer
- Promise: "it puts real food into the breakfast that they already eat." Bounded by the anti-promise.
- Timeline: "Most people notice a difference within weeks."
- Proof: nutrients, ingredients, clean label. No testimonial, stats or customer count.
- Offer: no price, bonuses or urgency. "100 days to try it. If your family doesn't get on with it, there's a full refund."
- CTA: no explicit CTA. Guarantee closes.

### Notes
- Cleanest against account rules: honest timescale, no "heal", person inside the story, a why, taste handled cold and hidden.
- Gaps: no proof element; about 5 symptoms vs the 6+ bar.
- Details: "5 organs" but only 4 named (lung missing); "coffee" in a kids list.

## Across all three

### Shared (provisional account rules)
1. Every ad explains why it works (heme iron and colostrum / peptides and 26,000 compounds / hemin vs plant iron).
2. Every ad handles taste in the body ("completely tasteless" / "milk at the bottom of a cereal bowl" / "start cold").
3. Every ad positions against something else (kids' pills / isolated supplements / plant iron).
4. Kids appear in all three, even the Hong Kong ad.
5. Same close: 100-day guarantee as last beat, refund framed on the specific objection, no price, discount, urgency or hard CTA.
6. Calm explainer voice, no hype.

### White space (none of the three do it)
- Cost per day against the supplement shelf (top fix for price, which nearly stopped half of buyers).
- The adult-woman iron job (cheapest sales).
- The top survey reason in customer words: "I know liver is good for me but I can't stomach it."

### Fix before reuse
- Customer count: 200,000 families / 100,000 people / none.
- "Heal" (ad 2), "seals the gut lining" (ad 1).
- "Almost immediately" (ad 1) vs "no instant results".

### Open
- Ad names in account and cost per sale for each. Spend share alone does not prove efficiency, especially ad 2.

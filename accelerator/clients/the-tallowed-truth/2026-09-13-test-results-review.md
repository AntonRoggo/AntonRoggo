## What the data says
Two angles have real volume: manufacturing trust (65 purchases) and sale/price/scarcity (72 purchases, 6.30x). Everything else is 10 purchases or fewer, so the ordering below #3 is directional, not proven.

The two lanes I wrote in most this cycle are #7 dryness (1.13x) and #9 appearance/glow (2.00x, $760 CPA). Crepey skin, which got its own hook set, is 0.14x.

## The tension with the historical winners
Nine of the 21 all-time top scripts are appearance and natural-aging stories (attractive neighbor, Botox hack, plastic surgeon). That lane is now #9. Two possible reads:
1. The lane is fatigued. The neighbor story has been re-cut at least four times.
2. The new creative in that lane isn't the same thing as the winners. The winners are Ben, founder-voice, retelling a real encounter. The recent adaptations are scripted UGC in a woman's voice. Same pain point, different proof mechanism.

Can't tell which without seeing the specific ads inside bucket #9.

## Kill, hold, double down
**Kill for now:** crepey (0.14x), retinol/barrier (0.65x), dryness/menopause (1.13x). Park the 12-beat, the sebum text hooks, the esthetician crepey set. Don't retest.

**Double down:** #1 manufacturing trust. The skeptic and kitchen-to-facility work is already aimed here. Every new script should carry a facility, founder, or in-house beat even when it leads elsewhere.

**Resolve before touching:** #2 sale/price/scarcity at 6.30x. The client file says the account has never run a discount, bonus, or urgency. It has now, and it's the top ROAS. Need to know what the offer was.

**Small sample, worth a real spend test:** #8 greasy/smell objection (5.70x on one purchase), #6 new scents (4.26x on 8), tick (4.77x on $99). Greasy/smell is the number one review complaint, so an objection-handler ad has a large audience to find.

## Untested with this demographic, from the review analysis
None of these appear in the tested list.
- **Family and household use.** 2,559 review mentions. "Both my husband and I love this tallow" [R14056]. The household-of-three customer call is a top-16 ad and the theme has never been an angle.
- **Sensitive skin and eczema.** 1,980 mentions, plus eczema reviews cited in the 69 ad. Acne got $466; sensitivity got nothing.
- **The anti-anti-aging position.** "Thank you for NOT using 'anti aging' in your narrative" [R19341]. "I will keep using it whether it makes me look younger or not" [C472]. "At my age I don't expect to erase 50 years of life" [R28071]. If #9 is failing because the audience rejects the looking-younger promise, this is the flip side of the same audience.
- **Value per tin and dose.** "A little goes a long way," "I found it greasy at first before realizing I was just using too much" [C296]. Different from a sale: it's why the price is fair, not a price cut.
- **Product selection.** "Which one do you recommend for face?" [C164] is one of the most repeated comments. Whip vs balm vs unscented has never been an ad.
- **Long hesitation and proof.** "I've been watching these ads for at least a year" [C306]. "Then I had seen the REVIEWS that I was waiting for" [R27076]. A retargeting angle for the people already in the comments.
- **Men.** "I've replaced a few different products with this one" [R8805, male user]. Untested.

## Occlusive science (part 3, sourced)
- Petrolatum at 5% or more reduces transepidermal water loss "by more than 98%." Lanolin, mineral oil, and silicones "only reduce TEWL by 20 to 30%." Sethi et al, Indian J Dermatol 2016, citing Lynde 2001 and Spruit 1971. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4885180/ (Ghadially 1992 is often cited for the 98% and does not contain it.)
- Silicones in vivo, 26 volunteers: "the tested silicones did not moisturize the skin"; their TEWL effect was not significantly different from untreated skin. De Paepe et al, Skin Pharmacol Physiol 2014. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24457536/ Dimethicone is in the top five of Crepe Erase, Gold Bond, Medix, RoC, and CeraVe.
- Plant oils vs petrolatum, in vivo: vegetable oils give "semi-occlusion"; "the most effective occlusion was found for petrolatum." Patzelt et al, Skin Res Technol 2012.
- Beef tallow: no clinical TEWL or hydration measurement exists. Russell et al, Cureus 2024: only two human studies, both sensory ratings. Any tallow occlusion percentage online is extrapolated from fatty-acid studies or vendor blogs. Do not state one.
- Honest read: petrolatum is the best sealant on the evidence; it seals without supplying fatty acids. Silicone is the ingredient with a published "did not moisturize" result, and it's what most of these creams use in the top five.

## What crepey skin is, and what a cream can do (part 4, sourced)
- Cleveland Clinic: "When your skin becomes thin and develops wrinkles or fine lines, it can have a crepe paper-like appearance." Primary cause sun damage breaking down elastin; also age, weight change, dry skin, medications. Usually begins in the 40s. https://health.clevelandclinic.org/whats-causing-your-crepey-skin-and-how-can-you-fix-it
- Harvard Health: "Estrogen fuels the production of oil and other substances that moisturize and protect the skin. When levels drop, skin can't hold moisture as effectively." https://www.health.harvard.edu/healthy-aging-and-longevity/fresh-faced-through-the-years
- Menopause journal 2026: collagen falls about 2.1% a year after menopause; elasticity 1.5% a year. Types I and III collagen drop "as much as 30% in the first 5 years after menopause" (Brincat 1985). https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13502372/ and PMC7078862
- It's both texture and laxity. The fine crinkle is partly a dry, thin epidermis (worse when dehydrated); the fragility is dermal collagen and elastin loss. A moisturizer smooths the surface crinkle through stratum corneum hydration. Dermal change needs an active with biopsy evidence, at concentrations above OTC body lotions.

## What the actives can and can't do (part 4)
| Active | Body-skin evidence | Studied vs what's in OTC body creams |
|---|---|---|
| Retinol | Yes. Arm biopsy, 36 subjects, fine wrinkles improved, collagen up (Kafi 2007, Arch Dermatol) | 0.4% studied. Body lotions run 0.05 to 0.1%. |
| Glycolic acid | Yes. Forearm biopsy, ~25% thicker skin (Ditre 1996) | 20 to 25% studied. OTC cap is 10%. |
| Peptides | One 10-woman forearm study. Everything else is face. | trace |
| Hyaluronic acid | Hydration only. No body study. | 0.1 to 2% |
| Niacinamide | Face only (Bissett 2005) | up to 3% in body lotions |
| Ceramides | Body, but for dryness and flaking only. Nothing on laxity. | 0.1 to 4% |
| Topical collagen | None. Skin absorbs molecules under 500 Da; collagen is ~300,000 Da. (Bos & Meinardi 2000) | n/a |
| Crepe Erase "TruFirm" (dill, apple, ursolic acid) | Company study only, 30 to 54 women, method unstated. "No independent clinical studies whatsoever." (thedermreview) | undisclosed |

Bottom line: of the seven products, only Medix (retinol #9) carries an active with body-skin evidence, at a fraction of the studied dose. The rest are emollient bases with botanicals or actives in the back half of the list. Crepe Erase is olive oil, cocoa butter, dimethicone, and squalane plus dill extract. That's an emollient base, same category of effect as F-Balm, at $84 with water first.

Tallow's own evidence: none clinical. Say "customers report," never "clinically."

## Copy lines the science supports
- Seven crepey skin creams. Water first on all seven.
- Collagen in a cream can't get through your skin. The molecule is six hundred times too big.
- The silicone in five of these seven creams was tested on 26 people. It did not moisturize the skin.
- Crepe Erase's "clinical study" is 30 women, and the company won't say how it was measured.
- Retinol works on crepey arms in the study. At four to eight times the dose in any body lotion you can buy.
- After menopause you lose about 30 percent of your skin's collagen in five years, and the oil that kept it soft goes with it.

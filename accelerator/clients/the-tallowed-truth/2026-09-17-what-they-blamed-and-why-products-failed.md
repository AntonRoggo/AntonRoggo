# What customers thought was wrong, and why they think their old products failed

Sources checked 2026-09-17: raw review export (36,267), post-purchase survey summary, the Completed Analysis worksheet (which cites the Facebook comment set as C-codes), Top_Facebook_Ad_Comments.xlsx (did not parse into comment text on this pass; its findings are carried through the worksheet citations).

The most common answer is no answer. They don't have a theory of why the creams failed; they have a pile of them.

## What they thought the problem was (reviews)

Identity labels, not causes:
- eczema, psoriasis, rosacea, dermatitis: 1,017 (heavily body and family use)
- "sensitive skin": 420
- "I have dry skin": 143

Causes, when named:
- age, getting old: 257. "I am 74 and my skin suddenly looked wrinkly and old. After years of pampering and meticulous care and moisturizing... I remember because I was horrified and cried, 'do something!!!!' She smiled and said it was all a part of aging."
- climate, winter, dry air: 832 (seasonal)
- menopause, hormones: 97
- medication, chemo, thyroid: 57
- sun: 56. "63 years old former smoker and sun lover"
- not drinking enough water: 23
- genetics: 9

"I thought" statements are about the product (greasy, smell), not about their skin. "No idea what was wrong": 0. Oil loss: 2. Nobody arrives with a mechanism; they arrive with a label and a pile.

What professionals told them: "My doctor had said she thought it was contact dermatitis. Most things she prescribed didn't stop it." "My dermatologist laughed when I mentioned tallow and said I must have seen it on TikTok. Told me to use OTC products!" Grandmother, 1985: "it was all a part of aging."

## Survey and comments (from the earlier files)
- #1 stated reason to buy: "cleaner alternative to conventional skincare" 22.7%. "Heard tallow was good for my specific issue" 10%. Price last at 5.4%. The write-in reasons and the write-in "what almost stopped you" (521 answers) were not exported.
- Completed Analysis, limiting beliefs: "beef fat will be greasy or smell," "this could cause breakouts," "a small tin is poor value," "Facebook sellers may be unreliable," "I need expensive products for results." Fears: "making their skin worse, paying for another ineffective product, being fooled by a seller." Negative experiences: "products that failed to improve wrinkles, moisturizers that did not last, heavy or waxy finishes, offensive smells, breakouts." One comment watched the ads for a year; another refuses Facebook purchases after two bad experiences; one says it does not replace Botox.

## What it means for the false-belief block
Bitters names three wrong causes the viewer blamed. Ours has two real families to draw from:
1. What they blamed: age (257), sun (56), hormones (97).
2. What they believed about the fix: "I need the expensive stuff" (87 plus the worksheet belief), "the next one will work" (488 tried everything), "it's all marketing" (20, the post-hoc version).

Candidate line: "You thought it was your age. You thought you needed the expensive stuff. You thought the next one would finally be the one." Then "It was none of those things. It was your skin making less of its own oil... and thirty years of creams that were mostly water."

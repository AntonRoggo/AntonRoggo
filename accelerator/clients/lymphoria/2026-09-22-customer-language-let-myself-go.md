## 1. Black women, 50s, on weight and menopause (qualitative study, 27 women, mean age 54)

Source: PMC8969889, "It just seems like people are talking about menopause, but nobody has a solution" (Journal of Clinical and Translational Science, 2022). Focus groups, verbatim.

- "It drives me crazy that I can't get rid of this weight that I've gained."
- "This weight settling in the mid-area and I didn't have weight that settled there before."
- "A couple of years ago I did lose 50 pounds which seemed relatively easier than I've been trying to do over the last 6 months or so."
- "So it's a number of things that are happening and I don't have the answers, I don't know what to do. Because it's bothersome."
- "It just seems like people are talking about [menopause], but nobody has a solution."
- "I asked my doctor and the first thing she said is there's not a whole lot you can do during menopause. That just threw me off."
- "I've been so in a rut, I don't know, it's part depression, part a lot going on... stress at work... my motivation just fizzled out and I've been trying, every week I tell myself I'm going to go walk in the park. I tell myself that. And then I go buy some chips."

Companion study (PMC8896166, 33 Black women, mean age 43, ranked responses, no verbatim): top stressors were safety of children and raising Black children, being head of the family, and finances. Top ways stress drives weight: emotional eating, not enough time for exercise, fast food for lack of time or money.

## 2. A Black woman in her 50s on "let herself go" (Cynthia Bailey, Blavity)

- "I was finding myself not wanting to do the red carpets anymore. I just wanted to be more in the background, trying to hide the weight."
- "The comments... are like, 'She really let herself go. What happened to her?' I'm still human. And I found myself agreeing with some of the comments."

The second one is the whole honest beat: other people say it, and she agrees.

# Lymphoria structure: The aunt's phone call (cortisol, blame transfer)

Filed 2026-09-21 from the operator's script. Female lead, ten months into a betrayal (her sister is marrying her ex-husband, she is the bridesmaid). She calls her aunt Marcia in Kingston, who runs a herb and oil shop. The aunt is the first person to ask how she is. She hears the whole story, then puts the blame for the weight on what was done to the lead: their stress, her cortisol, her drainage shut down. Zero accountability for the lead. Written in verse, short lines, loose rhyme.

How to use: keep every line marked FIXED word for word. Only touch the SLOTS. Match the source line count and line breaks, do not add lines or transitions.

## 4. Slots

- **The helper:** relation, where she is, her credential (herb and oil shop). Must be someone who loves the lead and is outside the situation.
- **The wound and who "they" are:** sister and ex-husband here. "They" must be specific people or a specific force with a face, never "life" or "stress" in general.
- **Duration:** ten months. Used three times (holding it down, the stress, implied in "the years of hardened waste"), keep it consistent.
- **Effort items and the deadline event.**
- **The two "Every" lines:** what her endurance looked like.
- **Three symptom places.**

## 5. What must never change

- The helper asks how she is before she diagnoses anything. Care first, mechanism second. (Opt-in since 2026-09-28: ask the operator per script; the operator expects to leave it out more often than not.)
- The effort inventory comes before the blame transfer, so the viewer already knows she did everything right.
- "They did this to you" comes before the word cortisol. The blame lands first, the science explains it second.
- The injury is extended from heart to body in two short lines.
- Cortisol is the bridge, not the villain. The chain is: them, stress, cortisol, drainage shuts down. The villain stays human.
- Her sleeplessness and holding it together are the cause, and they read as strength, not as failure to cope.
- No line anywhere suggests she ate wrong, let herself go, or should have handled the stress better. Zero accountability for the lead.
- The blame comes back after the mechanism and turns into "we" and a goal.
- The helper's herb lines reuse the story's own cause ("the circulation the stress shut down").
- Ends on a command from someone who loves her, not a pitch.

# Lymphoria structure: The coworker relay (sister's insight)

Filed 2026-09-21 from the operator's script. Blue collar lead (Curtis, narrator) is on a drive with one of his team members (Keith). Keith brings up his own experience: his sister, who is into natural health, told him the thing nobody else said. Keith relays the mechanism, names his own result, pulls out the bottle. First-person narrated story, reported dialogue ("I said," "He said"), not a two-hander scene.

How to use: keep every line marked FIXED word for word. Only touch the SLOTS. Match the source pacing beat for beat, do not add lines or transitions.

## 1. Source section (canonical, verbatim)

Then his sister, she's into natural health, she told him something nobody else ever said. She said Keith, you don't have a weight problem. You have a drainage problem.
I said, a drainage problem.
He said, your body makes waste every single day. Dead cells. Old hormones. Toxins. All the junk food, the stress, the years of running on nothing, all of that leaves waste behind. And your body has a system that's supposed to flush it out. Your lymphatic system. But here's the thing. It has no pump. No heart pushing it. When you run a man into the ground for 20 years and he's eating trash and sleeping four hours and carrying everything on his back, that system stops. The waste doesn't leave. It sits in your tissue. It thickens. It hardens. It turns into this sludge that just stays there.
He said, that gut hanging over your belt right now. That puffy face every morning. That heaviness you feel when you wake up. That's not fat, Curtis. That's 22 years of waste that your body tried to drain and couldn't. You can't burn it off at the gym. You can't starve it out. You have to drain it.
I looked at him like he was out of his mind.

I said, Keith, I've got 35 pounds sitting on me. You're telling me I just need to drain it?
He said, I'm telling you I lost 28 pounds in six weeks and I didn't change a single thing about my diet. All I did was give my body what it needed to start draining again.
He reached into his bag and pulled out a small dark bottle.
He said, four herbs. Liquid. You put half a dropper under your tongue in the morning. Half a dropper at night. Tastes like honey. That's it.
Stillingia root breaks down the hardened waste sitting in your tissue. Prickly ash bark restarts your circulation so everything starts moving. Cleavers sweeps the loosened waste out. Red clover calms the inflammation so it doesn't build right back up. One herb alone won't do it. You need all four. And you need it liquid, under the tongue, so it hits your bloodstream in fifteen minutes. Capsules die in your gut and do nothing.
I didn't say anything for the rest of the drive.
That night I ordered it. Not because I believed him. Because I had nothing left to try.

## 4. Slots

- **Names and relation:** Keith, Curtis, the sister. The insight source needs one credential clause ("into natural health").
- **Biography lines in the mechanism:** the waste sources and the clog cause are written from the lead's actual life (junk food, stress, four hours of sleep, carrying everything on his back). Swap to match the avatar.
- **Three symptoms:** each one opens with "That."
- **The years:** tied to the lead's backstory established earlier in the script.
- **The two numbers:** lead's pounds to lose, helper's pounds lost and timeframe.
- **Setting word in the close:** the drive.

## 5. What must never change

- The insight comes from a third person. The helper relays it and is the living proof, he does not claim to be the expert.
- The reframe is two short sentences: not a weight problem, a drainage problem. The narrator echoes it.
- The clog cause is the lead's own biography, so the mechanism explains his life, not a textbook.
- Symptoms are pointed at his body before "that's not fat."
- Gym and starving are both closed off before "you have to drain it."
- "You're telling me" is answered with "I'm telling you."
- Herb roles run in mechanism order: break down, move, sweep out, keep it from rebuilding. Each role uses the same waste language the mechanism set up.
- The lead does not convert in the scene. He orders out of desperation, not belief.

# Human-voice pass: Ready to Assign (Anton and Roko), 2026-10-13

This pass was read-only. Nothing was changed in ClickUp. It covers 35 tasks and 210 flagged lines. It checks only the spoken and sung lines, plus the headline and ad text on statics. Visuals, notes and the locked mechanism and close wording are skipped. Every fix keeps the same sentence structure. Where a quoted line contains an em dash, the dash is in the original script.

## Patterns that repeat across scripts (fix once, fixes many)

1. **Broken stress and menopause template.** "Every night you [X] / That your body was supposed to drain / Just sits there" sings as if the nights "just sit there". It's in Dad, Metal, FormerFitness and the menopause scripts. The template needs a subject, for example: "Every night you [X] / Left waste your body was supposed to drain / And it just sits there".
2. **"Locked in / Hardened / Stuck".** This triplet sits in most of the template scripts and reads as AI rhythm. Keep two words.
3. **Herb lines missing "that".** "Restarting the circulation / Menopause shut down" sings as a broken sentence. Use "that menopause shut down".
4. **Product spec lists** ("four herbs, whole plants, water and glycerin, no alcohol, triple-filtered, third-party tested"). These appear in 6 of the 8 statics and read like a label.
5. **"Identity tests" is jargon.** Say it the way a person would: "they don't even know what's in the bag."
6. **Disclaimer closes in a character's mouth.** "Read the details for yourself", "See the current offer and choose for you", "Learn more at the link". These are in AnniversaryBetrayal, PassedOverPromotion, WeLied and ILied.
7. **The three sale songs (WeLied, ILied, TheySaidWWL) are the most robotic in the set.** They have brand-speak rhymes, em dashes in sung lines, and label words like "botanicals". WeLied also has a stage direction written as a lyric.
8. **Continuity slips (not voice).** ChristmasPartySlideshow says twelve years at the office but talks about a fifteen-year picture, and "six years" doesn't match the ten years used elsewhere. NewDad says "climbing frame", which is British; American dads say "jungle gym".

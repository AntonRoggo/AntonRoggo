I walked in, ordered a glass of wine. Looked around, saw him, but he avoided my gaze, so I questioned if I had correctly picked him out from the other bro-looking dudes in here. Then I saw him look at his phone. I checked mine, and he unmatched as I was looking.

Class fucking act.

So I'm gonna sit here and drink my overpriced wine. He's the dick. Why should I leave? And yes, I look like my pics. They're all recent.

Guess I just didn't do it for him in person.

I was halfway through the glass, thinking about having the bartender send him a drink with a message, when this much older gentleman sat down next to me and ordered a beer. I knew a story about that brand. I told it. He laughed, and we started talking.

I'm forty-six. He's probably early seventies. I said, I'd like to ask you a favor. I told him what happened. I said, would you mind sharing a drink with me, because I'd rather do that than walk out with my tail between my legs.

He said he lost his wife a year ago. He said he's terrified of dating. He said he'd be very happy to keep me company.

His name was Walt. We talked for an hour about everything. And I completely forgot about the guy at the end of the bar. No drink sent. No drink thrown. When I left, he was nowhere to be seen.

Night well spent.

Then I got home and took my dress off in front of the mirror.

And I understood what he saw.

I look like my pics. That's the problem. The pics are recent. The face is puffy in every one of them. The jawline I had at forty is gone. The dress that used to hang now sits on a belly that got there sometime after forty and never left. Twenty-two pounds since I turned forty. I'd just stopped looking.

I stood there and thought: maybe he wasn't the dick. Maybe I just didn't do it for him in person.

I almost believed that.

Because I had tried. Three years. Cut the carbs. Cut the wine to weekends. Pilates twice a week. Ten thousand steps. A trainer for four months. The face never changed. The belly never moved. My doctor didn't look up from the screen. "You're forty-six. It's perimenopause. Eat less, move more." I cried in the car.

Walt said something at the bar that stuck. He said he'd been going to the same bars for twenty years because he could walk to them, and he was twenty years older, and the rest of the patrons were not. He said there are bars that cater to grown-ups.

So the next Saturday I went to one.

Quiet. Real chairs. Nobody under forty. I sat at the bar and ordered the same wine, and the woman next to me ordered the same wine, and we laughed about it.

She was fifty-one. She looked thirty-eight. Not tight. Not filled. Just clear. Jawline. Waist. The kind of face I used to have.

I said, I have to ask. How?

She said, you want the real answer?

I said, I'm forty-six and I got unmatched from across a bar last week. Yes.

She said, you know those women who never seem to age? The ones who look thirty-eight at fifty-one? They're not luckier than you. Not more disciplined. They get drained. Every single day. It's the secret nobody tells you.

She said, you don't have a weight problem. You have a drainage problem. Your lymphatic system is your body's drain. It moves the waste your body makes out of you every single day. It has no pump. It hardens into sludge. That's not fat. That's waste. You can't out-train it. You can't starve it. You cannot burn waste. You have to drain it.

I said, so the Pilates, the steps, the trainer.

She said, three years of pushing on a shut drain. Open it, and all of that starts to count. Keep the wine. I still have mine.

She pulled a small dark bottle out of her purse and put it on the bar between our glasses.

Four herbs. Stillingia breaks down the hardened waste. Prickly ash restarts the circulation your forties slowed down. Cleavers sweeps it out. Red clover calms the swelling so it doesn't build back up. Half a dropper in the morning, half at night, under the tongue. Tastes like honey.

I said, there's no way drops fix twenty years of this.

She said, six weeks. By week one your face. By week three your jawline. By week six you won't recognize yourself. Watch.

Her name was Joanne. She sent me the link before I got home.

Week one: bathroom every hour. Face in the mirror was mine again. My ring spun. Told no one.

Week two: the jeans. Bloat gone by Thursday. My coworker at the coffee machine: "You sound different." I shrugged.

Week three: jawline. Slept through the night for the first time since forty.

Week four: down fourteen. I took new pictures. Same dress, same light. The app went from four matches a week to forty. One of them was Paul.

Week five: down nineteen. The dress I bought for the date that never happened. Put it on. It hung. I cried in the mirror in a different way.

Week six: Paul asked where I wanted to meet. I said, I know a place.

The same bar. Saturday instead of Thursday. I walked in early, ordered the overpriced wine, and sat where I'd sat.

I felt eyes.

He was at the end of the bar with two of the bro-looking dudes. He looked at me the way you look at someone you're trying to place. Then he placed me. His mouth opened. He got up and walked over.

"Oh my God. Tara? You look so good. What did you do?"

I didn't answer. I didn't smile. I picked up my glass.

The door opened. Paul. Fifty, salt and pepper, the kind of shoulders that come from work and not a gym. He kissed my cheek, looked at the man standing at my elbow, and said:

"Do you know this guy?"

I said, "No. I don't."

And I didn't.

Later, in the bathroom, a woman my age at the sink caught my eye in the mirror. "I'm sorry, I have to ask. I just got unmatched at that bar last month and I'm fifty and I look like it. What are you doing?"

I told her. She ordered at the sink.

My coworker saw the bottle on my desk the next week. "Is this why you look like that?" Two weeks later she sent me a photo. "I look like you."

Here's what I know now.

He wasn't the dick because he unmatched me. He was the dick because he couldn't see me under it. And I couldn't either. Twenty years of wine nights and a body that stopped draining at forty locked everything in, and the second I drained it, I got me back. Not thirty-year-old me. Don't let them sell you Ozempic and another skincare routine when your body just needs to drain what your forties locked in.

Four herbs. Half a dropper morning and night. Joanne said they sell out constantly. Small batches. I checked yesterday and they were almost out. If you still see the orange button below, you got lucky. Sixty-day money-back guarantee. But honestly, after six weeks, you won't need it.

And find the bars for grown-ups. Who knew.

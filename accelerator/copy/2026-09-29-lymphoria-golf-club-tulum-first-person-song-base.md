PRODUCTION SCRIPT

Block 1: The setup
Visual: Narrator to lens. B-roll: a signing table, champagne; a new watch on Mark's wrist; the club gates; four men teeing off; Dave at a Christmas party with a much younger woman; Steve's girlfriend on his arm; Mark buttoning a fitted golf shirt; his phone face down on the counter.
Audio: "Let me back up. Mark sold his software company two years ago. Overnight he was a big shot. New watch. The club. Golf five days a week with his boys. Dave, Steve, Tom. All on their second wives. Then the second wives turned into girls. Dave brought the girl from the pro shop to the Christmas party. Steve's new one is twenty-six and calls him babe in front of everyone. And Mark started noticing. New shirts. Whiter teeth. His phone face down on the counter. One night he stopped in our bedroom doorway and said, 'Becca, you've gotten a little soft. Maybe look into something. For yourself.'"

Block 2: The catch
Visual: Reading glasses on the counter; the club parking lot; a golf cart behind a barn, two figures, her laughing, his hand. Wide, no faces.
Audio: "Two weeks later I drove to the club to bring him the reading glasses he forgot. I found him behind the cart barn with the cart girl. His hand on her thigh. Her giggling. She's twenty-two. He's sixty-six."

Block 3: The mirror
Visual: Clubhouse ladies' room. She stands at the mirror in her coat, hands flat on the counter.
Audio: "I didn't scream. I walked into the clubhouse bathroom and looked in the mirror for the first time in two years. Sixty-three. Twenty-two pounds since I turned sixty. Swollen stomach. Puffy eyes. My jawline gone into my neck. I stood there and thought, I know what a twenty-two-year-old has that I don't. I almost believed him."

Block 4: The effort
Visual: Walking shoes at dawn, a salad, the food app, the scale. The doctor's office. Her in the car.
Audio: "Because I had tried. Four miles every morning for a year. No bread. No wine. Every bite logged. Nothing moved. My doctor gave me thirty seconds. He said, 'At sixty-three, hormones change. Eat less, walk more.' I cried in the car."

Block 5: His card, Tulum
Visual: His Amex on the kitchen counter. A boarding pass on her phone. The plane window. A Tulum resort pool, white loungers, every one taken but one.
Audio: "The next morning I took his Amex and booked a flight to Tulum. I didn't leave a note. I just needed to be somewhere he wasn't. First afternoon, every chair at the pool was taken except one. Next to a woman reading in a black one-piece. Mid-sixties. Waist. Arms. Jawline. I said, 'Can I sit here?' She moved her bag."

Block 6: The ask
Visual: The two loungers. Becca in a cover-up, holding it closed over her middle. Joyce turns a page, doesn't look up. Then lowers her sunglasses.
Audio: "I lasted ten minutes. I said, 'I'm sorry. You look so good for your age. What are you doing?' She lowered her sunglasses and said, 'Honey. I was where you are.' I said, 'Where am I?' She said, 'Alone in Tulum on somebody else's card.'"

Block 7: Her story
Visual: Joyce turns on her side to face Becca. Plain, not preachy.
Audio: "She said, 'Four years ago I was forty pounds heavier. Walking every morning. Eating like a bird. My husband had stopped reaching for me at night. I came down here for the lymphatic drainage massages. And the woman who did them told me the thing nobody at home ever had. I can move it on this table once a month. The drain has to open every day.'"

Block 8: The mechanism
Visual: Simple animation: the drain slowing, then shut. Waste sitting in the lower belly, the face, under the jaw, thickening. No red glow, no fluid graphics. Back to Joyce on the lounger.
Audio: "She said, 'You don't have sixty weight. You have menopause sludge. When estrogen drops, your lymphatic system stops working. It has no pump. Every glass of wine. Every hormone. Every toxin from the last thirty years just sits there. Locked in. Hardened. Stuck. In your belly. In your face. Under your jaw. That's not fat, honey. That's waste. You can walk four miles for the rest of your life. You can't burn waste. You have to drain it.'"

Block 9: The bottle
Visual: Joyce reaches into her straw bag and sets a small dark dropper bottle on the table between the loungers, label facing away. Honey-colored liquid in the pipette. Four herb tags, plain.
Audio: "She reached into her bag and set a small dark bottle on the table between us. The liquid looked like honey. She said, 'Four herbs. Stillingia breaks down the hardened waste. Prickly ash restarts the circulation menopause shut down. Cleavers sweeps it out. Red clover calms the swelling so it doesn't come back. Half a dropper morning and night. Take it. I always bring two. Six weeks. By week three your waist. By week six you won't recognize yourself. Watch.' Her name was Joyce."

Block 10: Six weeks
Visual: Montage with timestamps: the ring spinning; the jeans; the scale; the dress on the bedroom door. Mark passing it with his golf bag, not looking.
Audio: "I flew home Sunday. Mark didn't ask where I'd been. He didn't check the card. Day four, my face. My ring spinning loose. Week two, the bloat I'd carried for two years, gone. Week four, down fourteen. Mark didn't notice. He was at the club by seven. Week six, down nineteen. The dress from his retirement party I never wore because it was a size too small. It fit."

Block 11: The spring dinner
Visual: The club dining room. She walks in, in the dress. Heads turn. The foursome's table by the window, Mark's back to the door. Dave sees her first. Mark turns. Kaylee at the bar, glasses in hand, looking.
Audio: "The club's spring dinner. He'd told me I didn't have to come. I walked in. Every head turned. Dave saw me first. His table went quiet, and Mark turned around with his mouth open. The cart girl was working the bar. He stood up and said, 'Becca? You look incredible. What did you do?' I didn't smile. I didn't answer. I walked past him to the far side of the room."

Block 12: The close
Visual: Back to the kitchen island. The same bottle, now turned so the label reads. She holds it, doesn't present it.
Audio: "He wasn't right. He was a coward, and menopause just locked everything in. I didn't read the label until the flight home. Lymphoria. Four herbs. Half a dropper morning and night. 30-day money-back guarantee, but after six weeks you won't need it. That's all it took for me."

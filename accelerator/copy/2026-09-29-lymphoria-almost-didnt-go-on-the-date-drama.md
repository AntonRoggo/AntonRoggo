## HOLD

**Scene 1: The setup**
[Linda's kitchen. Her friend holds up her phone with his photo.]
FRIEND: He's in his early forties.
LINDA: (laughs out loud) Absolutely not. I'm sixty-one. I'll feel ridiculous.
FRIEND: Linda, you keep waiting to feel ready. You're never going to feel ready. Just go.
[Linda looks at the phone. Doesn't say no again.]

**Scene 2: The dinner**
[A restaurant, candles between them. Linda has a glass of wine in her hand. The date is mid-story. He stops, looks at her.]
THE DATE: Can I ask you something? What do you do? You look incredible. It's unreal.
[Linda's face. Hold on it.]
OST: Two years ago, she'd stopped looking in mirrors.

**Scene 3: Two years ago**
[Flashback. Linda walks past a shop window, catches her reflection, doesn't recognize the tired, puffy face. A belly where there had never been one. The jawline gone.]
[Her bedroom. She pulls on a dress she's worn for years. It pulls at every seam. She takes it off and hangs it at the back of the closet, next to the others.]
[Her granddaughter's birthday party. Everyone gathers for a photo. Linda steps out of frame and picks up plates instead.]
[Linda turns down a dinner invitation on her phone. Then another.]
OST: "It's just age. This is what sixty looks like."

**Scene 4: The expensive stuff**
[Linda's doorstep. A $200 meal-plan box in beautiful insulated packaging. In the kitchen, a cabinet that looks like a supplement store: bottles, teas, powders. She lines up five supplements and takes them one by one.]
[Night. Linda on the couch logging food into an app. The clock goes from 9:00 to 9:15.]
[Morning. She turns sideways in the mirror. The belly hasn't moved. Her face is the same. She closes the banking app on her phone.]
LINDA: (to herself) That's it. I give up.

**Scene 5: The doctor's office**
[A clinic waiting room. Linda waits, there about something unrelated. The young assistant, Korean, a waist like a college girl, is making conversation while she files charts.]
LINDA: (half-joking) What do you do?
THE ASSISTANT: Back home, the obsession isn't diet pills or the shots at all. It's drainage. Every woman my mother's age gets drained.
LINDA: Drained?
THE ASSISTANT: Your lymphatic system is your body's drain. It carries out the waste your body makes every day, and it has no pump. After menopause it slows down and stops. The waste sits in your tissue, thickens, and hardens into sludge. That puffy face in the morning, that belly that won't move? That's not fat. That's waste. And you can't burn waste. You have to drain it.
THE ASSISTANT: You don't need ten diets. You need the drain open, every single day.
THE ASSISTANT: My mother switched to one. Four herbs in one bottle. It's called Lymphoria. Stillingia to break down the hardened waste, prickly ash to restart the circulation, cleavers to sweep it out, red clover to calm the swelling so it doesn't build back up. Half a dropper under the tongue, morning and night.
[Linda writes it on the back of a receipt. Folds it into her purse. At home, the receipt sits on the counter under her keys.]

**Scene 6: The weeks**
[Linda finds the receipt. A small dark bottle arrives. Half a dropper under her tongue at the sink.]
OST: Week 1
[Linda heading to the bathroom again, and again. Morning: she wakes up and her face isn't puffy. She presses her belly. Calmer.]
OST: Week 3
[Mid-routine, she stops. Turns sideways in the mirror. Stares. The belly has gone down. Her jawline has an edge again. She touches her face.]
OST: Week 6
[The scale: nineteen pounds down. The silk dress with the bare shoulder, from the back of the closet. It zips.]
[The supplement cabinet: she sweeps the teas, powders and bottles into a bag. The meal-plan box goes back out the door. One bottle left, by the sink.]

**Scene 7: Back to the dinner**
[The restaurant again. The date waits for her answer.]
THE DATE: What do you do?
LINDA: (laughs, the good kind) Half a dropper. Twice a day. That's it.
[Montage: Linda at a dinner with friends. The silk dress with the bare shoulder. Her hair silver, and she loves it. Her granddaughter's next birthday: Linda front and center in the photo.]

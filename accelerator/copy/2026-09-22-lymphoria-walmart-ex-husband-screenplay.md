FORMAT KEY

💬 CHARACTER NAME on its own line is spoken dialogue.
🗣 DENISE (V.O.) is her telling the story in past tense.
🔊 sound.
⏱ title card.
📌 the beat note: what the scene does and where it comes from.

CAST: Denise, 54, narrator. Reggie, her ex-husband, 60s. His Girl, 41. The Gym Woman, 60s, sculpted. Ana, 50s, Brazilian lymphatic drainage therapist. Her Daughter, late 20s.

HOOK 1: THE PAPER TOWELS (12s)
📌 The source's opening, 1:1. Four facts in four lines, and his voice before she turns.
🎬 Mid-action. Walmart, paper goods aisle, mid-morning. DENISE (54, a shirt that fits her now) reaching up for a pack of paper towels. Behind her, a man's voice.

SCENE 2: THE MIRROR (22s)
📌 The honest beat, rebuilt as a realization the divorce prompted. The lines trace to real women: "I have avoided mirrors for at least 4 years," "one glance in the face mirror on a morning and I was done," "I cant believe how much I have let myself go." Life built up is shown, not listed.
🎬 Denise's bathroom, morning. Four years younger and heavier: the face puffy, swollen under the jaw, a loose top chosen to hide her middle. On the counter, an envelope from a law office, opened. She looks up at the mirror. Really looks.
🗣 DENISE (V.O.): After the divorce, I finally looked in the mirror. (a beat) I'd been avoiding it for years. One look, and I was done.
🎬 She leans in. Touches under her jaw.
💬 DENISE (to the mirror, quiet): I really did let myself go.
🗣 DENISE (V.O.): I wasn't trying to. Life got harder and things built up.
🎬 [B-ROLL, quick] Denise in work clothes, 6 a.m., keys, out the door. The kitchen table: a tuition statement, a stack of bills, one chair pulled out. On the fridge, a photo: two kids, one in a cap and gown.
🗣 DENISE (V.O.): Working every day. Two kids, one still in university. And by the end, all of it on me. (a beat) The weight settled in my middle, where it never used to. My face puffy every morning, swollen under the jaw.

SCENE 4: EVERYTHING (24s)

SCENE 6: ANA (70s)
📌 The turn. The banked mechanism in Ana's mouth: the diagnostic by touch (soft or hard, from the locker room), the cortisol chain and endurance-as-clog from the aunt script, "life did" instead of "he did" per the operator's ruling, "then he left and it shut all the way" for the second hit. 🗣 DENISE (V.O.): She put her hands on my stomach. And went quiet. Then she asked me the one question nobody had asked in four years.
💬 ANA: When did this start?
🗣 DENISE (V.O.): And I told her everything. Reggie. The gym. The diets. The tears. The scale stuck still for four years.

🗣 DENISE (V.O.): Things built up. (a beat) She meant it literally.
💬 ANA: You can starve all you want. You can walk all you want. You cannot burn waste. You have to drain it. (a beat) My hands can move some of it today. But I see you once. This has to drain every day.

## What moved in the reformat

- The brand name used to be in Denise's answer to the 41-year-old ("It's lymphatic drainage drops, from a company called Lymphoria"). That question is gone, so the name now lands once, in the close. "You won't find it on these shelves" folded into "You won't see it at Walmart or GNC."
- Scene 1 ends on "You look incredible" and cuts to the card. The source ran the flashback straight from that line; the card does the same job on screen.
- Ana's bottle is unnamed in Scene 6. Same as the aunt and coworker scripts.
- The 41-year-old's basket and yoga mat are staging now, not V.O., so the mirror ("everything I used to do") is there for anyone who wants it without a line.

# Workshop: Retinol script vs. winner bank

Script under review: "Please stop using retinol on your face after 50." Full VSL, ~2 minutes, woman's testimonial voice with expert friend (Karen, esthetician) as the hinge.

Compared against: all 21 proven top ads in winners/ for The Tallowed Truth.

---

## What this script does well

**Specificity is real.** Karen, 26 years as an esthetician, $600, eight months, 1% retinol, $180 facials every six weeks, the kitchen light, the book club. The winners use the same move: Shoppers Drug Mart, Costco parking lot, St. Thomas, Ontario. This tracks.

**The reframe is the sharpest line in the whole account.** "You don't have a wrinkle problem. You have a strategy problem." And: "You've been attacking the lines. The problem is the skin they're sitting in." No winning ad has a line this quotable. That's a strength if the audience absorbs it, a risk if it reads as written.

**The identity beat is powerful.** Stopped being in photographs. Told her husband the lighting wasn't catching her right. Daughter asked why she won't be in the picture. No winning ad goes this deep emotionally. This could be the thing that makes the ad outperform everything in the bank, or it could be too heavy for an audience used to lighter creative.

**The objection handling is built in.** "I want to be honest about how that sounded to me" pre-empts the beef-fat objection. "The lines were still there, nothing erased them" hedges the result honestly. Both of these mirror what the winners do naturally.

**The timeline is realistic and calibrated.** Four days for tightness, two weeks for plumpness, hedged expectations. Better than the vaguer winner claims ("women are getting compliments").

---

## Where it diverges from the winners

### 1. The voice is too polished

Every winning ad sounds like a phone recording in one take. Filler is the credibility signal:
- "uh, we, we do, we charge more than some of the super cheap tallow balms"
- "she's like, are you... are you around here?"
- "five-star, or 12,000 reviews now, sorry, uh, most of them five stars"

This script has zero filler, zero self-correction, zero trailing off. Every sentence is complete, rhythmic, structured. Good writing. But good writing is the opposite of what's winning for this account. The messiness is the trust.

### 2. "I used to be just like you"

The "just like you" line tells the viewer she's watching an ad. It's a direct-response copywriting cliché and no winning ad uses it or anything close to it. The winners establish relatability through the STORY, not through a declaration. The parking lot woman doesn't say "I used to be skeptical just like you." She just IS skeptical, and the story shows it.

### 3. The command hook

"Please stop using retinol on your face after 50" is an authority command. Every winning hook is either a story opening or a casual observation:
- "Okay, the video about my attractive 50-year-old neighbor..."
- "So I was standing beside this woman at a concert..."
- "I just bought a popular face cream at the Shoppers Drug Mart..."
- "So I was walking out of the grocery store..."

Commands position the speaker as someone telling you what to do. The winners position the speaker as someone telling you what happened to them. One sounds like an ad. The other sounds like a friend.

### 4. The teardown is too clean

"That's a life sentence." Punchy, memorable, reads as scripted.

In the winners, Ben gives the competition a concession before dismissing it:
- "I haven't used that product. I don't know, maybe it's great."
- "I don't think that works as well as my Botox, like I'm always gonna need my Botox."
- "she was, would have been attractive, but it was too obvious"

The hedge-then-dismiss move builds trust. This script attacks retinol and Botox directly without conceding anything. The audience's guard goes up when a speaker is too certain about what's wrong with their current approach.

### 5. The emotional register is different

The winners are light, funny, surprising, observational. The "I stopped being in photographs" section is confessional, almost painful. This isn't wrong. It's a different lane. But it's untested for this account, and the brand's entire winner bank earns trust through LOW emotional pressure, not high.

### 6. The close is a tagline

"It's not your face. It's not your age. It's the approach." reads as a copywriter's line. The winning closes are loose and personal:
- "F-Balm for the win, peace."
- "I'll see you there."
- "check it out below."

The tightness of the close reinforces the "this was written by a professional" signal.

### 7. Em dashes

"nothing erased them [em dash] but they weren't sitting in dry, papery skin anymore" has two em dashes. Voice rule: never.

---

## The structural difference

The winners follow this shape:
Casual story opening > story unfolds > proof arrives THROUGH the story > mechanism mentioned briefly > product and guarantee > loose close

This script follows:
Authority command > compressed failure stack > emotional confession > expert diagnosis > competitor teardown > product introduction > result timeline > tagline close

The winners are HORIZONTAL. They wander through a story and the product surfaces naturally. This script is VERTICAL. It's a deliberate funnel from problem to solution, structured like a VSL.

Both can work. But for this account, horizontal is proven. Vertical is untested.

---

## Recommendations

The bones of this script are strong. The specifics are real, the reframe is sharp, the identity beat is powerful. The question is whether to FIX it to match the winning voice or TEST it as a deliberately different format.

**If fixing to match the winner voice:**
- Kill "I used to be just like you." Let the story do the work.
- Rewrite the hook as a story opening, not a command. Example: "My friend Karen, who's been an esthetician for twenty-six years, told me to stop using retinol. I thought she was out of her mind."
- Add filler and self-correction to the delivery. The messiness is the credibility.
- Give retinol a concession before dismissing it. "Look, retinol works for some people. I'm not saying it's garbage. But after eight months of it..."
- Loosen the close. Drop the tagline.
- Remove the em dashes.
- Keep the identity beat (photographs, daughter). It's the best part and could be the move that outperforms the bank.

**If testing as a different format:**
- Run it alongside a winner in the same angle (Botox-in-the-Caribbean or natural-aging-vs-obvious-Botox).
- Accept that it reads as more polished/scripted and see if a different audience segment responds.
- Still kill "I used to be just like you" regardless. That line hurts both formats.

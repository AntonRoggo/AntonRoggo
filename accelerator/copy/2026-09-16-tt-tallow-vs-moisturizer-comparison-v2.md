## HOLD

Drugstore moisturizer versus beef tallow.

They both promise to smooth your skin, but NOT in the same way.

Your moisturizer follows the drugstore formula. It's 70 to 80 percent water, so it sits on top of your skin and evaporates. The rest is mostly glycerin to hold the water and silicone to make it feel smooth. And silicone, tested on 26 people, did not moisturize the skin.

But here's the part most people miss. Your skin looks wrinkly and crepey because it stopped making its own oil. After menopause it makes about a third less every ten years. And water doesn't replace oil.

(Oil-gap beat rewritten 2026-09-16 to state the consequence. "Thin" excluded: not customer language for the face, and the literature attributes thinning to estrogen and collagen loss, not sebum. "Looks" keeps the claim on appearance, which a dry epidermis supports. Alternate order: "After menopause, your skin makes about a third less of its own oil every ten years. Without it, your skin dries out, and dry skin looks wrinkly and crepey.")

Beef tallow, on the other hand, takes a very different approach. Your skin's oil is three things: fatty acids, squalene, and wax. Beef tallow is stearic, palmitic, and oleic acid, the same three fatty acids in your skin's oil. Olive oil is the richest plant source of squalene. Beeswax is wax. So your skin absorbs it instead of wearing it. The Tallowed Truth's F-Balm is five ingredients: tallow, olive oil, honey, beeswax, frankincense. Made in-house in Ontario.

One reviewer, 65, wrote that her wrinkly neck "is not as wrinkly any more." Another, with deep smoker lines above her lip: "they are diminishing."

That just shows how far the tallow gets. Under the lines, not on top of them.

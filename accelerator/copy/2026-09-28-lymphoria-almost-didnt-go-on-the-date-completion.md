## Phase 3: Execution

### HOOKS

**V1**
I almost didn't go on the date.

**V2**
A man in his early thirties asked me what I do to look like this. I'm sixty-one. Two years ago I'd stopped looking in mirrors.

**V3**
At sixty-one, I went to dinner with a man nearly thirty years younger. Halfway through, he stopped mid-sentence and asked what I do. Six weeks earlier, I couldn't zip a dress.

### HOLD

My friend had set it up, and when she told me he was in his early thirties I actually laughed out loud. "Absolutely not," I said. "I'm sixty-one. I'll feel ridiculous."

She said one thing that changed my mind: "Linda, you keep waiting to feel ready. You're never going to feel ready. Just go."

So I went. And halfway through dinner, candles between us, a glass of wine in my hand, he stopped mid-sentence, looked at me, and said, "Can I ask you something? What do you do? You look incredible. It's unreal."

I want to be honest about why that moment hit me so hard. Because it wasn't always like this.

**Two years ago I'd stopped looking in mirrors**

Not dramatically. Quietly. The way a lot of women my age do.

I'd catch my reflection in a shop window and not recognize the tired, puffy face looking back. A belly where there had never been one. A jawline that had quietly gone. The dresses I'd worn for years now pulled at every seam and made it worse. I started avoiding photos. Then I started avoiding the camera at my own granddaughter's birthday.

I told myself it was just age. That this is what sixty looks like and I should make peace with it.

But quietly, I'd stopped wearing the dresses I loved. I'd stopped going to dinners. And somewhere in there, I'd stopped feeling like me.

**I tried the expensive stuff. I want my money back.**

Like most women, I assumed the answer was money.

So I spent it. The $200-a-month meal plan in the beautiful insulated box. The supplements. The "miracle" teas everyone on the internet swore by. I had a kitchen cabinet that looked like a supplement store and an app that took me fifteen minutes a night to log.

And my body? Marginally less bloated for a day. That's it. None of it touched the belly. None of it touched my face. I was stacking five supplements and seeing nothing, except a smaller bank balance.

I was genuinely ready to give up.

**What my doctor's assistant told me**

It was an offhand comment, actually. I'd gone in about something unrelated, and the young assistant, Korean, a waist like a college girl, was making conversation while I waited.

I asked her, half-joking, what she did. She told me that back home, the obsession isn't diet pills or the shots at all. It's drainage. Every woman her mother's age gets drained.

Your lymphatic system is your body's drain, she said. It carries out the waste your body makes every day, and it has no pump. After menopause it slows down and stops. The waste sits in your tissue, thickens, and hardens into sludge. That puffy face in the morning, that belly that won't move? That's not fat. That's waste. And you can't burn waste. You have to drain it.

"You don't need ten diets," she said. "You need the drain open, every single day."

She mentioned the one her own mother had switched to, four herbs in one bottle called Lymphoria. Stillingia to break down the hardened waste, prickly ash to restart the circulation, cleavers to sweep it out, red clover to calm the swelling so it doesn't build back up. Half a dropper under the tongue, morning and night. I wrote it down on the back of a receipt and, frankly, almost forgot about it.

**Three weeks in, I stopped mid-routine and just stared**

I'm not going to tell you it was magic overnight. It wasn't.

But by the end of the first week I was in the bathroom every hour, and my face wasn't puffy when I woke up. The bloat that had lived in my belly for two years had calmed right down.

It was around week three that I actually stopped, turned sideways in the mirror, and stared. The belly had gone down. My jawline had an edge again. My face looked... like it had drained overnight. Like it used to, years ago, before I'd stopped paying attention.

By week six, nineteen pounds, and the silk dress with the bare shoulder zipped.

And the part I still can't get over: it replaced everything. The teas, the supplements, the app, all of it, gone, folded into one bottle and one ten-second step. My fifteen-minute routine became half a dropper. My cluttered cabinet is now one bottle by the sink.

I went from spending the most money for the worst results, to spending a fraction of it for the first real change I'd seen in a decade.

**Which brings me back to that dinner**

When he asked what I do, I laughed, the good kind, this time, and told him the truth. Half a dropper. Twice a day. That's it.

But here's what I actually want you to take from this, because it isn't really about a man nearly thirty years younger thinking I look incredible.

It's that I'm going to dinners again. I'm wearing the silk dress with the bare shoulder. I let my hair go silver and I love it. I'm in the photos now, front and center. I stopped waiting to feel ready, and somewhere along the way, I started feeling like myself again.

If you've been quietly avoiding mirrors, avoiding cameras, telling yourself this is just what your age looks like, please hear me. It doesn't have to.

You don't need the $200 meal plan. You don't need the shots. You need the drain open. Four herbs, one bottle, half a dropper twice a day.

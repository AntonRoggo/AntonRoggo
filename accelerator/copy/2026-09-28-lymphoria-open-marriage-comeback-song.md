## 4. Story logic pass (workflows/story-ad-logic.md)

Chain: married at 19, the business, twenty years alone → Brooke pushes, the park, Val, six months in the gym, nothing moved → the ask: not happy, not enough, the secretary → "divorce him" / "I don't believe in it" → Val and Brooke: say yes, on transparency → he's with the secretary that week → the mirror, "I almost believed him" → Val at the door, asks first, the effort on record → "he did this to you," heart then body → cortisol, the drain, no pump, nothing leaves → waiting up and holding it together sat there, hardened → belly, face, jaw → "I'm a trainer and I'll tell you what no trainer will: that's not fat" → "I had it after my divorce" → "he put this weight on you, then asked to leave you for it" → the bottle, four herbs, six weeks → the weeks, Brooke's laptop at week four, the dress at five → the first date at six, Ray watches her leave, she tells him → four dates, three stayed, every name → he screams, she says no → every night, "almost said the word" → "let's close it" / no → hotel, served, "she cheated," the screenshots → the church women → the business in her name → the courthouse steps, Theo's hand, walks past → coward, not right → the close.

What it caught and what I did:
1. **Every hook clause answers "for what."** "I said yes" and "the last word" both pay off: the transparency reports and the business. V3's "begged me to close it" is the "let's close it" scene.
2. **The guide can know the secret (checklist 4).** Val is a trainer who trained her six months with nothing moving, so "that's not fat" is a confession against her own trade, and "after my divorce it was me" is where she learned the drops. The source gives both: the trainer friend, divorced.
3. **The cortisol rule, all seven pieces.** Val asks "how are you" first; nobody had in twenty years. The effort is listed before the blame. "He did this to you" lands before the word cortisol. Heart, then body. Cortisol is the bridge and Ray keeps his face ("twenty years alone in a marriage"). Waiting up and holding it together for Brooke are the clog, written as endurance. The blame comes back as a mission: "He put this weight on you, then asked to leave you for it. We're taking it back."
4. **The concession dial.** "I almost believed him" is 002's setting, not 001's. The reconfirmed Walmart ruling says she never agrees on camera; "almost" keeps the doubt and doesn't concede. Cut it to "And I thought, maybe he's right / Maybe I'm not enough" then straight to Val if you want zero.
5. **The wife starts overweight, per the operator.** Six months in the gym before the ask so her effort is on record before he says "not enough." Ray's ask is then about her body, which is why the mechanism's absolution has to land on him.
6. **She tells him every time, and it costs him.** Transparency was her condition, he said yes "so fast," so every report is his own deal. "He wanted transparency. He got it." The fights, the near-word, "let's close it" follow the source's order.
7. **Dramatize, don't assert.** "Ray watched me leave in that dress, one beat too long" is the stare; "He almost said the word, he stopped himself" is the source's line; "His mother read every one and stopped talking" is the grandparents; the courthouse is the stranger's stare with Theo's hand. No "he regretted it" anywhere.
8. **Timeline.** Six weeks to the first date. The dates, the fights, "close it," the hotel, the divorce and the courthouse are after the six weeks and the song lets them be months ("the day it was final"). Brooke is 22, married-at-19 puts Diane at 41 if Brooke was born at 19; she is 39, so Brooke is 20. Either fix Brooke to 20 or Diane to 41. Left Brooke unaged in the song (no number on her) so it holds as written; the sheet says 22 from the source.
9. **Characters act like people.** She refuses the divorce for a reason the source gives (her faith), then agrees to the open marriage on a condition she can enforce. Ray asks to close it only after the reports, which is the source. The church women and his mother arrive after he tells them she cheated.
10. **The business is a kicker, not the spine (operator ruling).** The storyline is the open marriage, the drops, the men, the divorce, Theo. The business gets six lines after the courthouse: it made him rich, rich enough to want a secretary, and it was in her name the whole time. She does not take it over; the song never says she runs it. The setup plants it twice so the kicker is a turn and not a contradiction: "My father set up a business for him in the city," and "The business made him money, the money made him a belly, and a secretary." No hook mentions it.

# w36. ScaleIsBroken (the woman who was skinny her whole life steps on her sister-in-law's scale, says "this thing's broken," and hears through the door that it isn't)

FORMAT: SONG

Why song: the moment is one line through a bathroom door, not a scene of dialogue. The power is the slow identity loss after it (the closet, the lunches she stops going to) and the return to the same scale, which is Spine A's mirror, six weeks and public payoff. Flight fork: her best friend drives in.

## Source

Source summary (our words): A patient story on a commercial weight-loss treatment site. The woman says she had always been slim, then gained weight quickly around menopause and after giving up smoking, until the scale showed a number she could not square with how she saw herself. She describes losing confidence, avoiding clothes shopping and pulling back from social plans. She reports losing a large amount of weight over most of a year on the provider's program, along with an improvement in a pre-diabetes reading, and says she now feels "more like me." Her medical and treatment claims are the provider's marketing and are not verified. Nothing in our script uses her name, her treatment or her numbers; the family scale scene is invented.

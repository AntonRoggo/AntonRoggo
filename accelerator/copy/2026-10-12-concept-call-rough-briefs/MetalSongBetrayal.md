**ROUGH BRIEF: idea from the Oct 12 concept call. V numbers and editor get assigned later.**

#### THE IDEA
An untapped ICP from the call: older men who love aggressive rock ("you think you can break me"), many with rough childhoods. A 48-year-old mechanic's wife walks out and tells him he let himself go, the same way his father walked out on him at nine. The song is angry and defiant, Linkin Park energy, with a shouted chorus, "You thought you broke me." His old drummer, who went through the same divorce and got himself back, shows him the drain. The emotional core: two people walked out on him, and he refuses to stay down. Payoff: he plays a bar gig and she walks in.

#### EDITING GUIDELINES
Song ad, aggressive rock/metal style: heavy guitars, verses half-sung and half-growled, the chorus shouted. Duane sings every plain line, including other people's quotes. `Orange code lines` are visuals for the editor, never sung. Each version is one hook + the full Hold Body.
*   **V1:** Version 1 + Hold Body.
*   **V2:** Version 2 + Hold Body.
*   **V3:** Version 3 + Hold Body.

**CAST**

*   **Duane:** White, 48. Runs a two-bay auto shop, fixes every car on his street. Played guitar in a bar band at 25. His father walked out when he was nine. *   **Young Duane:** White, 9. 1987 flashback, on the porch.
*   **Bar crowd (about 60):** mixed, 30s to 50s, a packed dive bar.

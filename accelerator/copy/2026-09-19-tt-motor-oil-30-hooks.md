# Motor oil in a cup: 30 hooks

Visual: she pours motor oil into a cup. HOLD opens: "Well, assuming you just said no to that and I really hope you did. Why would you ever put any of these products on your skin?" So every hook is a question whose answer is obviously no.

Operator's three: Would you drink this? / Would you put this on your skin? / Would you put motor oil on your skin every morning?

## A. Drink it (the cup)
1. Would you drink this?
2. Would you take one sip of this for a hundred dollars?
3. If I handed you this with your coffee tomorrow morning, would you drink it?
4. You're careful about what you eat. So would you drink this?
5. Would you let your granddaughter take a sip of this?

## B. Put it on your skin
6. Would you put this on your skin?
7. Would you put motor oil on your skin every morning?
8. Would you rub this on your face before bed tonight?
9. Would you rub this into the lines around your eyes?
10. Would you put this on your lips?
11. Would you put this on your face every day for the next thirty years?

## C. Someone you love
12. Would you rub this on your grandbaby's cheeks?
13. Would you put this on your daughter's face?
14. Would you put this on your skin and then let the dog lick your leg?
15. Would you let your husband put this on your back?

## D. The label
16. If the label said "made from crude oil," would you put it on your face?
17. If the first ingredient was motor oil, would you still buy the cream?
18. Would you buy a face cream if it came out of this bottle?
19. Would you pay forty dollars for a jar of this?
20. If you couldn't pronounce a single ingredient, would you still put it on your skin?

## E. What goes on goes in
21. You watch what you eat. Do you watch what you put on your skin? Would you put this on it?
22. Your skin absorbs what you put on it. So would you put this on it?
23. Your skin is your largest organ. Would you feed it this?
24. If what you put on your skin ends up in your body, would you put this on?

## F. Over 50
25. If you're over 50 and you moisturize every morning, would you put this on your face?
26. Thirty years of moisturizer, every single morning. Would you have used this?
27. If your skin is already drying out, would you put this on it?

## G. Dare and pattern interrupt
28. This is motor oil. Would you wear it to bed?
29. Be honest. Would you put this anywhere near your face?
30. I'm about to put this on my face. Would you?

## Notion rows these lean on
- H2: "Never put Vaseline anywhere on your skin. It's called petroleum jelly because it is literally made from petroleum, the same stuff used to make gasoline and plastic." (already a TT hook in the database, Testing). The HOLD here makes the same argument; these hooks ask it as a question instead.
- H10: "3 ingredients you don't want anywhere near your skin" (Potential). Hook 29 borrows "anywhere near."
- H5: "What is the number one ingredient in most [category]? I'll give you a hint, it's not..." Hooks 16 and 17 are that move on the label.

## Customer language behind them
- chemicals 640 reviews, toxic 127, "no hormone disruptors" 4, "not cancer causing" in 73 cancer mentions, fragrance 354.
- "If I am conscious of what I eat, this should extend to what I put on my skin, since it is absorbed by the body." (hooks 4, 21, 24)
- "It's refreshing to be able to pronounce every ingredient on a label and know it's not cancer causing!!" (hook 20, and the HOLD's "you can pronounce every one of them")
- "I love not having a petroleum based product on my body if my dog comes over and licks my leg." (hook 14, verbatim situation)
- Grandkids, babies, kids use it: 367 reviews. "I love it for every day skin care, as well to use on my grandchildren's skin." (hooks 5, 12)
- Largest organ, what I put on my skin: 100 reviews.

## Flags
- Petroleum itself is a small customer topic: 14 reviews, Vaseline 30, and the survey has Vaseline at 0.4 percent of prior brands. The lane that is big is "chemicals" and "I want to know what's in it" (the survey's #1 reason to buy, cleaner alternative, 22.7 percent). The hooks work because motor oil is a picture of "chemicals," not because she uses Vaseline. The fragrance paragraph in the HOLD is the part closer to what she actually owns.
- Half the Vaseline reviews compare F-Balm's own texture to Vaseline ("hesitant to put something on my face the consistency of Vaseline"). A greasy-looking black oil in the first frame, then a rich balm, can invite that comparison. The HOLD's "soaks in without leaving them greasy" line is doing important work; keep it.
- Accuracy for the HOLD: petrolatum and mineral oil are refined from crude oil, the same source as motor oil. "This type of oil is what's inside these products" is loose. "It comes out of the same barrel" is exact.
- "Hormone disruptors, carcinogens" is the operator's claim and compliance call. Customers do say it (above).

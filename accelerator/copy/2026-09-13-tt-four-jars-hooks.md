# Hooks: four jars / $57 water script

20 hooks for the ingredient-value script. Angle is bucket #1 in the 2026-09-13 test results (ingredient quality / manufacturing trust, $104 CPA), so every hook stays on water, ingredients, price-for-what, or the shop. Customer language marked with source keys.

Hold line 1 is "That sounds impossible until you check the first ingredient. Water." Hooks marked (W) already reveal water, so start the hold at "70 to 80 percent of the formula."

---

## The math

1. You'd have to buy four jars of that $57 face cream to get the amount of actual skincare ingredients in one jar of F-Balm.

2. Your $57 face cream has less than $15 of actual skincare in it. The rest is water. (W)

3. Three out of every four dollars you spend on face cream goes to water. (W)

4. One jar of F-Balm has more actual skincare in it than four jars of the $57 stuff at Walgreens.

## The tap

5. You're paying $57 a jar for something that comes out of your kitchen tap. (W)

6. Most of what's in a $57 face cream is free. (W)

7. The most expensive thing in your bathroom is mostly tap water. (W)

## The first ingredient

8. Go check the first ingredient on your face cream. I'll wait.

9. The first ingredient on a $57 jar of face cream is the same as the first ingredient in a glass of water. (W)

10. If the first ingredient on your moisturizer is water, you've been paying for the jar. (W)

## The routine [R15709, C173]

11. Six products on your counter and you can't say what a single one of them did. Check the first ingredient.

12. I had a 6-step routine for years and couldn't tell you what I was paying for. Then I read one label.

13. Every jar on your bathroom shelf has the same first ingredient. (W)

## The skeptic [C306, R8398]

14. I watched these ads for a year before I bought one. What finally got me was the label on my old cream.

15. I was skeptical about rubbing beef fat on my face. Then I checked what I'd been rubbing on it for ten years.

## The compliments [R30]

16. People think I use expensive skincare. My jar is mostly beef fat. Theirs is mostly water. (W)

## The shop (bucket #1)

17. We make F-Balm in a shop in Ontario. The $57 cream is made in a factory that mostly bottles water. (W)

18. I can show you the room where every jar of F-Balm is made. Ask your $57 face cream where it was made.

## The reframe

19. Your skin isn't the problem. Your $57 cream is mostly water. (W)

20. The reason your moisturizer doesn't last isn't your skin. It's what the jar is made of.

## Replacements for "skincare ingredients"

Base line: "What has more ___ in it: 4 of these regular moisturizers, or 1 of these tins of F-Balm?"

The word has to be something water can't claim to be. Ranked, top three first.

1. actual skincare
2. real ingredients
3. stuff that isn't water
4. the good stuff
5. skin food
6. food for your skin
7. moisturizer (the cream is mostly water, so it's mostly not moisturizer)
8. oil
9. fat
10. nutrients
11. real nutrients [Ben's words in the 69 ad: "real ingredients with real nutrients"]
12. stuff that actually does something
13. things your skin can use
14. actives
15. edible ingredients [Caribbean ad: "five edible ingredients"]
16. nourishment
17. skincare (just the word: "which has more skincare in it")
18. product ("I put product on" is how women say it)
19. what you're actually paying for
20. stuff that costs money (water is free)

Top three, written out:

- What has more actual skincare in it: 4 of these, or 1 of these?
- What has more real ingredients in it: 4 of these regular moisturizers, or 1 tin of F-Balm?
- Which has more stuff that isn't water: 4 of these, or 1 of this?

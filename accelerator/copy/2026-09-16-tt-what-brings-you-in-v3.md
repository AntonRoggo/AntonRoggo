# "And what brings you in today?" v3: fine lines first, dossier-backed prescriptions

Supersedes [[2026-09-15-tt-what-brings-you-in-v2]]. Source skit: Everyday Dose consult (symptom, ingredient, symptom, ingredient, objection, reveal, proof, offer). Symptoms ranked by the post-purchase survey (2,081 buyers): fine lines 65%, dryness 39%, redness 14%, sensitive 18%. Prescriptions use STRONG-tier claims from the operator's two research dossiers (tallow and raw honey; cold-pressed olive oil), 2026-09-16, plus the sebum literature from the brief. Nothing EMERGING or SPECULATIVE is in the practitioner's mouth.

## Phase 1: Surgical analysis

### Preserve exactly
- "And what brings you in today?" / "Well, I'm constantly [symptom] every single day."
- "That sounds like a [category] issue! Get you some [ingredient] to [mechanism] and help bring that [symptom] down."
- "Hellooo. Hey. Yoohoo. Sorry." then symptom two, "even if I [do the right thing]."
- "Okay. Let's get you some [ingredient] then, okay? It'll help [benefit]."
- "Also. Also. Also." then symptom three, "Do you have anything for that?" / "Sure do. [Ingredient]."
- "Oh, and one more thing." symptom four. "Okay. Yeah. Let's definitely add some [ingredient] in there as well."
- "You're not just giving me a bunch of [objection], right? I hate [objection]." / "No [objection] here. Just try this."
- "This is [product]. It's not your regular [category]. It's [base] blended with [list]. Everything I just listed in a single [unit], ready in [time]."
- "Wait, this actually [sensory] really good. And this stuff actually works?" / "Sure does. I [use] it every single day and have seen huge improvements. Plus they have over [N] five-star reviews."
- "Okay, where do I get this?" / offer / "So I'll leave the link right here for you."

### Angle-specific modifications
- Bloated and tired → fine lines getting deeper. Chaga → beef tallow, with the sebum mechanism (about a third less oil every ten years after menopause; tallow is the same three fatty acids).
- Foggy focus → dry all the time even when I moisturize. Lion's mane → raw honey (pulls water in; 2026 systematic review of clinical trials) plus beeswax (seals it in; the humectant-plus-occlusive principle, 2025 clinical support).
- Dull skin, achy joints → cheeks get red. Collagen → cold-pressed olive oil (2025 human study: improved hydration, reduced redness) and why cold-pressed (refining destroys about 85% of the polyphenols).
- Stressed, moods → extremely sensitive, irritated easily. L-theanine → nothing added: no water, so no preservatives, leaves your skin's ecosystem alone (the dossier's most ownable structural claim; phrased positively, no "rebalance").
- Pills → greasy. 2,356 reviews. (Survey says 73% already use one or two products, so "too many products" is the wrong objection for this audience.)
- Coffee, 30 seconds → F-Balm, pea-sized amount. Tastes → smells (frankincense 441, honey 282).
- "Huge improvements" → "my brother asked if I'd had Botox," verbatim review.
- 100,000 reviews → 15,000, operator's current claim.

### Replacements required
- Sale, VIP kit, free express shipping → in-house in Canada, money-back guarantee, 3 to 5 day shipping. No bonus in the account's history; survey says price is the last reason anyone buys (5%).
- Every dossier claim rated EMERGING or SPECULATIVE (defensin-1, quorum sensing, vitamin K2 trial, PPAR signalling, microbiome "fixing," oleocanthal as ibuprofen, hydroxytyrosol and collagen, squalene replenishment). Kept out of the ad. They belong in email and long-form per the dossier's own staging.
- Anything that reads as a disease claim: no eczema, rosacea, acne, wound, or barrier-repair language. Redness is stated as a healthy-adult study result, not a condition.

## Cluster check (2026-09-16)
Does one woman have all four? Survey: all four together 3.9% of buyers; 41% named exactly one problem; lift of dry, sensitive, or redness among fine-lines buyers is 1.15 each (near independence). Biggest real pairs: lines + dry (29% of all buyers), lines + cleaner ingredients (43% of lines buyers). Reviews: 2 of 36,267 mention all four; among lines reviews, dry co-occurs 16% (lift 0.58), sensitive 2%, redness 2%.
Source ad stacks four unrelated complaints as a comedy conceit. Options: keep four as conceit, or cut to the three-beat patient the data supports: fine lines, dry skin, "I want something without chemicals" (43% of lines buyers, #1 stated reason to buy, maps to the no-water-no-preservatives beat). Sensitive and redness describe a different buyer (21% and 16% of lines buyers) and belong in a separate creative aimed at them.

## Notes
- Every prescription now carries a citable study or a structural fact, and every study cited is one the operator's dossier rates STRONG. The v2 problem (three prescriptions on one review each) is gone.
- "Cut redness" is the 2025 study's finding in healthy adults. It is not a rosacea claim. Don't add "rosacea" or "blotchy" to symptom three; the dossier says olive oil should not be marketed to eczema, rosacea, or fungal-acne-prone skin.
- "We leave your skin's ecosystem alone" is the dossier's own recommended phrasing. It does not say rebalance, restore, or microbiome-friendly.
- Length: each prescription is one sentence longer than the source's. The source's practitioner speaks in half-lines; this one cites a year. If it needs to come back, cut the citation clauses ("There's a 2026 review..." and "A 2025 study...") and keep the mechanism.
- "It has to be cold-pressed" only holds if the olive oil in F-Balm is actually cold-pressed extra-virgin. The client file says "organic olive oil." Confirm before this runs.
- The dossiers are the operator's research. The sebum, Draelos, Cureus, and USDA figures were verified in this workspace on 2026-09-15; the 2024 to 2026 honey and olive oil citations were not independently checked here.

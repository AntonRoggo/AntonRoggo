## Story logic pass (workflows/story-ad-logic.md)

Caught and fixed:
1. **Checklist 3, hook arithmetic.** H3 said the crew member told him "a few weeks later." The hold runs three months of gym, then the doctor, then three more weeks. Changed to "a few months later." H1 and H2 make no timeline claim.

Holds, checked:
- 46 now, 22 years of work, married long enough for a 13-year-old and "35 pounds heavier than the day we got married." Jacket bought at 32, "hadn't zipped in six years," gut "sitting on me for four years." All consistent.
- Timeline: the catch, she moves out, three months of gym (six pounds), the doctor, three weeks, the truck, six weeks on the drops, first game of the season. About five months end to end. Football season puts the catch in spring. Nothing contradicts it.
- Down 16 at week four, 22 at week five, from 35 over. About four pounds a week, the operator's stated ceiling. Luis's 28 in six weeks is the same rate.
- Checklist 4, the guide: Luis is living proof (40 pounds after his divorce, lean now), relaying his sister. That is the banked coworker structure. "Into natural health" is thin by the checklist's own standard, but it is the operator's canonical line and was left alone.
- Checklist 6: nobody delivers his shame. The rearview at a red light does it. His wife never insults him; she freezes.
- Checklist 9: first night a feeling (five hours of sleep), week one small and specific, week two the belt and a witness. Right order.
- Checklist 11: he acts like a real man of this cohort. Silence at the door, the couch, aguantar. The research says this is exactly how these men respond. The one addition that could break it, the Sancho lines, is played as the crew going quiet, not as him being mocked.
- Every head-turn is earned: the woman on the bleachers, the ex at the concession stand, the son. Three witnesses, three different reasons.

Watch: "Sharp. Lean. Awake. Present." is an assertion (checklist 8). At 22-plus pounds down from 35 over, "lean" is within reach, so it was left as the source wrote it.

## Suggestions, not applied

1. **The wife's job.** The research profiles the spouse as cleaning, housekeeping, food prep, or childcare, not nursing. Kept her a nurse because "I built the clinic where she did her clinicals" and "I paid for her boards" are the two strongest sacrifice lines in the script and both die without it. A Latina nurse is entirely plausible. If you want the research's profile instead: "She's a CNA. He's a CNA," "the nursing home where she did her CNA course," and "I paid for her scrubs, her CNA course, her certification."
2. **Luis's sister.** If you want the credential stronger for this ICP without breaking the banked block: "his sister, she's a nurse, and she's into natural health." One clause. Or the grandmother's remedies, which is real in this culture but I did not want to assume it.
3. **Spanish.** The script uses four Spanish words (mayordomo, lonchera, aguantar, Apá) and no Spanish sentences. That is deliberate: a 46-year-old Texas-born or long-naturalized tradesman telling this story on camera would tell it in English with those words in it. If the ad is for Spanish-dominant viewers, that is a translation job, not a swap.
4. **"Me friego para que ellos salgan adelante."** The research quotes this as the cohort's own line for the sacrifice. It could replace "You carry everyone. You don't complain" as a spoken line from his father. Left out because the operator said keep the structure, and the English version already carries it.

# Messaging extraction: Everyday Dose consult skit vs F-Balm v3

Two extractions in the fixed format, then a match table.

══════════════════════════════
MESSAGING EXTRACTION: ORIGINAL (Everyday Dose, UGC skit)
══════════════════════════════

STRATEGIC LAYER

● Angle: One cup of coffee replaces the supplement stack for the person who is tired, foggy, dull, achy, and stressed. "Everything I just listed for you in a single cup of coffee that's ready in less than 30 seconds."

● Market Awareness Stage: Problem-aware. She names symptoms and does not know the cause or the ingredient: "Well, I'm constantly bloated and tired every single day." / "That sounds like a gut issue!"

● Sophistication Stage: Stage 3, moving to 4. Differentiation is the delivery format, not the ingredients (chaga, lion's mane, L-theanine, collagen are category-standard). "You're not just giving me a bunch of pills right? Hate taking pills." / "No pill here. Just try this, this is coffee, not your regular coffee."

● Tone of Voice: Practitioner who is really a peer. The "doctor" prescribes but talks like a friend. Register: sitcom, fast, playful, interruptive ("Hellooo. Hey. Yoohoo. Sorry."). No clinical language, no citations, no numbers except the review count.

AVATAR LAYER

● Primary Avatar: An adult with a stack of everyday complaints and no diagnosis, who already drinks coffee.

● Sub-Avatar 1 (gut): "constantly bloated and tired every single day."
● Sub-Avatar 2 (focus): "My focus is in absolute mess too, my brain just like always feels foggy even if I have good sleep."
● Sub-Avatar 3 (skin and joints): "skin has been super dull, dry, joints are so achy."
● Sub-Avatar 4 (stress): "I am constantly stressed out, my moods have been all over the place."

● Age (explicit + implicit): Explicit: Not addressed. Implicit: "joints are so achy" and "skin has been super dull, dry" signal 35+; "collagen" as the fix signals aging concern.

● Gender (explicit + implicit): Explicit: Not addressed. Implicit: skin dullness and mood language skew female; no gendered address in the copy.

● Daily Occurrences: "bloated and tired every single day"; "always feels foggy even if I have good sleep"; "ready in less than 30 seconds" (the morning slot).

● Habits / Lifestyle Callouts: Sleeps well ("even if I have good sleep"); drinks coffee ("not your regular coffee"); avoids supplements ("Hate taking pills"); wants daily use ("drink it every single day").

PSYCHOLOGY LAYER

● Pain Points, Physical:
  1. "constantly bloated"
  2. "tired every single day"
  3. "brain just like always feels foggy"
  4. "skin has been super dull"
  5. "dry"
  6. "joints are so achy"

● Pain Points, Emotional:
  1. "constantly stressed out"
  2. "my moods have been all over the place"

● Pain Points, Social / Relational: Not addressed.

● Pain Points, Identity: Not addressed.

● False Beliefs: Implicit only. That good sleep should fix fog ("even if I have good sleep"); that the fix is pills ("You're not just giving me a bunch of pills right?").

● Felt Solutions Tried: Sleep ("good sleep"); pills, by refusal ("Hate taking pills"). Nothing else named.

● New Mechanism Introduced: One ingredient per symptom, then all in one cup. "chaga to balance out the bacteria" / "lion's mane... sharpen your focus and your mental clarity" / "hydrolyzed collagen, it'll help rebuild that skin barrier and strengthen your joints" / "L-theanine... balance out your cortisol levels." The real mechanism is the format: "Everything I just listed for you in a single cup of coffee."

PROMISE & OFFER LAYER

● Core Promise: Fix bloating, fog, skin, joints, and mood with one daily cup. "Everything I just listed for you in a single cup of coffee."

● Results Timeline: Not addressed for results. Only prep time: "ready in less than 30 seconds."

● Proof Elements: Taste ("Wait, this actually tastes really good"); first-person daily use ("I drink it every single day and have seen huge improvements"); review count ("over 100 thousand 5-star reviews"). No named customer, no study, no before/after.

● Offer Structure (price, discount, bundle, guarantee, urgency): "running a sale on their website right now so you can buy the coffee at a huge discount, plus VIP starter kit completely free with your order, free express shipping." No price, no guarantee, urgency by "right now."

● CTA Copy + Positioning: "So I'll leave the link right here for you." Lands last, after "Okay, where do I get this?" Framed as a favor from a friend, not a pitch.

STRATEGIC LAYER

● Angle: One five-ingredient tin answers the four things women over 50 buy skincare for, because each ingredient does one job. "Everything I just listed for you in a single tin, and you only need a pea-sized amount."

● Market Awareness Stage: Problem-aware. "Well, I've been noticing fine lines. Around my eyes, and around my mouth." She does not know the cause: "That sounds like your skin stopped making its own oil!"

● Sophistication Stage: Stage 4. Differentiation is mechanism plus sourced proof, not format. "After menopause it makes about a third less every ten years." / "There's a 2026 review of clinical trials behind that one." / "A 2025 study in healthy adults found it improved hydration and cut redness."

● Tone of Voice: Practitioner who is really a peer, same as the source. Register: sitcom interruptions preserved ("Hellooo. Hey. Yoohoo. Sorry.") but the prescriptions cite years and percentages, which shifts the register toward seminar in beats 2, 4, and the reveal.

AVATAR LAYER

● Primary Avatar: A woman past menopause buying skincare for fine lines. "After menopause it makes about a third less every ten years."

● Sub-Avatar 1 (lines): "I've been noticing fine lines. Around my eyes, and around my mouth."
● Sub-Avatar 2 (dry): "I have very dry skin too. Especially in the winter."
● Sub-Avatar 3 (sensitive): "I have extremely sensitive skin and it gets irritated easily. I react to most things."
● Sub-Avatar 4 (redness): "The redness. On my cheeks, around my nose."

● Age (explicit + implicit): Explicit: "After menopause." Implicit: "the winter" (Canadian buyer), "fine lines around my mouth," "my brother asked if I'd had Botox."

● Gender (explicit + implicit): Explicit: Not addressed. Implicit: menopause, Botox, "girl"-register interruptions.

● Daily Occurrences: "very dry skin... especially in the winter"; "it gets irritated easily"; "put it on every single day."

● Habits / Lifestyle Callouts: Reacts to products ("I react to most things"); avoids greasy ("I hate greasy"); daily use; Canadian winter.

● Pain Points, Physical:
  1. "fine lines. Around my eyes, and around my mouth"
  2. "very dry skin"
  3. "extremely sensitive skin and it gets irritated easily"
  4. "The redness. On my cheeks, around my nose."

● Pain Points, Emotional: Not addressed directly. Implied by "Yoohoo. Sorry." and the Botox line.

● Pain Points, Social / Relational: "my brother asked if I'd had Botox" (positive social payoff, not a pain).

● Pain Points, Identity: Not addressed.

● False Beliefs: Implicit: that a moisturizer moisturizes ("It's not your regular moisturizer"); that balms are greasy ("You're not giving me some greasy balm, right?").

● Felt Solutions Tried: Implied only: "your regular moisturizer"; "I react to most things." No named prior product.

● New Mechanism Introduced: Sebum loss after menopause, replaced by the same fatty acids. "your skin stopped making its own oil... about a third less every ten years... beef tallow, it's the same three fatty acids as your skin's oil." Then one ingredient per symptom (honey pulls water, beeswax seals, no water means no preservatives, cold-pressed olive oil for redness). The format claim mirrors the source: "Everything I just listed for you in a single tin."

● Results Timeline: Not addressed. Only "you only need a pea-sized amount" and "put it on every single day."

● Proof Elements: Smell ("this actually smells really good"); first-person daily use with a named reaction ("my brother asked if I'd had Botox"); review count ("over 15,000 five-star reviews"); three cited studies in the prescriptions.

● Offer Structure (price, discount, bundle, guarantee, urgency): "made in-house in Canada, there's a money-back guarantee on every order, and it ships in 3 to 5 days." No price, no discount, no bonus, no urgency.

● CTA Copy + Positioning: "So I'll leave the link right here for you." Same line, same position, same friend-favor framing.

## What the comparison says
The structure, awareness stage, speaker, mechanism engine, proof beat, and CTA all match. Two things don't, and they're the same thing: the source is a Stage 3 ad that wins on lightness (a coffee instead of pills, said fast and funny), and ours became a Stage 4 ad that wins on evidence (a study per beat). The citations are the difference in register. They're the strongest version of each claim and they cost the skit its pace.

Two fixes, either direction:
- Cut the citation clauses ("There's a 2026 review of clinical trials behind that one," "A 2025 study in healthy adults found," "Refining strips out about 85 percent") and keep only the mechanism. The skit returns to the source's register and length. Keep the studies for the landing page and email.
- Or keep them and accept a heavier ad. The buyer is 55+, skeptical, and names "trust" as the top blocker, so evidence in the practitioner's mouth may land with this audience where it wouldn't with the coffee buyer.

One cheap add the source doesn't have but our data does: a named prior product. "Well, I've been noticing fine lines... and my Olay isn't doing anything about them." Olay is the #1 named prior brand in the survey and 17 reviews.

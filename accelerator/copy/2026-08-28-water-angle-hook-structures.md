# Water angle: hook structures

Three directions from the "water is free, beef tallow is not" seed, each built on a structural skeleton showing where the six elements land. Related: [[2026-08-28-water-value-math-hooks]] [[2026-08-28-57-face-cream-is-mostly-water]] [[2026-08-28-water-is-free-beef-tallow-is-not]]

## Structure 1: The Switcher

She keeps changing products and nothing works. The open loop is WHY nothing worked. The mechanism (water) closes the loop after the frustration is already real.

**Element map:**
- Relevance in first line: name the switching behavior she's already doing
- Emotionally charged: the frustration of trying everything and still failing
- Open loop: something is wrong with ALL of them, and she never caught it
- Stakes: money gone, time wasted, skin no better
- Specificity: number of products, dollar amounts, time of day it fails
- Consequence first: her dry skin, the tight feeling, the repeated disappointment

### 1A
You've switched moisturizers four times this year and your skin is still dry every morning.
You've tried the $30 one. The $60 one. The one your friend swore by.
Same tight feeling by noon. Same flaking by 3pm.
Every single one of them had the same first ingredient, and you never thought to check.

### 1B
Three moisturizers sitting in my bathroom right now. Three different brands, two different price points.
My skin is just as dry as it was before I bought any of them.
I wasn't picking the wrong brand. I was picking the wrong ingredient.
All three start with water. I was paying for water.

### 1C
I spent two years switching moisturizers like I was on a dating app for face cream.
Nothing stuck. My skin was dry in the morning, tight by lunch, flaking by dinner.
Then someone told me to stop reading the front of the jar and start reading the back.
First ingredient on every single one: water.

## Structure 2: The Deeper Fine Lines

She's doing MORE and her skin is getting WORSE. The contradiction is the emotional charge. The open loop is that the moisturizer isn't failing, it was never the right tool.

**Element map:**
- Relevance in first line: the fine lines she can see getting worse right now
- Emotionally charged: betrayal of spending more and getting less
- Open loop: the products aren't failing. They were never built for this.
- Stakes: the lines are getting deeper every month she keeps using the wrong thing
- Specificity: the year, the number of products added, what she sees in the mirror
- Consequence first: what her face looks like, not why

### 2A
Your fine lines are deeper this year than they were last year.
You're using more products than last year. Spending more too.
The lines don't care.
The products aren't failing you. They were never built for what your skin actually needs right now.

### 2B
Look at the lines around your eyes compared to a year ago.
Deeper. You're not imagining it.
You've added a serum, an eye cream, and a night routine since then.
Three new products and the lines got worse. Because more of the wrong thing is still the wrong thing.

### 2C
Every woman I talk to says the same thing. My fine lines are worse than last year. I'm doing MORE than last year.
More products. More money. More steps in the morning.
And every one of those products has the same first ingredient, and it's the one ingredient that can't fix this problem.

## Structure 3: The Quit and Won

She stopped using moisturizer. Her skin got BETTER. The open loop is how that's even possible. The emotional charge is the reversal of everything she believed.

**Element map:**
- Relevance in first line: someone notices her skin is better
- Emotionally charged: the awkwardness of the truth, the relief, the disbelief
- Open loop: she stopped the thing everyone says is essential, and won
- Stakes: everything she believed about skincare might be wrong
- Specificity: how many people noticed, how long it took, what she replaced it with
- Consequence first: the compliment, the visible change

### 3A
My friend asked me why my skin looks better since I STOPPED using moisturizer.
I didn't stop caring about my skin. I stopped rubbing water on it.
Same face. Same routine. Different jar.
Five ingredients, and not one of them is water.

### 3B
I quit my $50 moisturizer two months ago and three people have told me my skin looks better.
Better without the cream I'd been loyal to for years.
I didn't quit skincare. I quit paying for water.
I switched to something where the first ingredient is beef tallow, not tap water.

### 3C
My sister told me I look like I'm sleeping better. I'm not.
I stopped using my moisturizer six weeks ago and replaced it with beef tallow.
My skin cleared up, my fine lines softened, and she noticed before I did.
The moisturizer wasn't helping. Eighty percent of it was evaporating off my face every morning.

## How to use these structures

Each structure is a different entry point into the same truth (your cream is mostly water):
- **The Switcher** enters through frustration. Best for cold audiences who've tried everything.
- **The Deeper Fine Lines** enters through fear. Best for women 40+ watching their skin change.
- **The Quit and Won** enters through social proof and surprise. Best for warm audiences ready to try something different.

All three delay the mechanism. The water reveal lands after the reader is already in the story.

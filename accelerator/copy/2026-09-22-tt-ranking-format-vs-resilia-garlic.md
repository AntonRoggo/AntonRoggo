# Ranking format: our lip-lines script vs the Resilia "Dad, rank these" ad (2026-09-22)

| Beat | Resilia (garlic, ~330 words) | Ours (lip lines, ~560 words) |
|---|---|---|
| Speaker | Authority (cardiovascular researcher) interrogated by his kid | Peer, 54, who tried it all |
| Prompt | Each item is a question from the kid ("Beetroot?") | Narrator lists them herself |
| Score range | 0 to 10, with a 0 (vegan) as the pattern interrupt and "9, maybe a 10" as the honest hedge | 1 to 9, hero at 9 |
| Why each score | A mechanism per item: beetroot "only gives nitric oxide, relaxes arteries for an hour or two, no lasting result" | Experience per item: "didn't move," "right there waiting for you," "never lasted" |
| Hero's mechanism | Named compound, forms only under one process (20 months, darkness), 40 human studies, two actions | Water-and-oil mechanism, delivered after the results ladder |
| Personal results | None. No timeline, no witnesses | Week two, husband, month, compliments at work |
| Brand entry | "Where can people get this?" then one small company that does it the right way | "This tallow balm, nine out of 10," then knockoff warning |
| Authenticity test | Visible: real extract is dark amber, fakes are yellow | Stated: knockoffs are bleached and deodorized. Nothing the viewer can check |
| Combination | Adds CoQ10 (the 8) to the garlic (the 9) in one gel | Five ingredients, not framed as combining the ranked items |
| Guarantee | 30 days, "the risk is on them" | 30 days, "you're not gonna wanna return this" |
| Scarcity | Limited sale on last batches, and a manufacturing reason: "fermentation cannot be rushed, the next batch takes months" | None |
| Close | Fear: "before it's too late" | Reassurance |

## What to borrow
1. A reason behind every score, in the mechanism's own logic. The beetroot line is our water line: works for an hour, nothing lasting. Lip creams 3: "it's water, it sits on top and it's gone in an hour." Patches 1: "they're water on plastic." Retinol 5: "it thins the skin instead of feeding it, and it doesn't put the oil back." Botox 7: "it freezes the muscle, it doesn't touch the skin, and it wears off." Tallow 9: "it's the only one that replaces the oil." Now the scores teach the mechanism before the mechanism paragraph arrives.
2. The two-hander. The kid asks, the authority answers. Ours already has a daughter; flip it: the daughter asks, the mother ranks. Or the mother asks and the retired nurse ranks. The Everyday Dose consult hold is the same shape.
3. A 0. Something on the list should get a 0 with a reason. Candidates from the reviews and Reddit: the $600 prescription cream (a review), lip plumpers, "drinking less through a straw."
4. "9, maybe a 10." The hedge is the honest beat and it reads as more confident, not less.
5. A visible authenticity test. Theirs is a color. Ours could be: the tin says grass-fed and made in Ontario on the label; the balm is off-white, not pure white; it smells like something. None of that is in the client file. Ask Ben what a customer can see or smell that separates real rendered tallow from a bleached one.
6. Scarcity with a reason. "Made in-house in small batches" is a real review wish ("hoping company continues to produce in small batches") but not an established claim. If it's true, the line is: "he makes it himself, so when a batch is gone it's gone until the next one."
7. Combine the ranked items. Their gel includes the 8 and the 9. Ours: the balm is tallow (the 9) plus honey and beeswax, which do what the 3 and the 5 were supposed to.
8. Length. Theirs does more in 330 words than ours in 560; the personal ladder is what we have that they don't. Keep the ladder, cut the item descriptions to one line each.

## What not to borrow
- "40 human studies." The Cureus 2024 review found zero RCTs on leave-on tallow. Our number is reviews, not studies.
- The fear close. Skincare's version is "it only gets drier from here," already in the Sarah script; "before it's too late" is the blood-pressure register.

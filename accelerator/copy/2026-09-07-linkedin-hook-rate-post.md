# LinkedIn post: hook rate

Would you want an ad with a hook rate of 99%?

The truth is probably not.

After generating 200M+ views organically, this didn't make sense to me either.

But in paid advertising, a hook's job isn't to maximize views.

It's to qualify the right viewer.

Even unaware ads have a hint of relevance baked into the hook.

A low hook rate doesn't automatically mean you have a bad ad.

One of the easiest ways to kill a profitable ad is to judge it by the wrong metric.

If an ad has a 20% hook rate but strong ROAS, the hook is doing its job.

It's filtering out everyone except the people most likely to buy.

The opposite happens too.

I've seen creatives with 53 to 99% three-second view rates that still failed commercially.

The opening was entertaining enough to make almost anyone stop scrolling.

But the ad never signaled the problem, the promise, or the buyer it was for.

So the platform found more curious viewers. Not more customers.

Higher engagement.
Higher CPMs.
Weak conversion.

The question isn't "How many people watched the first three seconds?"

It's "Did the hook attract the right person, and did the rest of the ad fulfill the promise?"

Look at the relationship between:

- Hook rate
- Cost per unique outbound click
- Click-to-purchase rate
- Landing page congruence

A hook that gets fewer views but more qualified clicks is often stronger than a viral hook that attracts everyone.

P.S. This is why you need to build out a full funnel. The hook qualifies. The body converts. The landing page closes. If any piece is missing, the metrics lie to you.

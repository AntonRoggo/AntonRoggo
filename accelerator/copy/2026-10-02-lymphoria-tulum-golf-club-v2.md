V1 (the catch)
"I drove to my husband's golf club to bring him the reading glasses he forgot. I found him behind the cart barn with the cart girl. His hand on her thigh. Her giggling. She's twenty-two. He's sixty-six. What a stranger at a pool in Tulum put in my hand the next day is how I got the last word."

V2 (withholds)
"Six weeks after I found out what my husband really does at his golf club, I walked into the club's spring dinner. Every head turned. It started with a stranger at a pool in Tulum."

V3 (the doorway, then who)
"My husband sold his company, took up golf, and told me from the bedroom doorway I was 'getting a little soft.' Then I found out who he'd been comparing me to. She drives the beverage cart. What a stranger at a pool in Tulum put in my hand is how I got the last word."

PRODUCTION SCRIPT

Block 1: The setup

Visual: Narrator to lens. B-roll: a signing table, champagne; a new watch on Mark's wrist; the club gates; four men teeing off; Dave at a Christmas party with a much younger woman; Mark buttoning a fitted golf shirt; his phone face down on the counter.

Audio: "Mark sold his software company two years ago. Overnight he was a big shot. New watch. The club. Golf five days a week with his boys. All on their second wives. Then the second wives turned into girls. Dave brought the girl from the pro shop to the Christmas party. And Mark started noticing. New shirts. Whiter teeth. His phone face down on the counter. One night he stopped in our bedroom doorway and said, 'Becca, you've gotten a little soft. Maybe look into something. For yourself.'"

Block 2: The catch

Visual: Reading glasses on the counter; the club parking lot; a golf cart behind a barn, two figures, her laughing, his hand. Wide, no faces.

Audio: "Two weeks later I drove to the club to bring him the reading glasses he forgot. I found him behind the cart barn with the cart girl. His hand on her thigh. Her giggling. She's twenty-two. He's sixty-six. He didn't see me."

Block 3: The mirror

Visual: Clubhouse ladies' room. She stands at the mirror in her coat, hands flat on the counter.

Audio: "I didn't scream. I walked into the clubhouse bathroom and looked in the mirror for the first time in two years. Sixty-three. Twenty-two pounds since I turned sixty. Swollen stomach. Puffy eyes. My jawline gone into my neck. I stood there and thought, I know what a twenty-two-year-old has that I don't. I almost believed him."

Block 4: The effort, and the clock

Visual: Walking shoes at dawn, a salad, the food app, the scale. The doctor's office. Her in the car. Then the fridge at home: the club's spring dinner invitation under a magnet.

Audio: "Because I had tried. Four miles every morning for a year. No bread. No wine. Every bite logged. Nothing moved. My doctor gave me thirty seconds. He said, 'At sixty-three, hormones change. Eat less, walk more.' I cried in the car. That night the invitation was still on the fridge. The club's spring dinner. Six weeks out. Mark had already told me I didn't have to come."

Block 5: His card, Tulum

Visual: His Amex on the kitchen counter. A boarding pass on her phone. The plane window. A Tulum resort pool, white loungers, every one taken but one.

Audio: "The next morning I took his Amex and booked a flight to Tulum. I didn't leave a note. I just needed to be somewhere he wasn't. First afternoon, every chair at the pool was taken except one. Next to a woman reading in a black one-piece. Mid-sixties. Waist. Arms. Jawline. I said, 'Can I sit here?' She moved her bag."

Block 6: The ask

Visual: The two loungers. Becca in a cover-up, holding it closed over her middle. Joyce turns a page, doesn't look up. Then lowers her sunglasses.

Audio: "I lasted ten minutes. I said, 'I'm sorry. You look so good for your age. What are you doing?' She lowered her sunglasses and said, 'Honey. I was where you are.' I said, 'Where am I?' She said, 'Alone in Tulum on somebody else's card.'"

Block 7: Her story

Visual: Joyce turns on her side to face Becca. Plain, not preachy.

Audio: "She said, 'Four years ago I was forty pounds heavier. Walking every morning. Eating like a bird. My husband had stopped reaching for me at night. I came down here for the lymphatic drainage massages. And the woman who did them told me the thing nobody at home ever had. I can move it on this table once a month. The drain has to open every day.'"

Block 8: The women who never age, and the mechanism

Visual: Joyce nods at the pool: women in their fifties and sixties, easy in their swimsuits. Back to Becca, arms crossed over her middle. Then simple animation: the drain slowing, then shut. Waste sitting in the lower belly, the face, under the jaw, thickening. No red glow, no fluid graphics. Back to Joyce on the lounger.

Audio: "She said, 'Look around this pool. You know those women who never seem to age? The ones who look fifty at sixty-five? They're not luckier than you. They're not more disciplined than you. They get drained. Every single day. It's the secret nobody tells you.' I said, 'I gained twenty-two pounds. I don't need drainage. I need to lose weight.' She said, 'You don't have menopause weight. You have menopause sludge. When estrogen drops, your lymphatic system stops working. Every glass of wine. Every hormone. Every toxin from the last thirty years that your body was supposed to drain just sits there. Locked in. Hardened. Stuck. In your belly. In your face. Under your jaw. That's not fat, honey. That's waste. You can walk four miles for the rest of your life. You'll never burn it off. Because you can't burn waste. You have to drain it.'"

Block 9: The bottle

Visual: Joyce reaches into her straw bag and sets a small dark dropper bottle on the table between the loungers, label facing away. Honey-colored liquid in the pipette. Four herb tags, plain.

Audio: "She reached into her bag and set a small dark bottle on the table between us. The liquid looked like honey. She said, 'Four herbs. Stillingia breaks down the years of hardened waste. Prickly ash restarts the circulation menopause shut down. Cleavers sweeps the loosened waste out. Red clover calms the swelling so it doesn't build back up. Half a dropper morning and night.' I said, 'There's no way drops fix three years of this.' She said, 'Six weeks. By week two you'll be in the bathroom constantly. By week three your waist will be smaller. By week six you won't recognize yourself. Watch. Take it. I always bring two.' Her name was Joyce."

Block 10: Six weeks

Visual: Montage with timestamps: the bathroom door; the ring spinning on the sink; the phone call; the jawline in the mirror; Mark's empty side of the bed at 7 a.m.; the scale; the dress on the bedroom door. Mark passing it with his golf bag, not looking.

Audio: "I flew home Sunday. Mark didn't ask where I'd been. He didn't check the card. Week one. Bathroom every hour. Day four, my face less puffy in the morning. My ring spinning loose on my finger. I told no one. Week two. The bloat I'd carried for two years, gone. My daughter called and said, 'Mom, you sound different.' Week three. My waist back. My jawline back. Sleeping through the night for the first time since sixty. Mark didn't notice. He was at the club by seven. Week four. Down fourteen. Eating normally. Week five. Down nineteen. The dress from his retirement party. The one I never wore because it was a size too small. It fit. I put it on and cried. Week six. The spring dinner."

Block 11: The spring dinner

Visual: The club dining room. She walks in, in the dress. Heads turn. The foursome's table by the window, Mark's back to the door. Dave sees her first. Mark turns. Kaylee at the bar, glasses in hand, looking.

Audio: "He'd told me I didn't have to come. I went. I walked in. Every head turned. Dave saw me first. His table went quiet. Mark turned around. Mouth open. Like he'd seen a ghost. The cart girl was working the bar."

Block 12: The stranger at the sink

Visual: The same clubhouse ladies' room from Block 3, now in the dress. A woman her age at the next sink, sixties, put-together but tired, watching her in the mirror. She takes out her phone at the sink.

Audio: "I went to the ladies' room. The same mirror. A woman my age was at the sink next to mine. Beautiful but tired. She looked at me in the mirror. 'I'm sorry. I have to ask. You look incredible. What are you doing?' Her husband plays with Mark on Saturdays. I told her about Joyce. About the drops. She pulled out her phone right at the sink. And ordered."

Block 13: Mark, alone

Visual: The hallway outside the ladies' room. Mark waiting, alone. She walks past him to the far side of the room.

Audio: "I walked out. Mark was standing there. Alone. He said, 'Becca? You look incredible. What did you do?' I didn't smile. I didn't answer. I walked past him to the far side of the room."

Block 14: The circle

Visual: Her kitchen, two weeks later. Her daughter (thirties) picks up the bottle from the counter. Then a phone screen: the daughter's selfie and the text.

Audio: "Two weeks later my daughter came over. Saw the bottle on my counter. 'Mom, what is this? Why do you look like that?' She tried it. Two weeks later she texted me a selfie. 'I look like you.'"

Block 15: The truth, and the close

Visual: Back to the kitchen island. The same bottle, now turned so the label reads. She holds it, doesn't present it.

Audio: "Here's the thing. I thought he wanted her because I got old. He didn't. He wasn't right. He was a coward. He couldn't handle a woman his own age. Menopause just locked everything in. The second I drained it, I got me back. Not thirty-year-old me. The me I was supposed to be at sixty-three. The woman he should have been terrified to lose. Look. I don't know where you're at right now. Maybe menopause hit and never let up. Maybe you've tried the walking. The logging. No bread. No wine. Maybe your doctor gave you thirty seconds. Don't let them sell you Ozempic when your body just needs to drain what menopause locked in. I didn't read the label until the flight home. Lymphoria. Four herbs. Stillingia. Prickly ash. Cleavers. Red clover. Half a dropper morning and night. Joyce said they sell out constantly. Small batches. If you still see the orange button below, you got lucky. 30-day money-back guarantee. But after six weeks, you won't need it. That's all it took for me."

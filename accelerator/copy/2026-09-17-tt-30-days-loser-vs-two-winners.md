# Why the F-Balm "30 days" script lost and the bitters and Rise scripts won

Three extractions in the fixed format, then a section-by-section comparison, then the loser's language checked against the reviews.

══════════════════════════════
MESSAGING EXTRACTION: WINNER 1 (Soursop bitters, "what drops off your body")
══════════════════════════════

STRATEGIC LAYER

● Angle: Parasite-driven weight gain. "It was organisms living inside your gut, eating your food, stealing your nutrients."

● Market Awareness Stage: Problem-aware. She knows the belly, hunger and puffiness; she does not know the cause. "You thought it was your metabolism. You thought it was your age. You thought you just needed more willpower. It was none of those things."

● Sophistication Stage: Stage 4 to 5. Differentiation is a new enemy (organisms), a new proof event ("Stringy white things. Dark clumps."), and an inverted objection ("Tastes absolutely disgusting. That is the point. Sweet supplements feed what is living inside you.").

● Tone of Voice: Omniscient narrator, second person, present tense. Register: clinical-gross, calm, certain. "Do not be alarmed. That is the weight leaving."

AVATAR LAYER

● Primary Avatar: A woman with a belly pooch who eats normally and cannot lose it.

● Sub-Avatar 1: The constant-hunger overeater: "The constant hunger that made you feel like you were always overeating."
● Sub-Avatar 2: The puffy, swollen one: "the puffiness is gone," "the water weight disappears."
● Sub-Avatar 3: The one who blames age and metabolism: "You thought it was your metabolism. You thought it was your age."

● Age (explicit + implicit): Explicit: "at every life stage." Implicit: husband, work, 3 p.m. crash, "your age" as a blamed cause signals 40+.

● Gender (explicit + implicit): Explicit: "Your husband notices." Implicit: jeans, puffy face, "no matter what you wore."

● Daily Occurrences: "You reach for a snack." "You button your jeans." "Your energy does not crash at 3 p.m." "People at work start asking." "You wake up and your clothes feel different putting them on." "You visit the bathroom."

● Habits / Lifestyle Callouts: "You have not changed your diet. Not one thing." "No diet change. No extra exercise." "Sweet supplements" as the prior habit.

PSYCHOLOGY LAYER

● Pain Points, Physical:
  1. "belly pooch outward every single day"
  2. "constant hunger"
  3. "bloating"
  4. "puffiness"
  5. "clothes not fitting"
  6. "energy... crash at 3 p.m."
  7. "your body swollen and storing fat"

● Pain Points, Emotional:
  1. "You thought you just needed more willpower" (self-blame)
  2. "made you feel like you were always overeating" (guilt)

● Pain Points, Social / Relational:
  1. "Your husband notices your stomach does not stick out anymore" (the inverse: he noticed when it did)
  2. "People at work start asking what you are doing differently"

● Pain Points, Identity:
  1. "That belly that stuck out no matter what you wore"
  2. "You thought it was your age"

● False Beliefs: "You thought it was your metabolism. You thought it was your age. You thought you just needed more willpower."

● Felt Solutions Tried: Diet and exercise by negation ("No diet change. No extra exercise."); "Sweet supplements feed what is living inside you."

● New Mechanism Introduced: "organisms living inside your gut, eating your food, stealing your nutrients, producing inflammatory waste that kept your body swollen and storing fat as a survival response." Each herb gets a kill job: "Soursop cuts off energy to the organisms and kills them. Neem disrupts their reproductive cycle... Senna and Tamarind flush the dead waste."

PROMISE & OFFER LAYER

● Core Promise: "Your stomach is flat. You have lost an average of eight to twelve pounds just from two tablespoons a day."

● Results Timeline: "Day one... Day three... Day five... Day seven... Day ten... Day twelve... Day fourteen." "Two tablespoons. Fourteen days. That is all it took."

● Proof Elements: "Over three hundred thousand people have experienced this exact transformation." The bathroom event as visible proof. Ingredient-by-job list. No testimonial, no study.

● Offer Structure: "Free shipping. 60 day money back guarantee. Small company. Limited bottles. Direct from Africa."

● CTA Copy + Positioning: "Link below. Grab yours before this batch is gone." Last line, after scarcity.

══════════════════════════════
MESSAGING EXTRACTION: WINNER 2 (Rise mushroom coffee, "a dude who smokes weed")
══════════════════════════════

STRATEGIC LAYER

● Angle: Morning fog for the nightly weed smoker, fixed without quitting. "You didn't quit weed. You just added the one thing your mornings were missin'."

● Market Awareness Stage: Problem-aware. He knows the groggy mornings; he has not connected them to a fixable cause. "That heavy, groggy mornin' feelin' you wake up with isn't hittin' as hard."

● Sophistication Stage: Stage 4. Category is known (mushroom coffee); differentiation is dose transparency: "Most mushroom coffees don't use full-spectrum blends or disclose what's actually in the dose."

● Tone of Voice: Peer narrator talking about "a dude" who is you. Register: raw, profane, anti-hype. "You kinda feel scammed as fuck." "You're not superhuman."

AVATAR LAYER

● Primary Avatar: "A dude who smokes weed every single fuckin' night."

● Sub-Avatar 1: The one who drags through the first three hours: "You're not draggin' through the first three hours of your day."
● Sub-Avatar 2: The one who loses the sentence: "Till that mid-sentence fuckin' scatters."
● Sub-Avatar 3: The one who already tried mushroom coffee and tasted mushrooms: "No weird mushroom taste at all."

● Age (explicit + implicit): Explicit: Not addressed. Implicit: "locked in at work," "drive back," a working adult.

● Gender (explicit + implicit): Explicit: "A dude." Implicit: the register.

● Daily Occurrences: "That heavy, groggy mornin' feelin' you wake up with." "The first three hours of your day." "The fog that usually sits on your brain till noon." "Locked in at work. Drive back, head clear."

● Habits / Lifestyle Callouts: "smokes weed every single fuckin' night." Drinks a morning coffee (the swap). "You didn't quit weed."

PSYCHOLOGY LAYER

● Pain Points, Physical:
  1. "heavy, groggy mornin' feelin'"
  2. "draggin' through the first three hours"
  3. "the fog that usually sits on your brain till noon"
  4. "mid-sentence... scatters"
  5. "runnin' on empty"

● Pain Points, Emotional:
  1. "you kinda feel scammed as fuck" (the pre-empted disappointment)

● Pain Points, Social / Relational: Not addressed.

● Pain Points, Identity:
  1. "That's the version of me that wasn't runnin' on empty."

● False Beliefs: Implicit: that the fix is quitting weed. "You didn't quit weed. You just added the one thing."

● Felt Solutions Tried: Other mushroom coffees, by contrast ("Most mushroom coffees don't use full-spectrum blends"; "No weird mushroom taste").

● New Mechanism Introduced: Adaptogens, each with a job tied to a felt outcome: "Lion's Mane helps support your focus and mental clarity." "Cordyceps help support clean, steady energy with no jitters and no crash." "Turkey Tail supports your gut, which is wired straight to your brain."

PROMISE & OFFER LAYER

● Core Promise: "That's the version of me that wasn't runnin' on empty." Clear mornings without quitting.

● Results Timeline: "one cup... every mornin' for a few months." First sip: "nothing's different." Then "after a few more weeks the adaptogens really start doin' their thing." "Eventually you catch yourself locked in at work."

● Proof Elements: "Over 200,000 people drink Rise daily." "Third-party tested and certified organic." "Grown in California." "All six adaptogenic mushrooms at meaningful doses."

● Offer Structure: "50% off." "30-day money-back guarantee. If you don't love it, send the empty bag back for a full refund." Stock scarcity: "If it's still in stock when you see this."

● CTA Copy + Positioning: "Grab it before they sell out." Last line.

══════════════════════════════
MESSAGING EXTRACTION: LOSER (F-Balm, "what happens to your skin in 30 days")
══════════════════════════════

STRATEGIC LAYER

● Angle: Nutrient replacement. "Your skin is finally going to absorb all the missing bioavailable nutrients it's been BEGGING for, for years."

● Market Awareness Stage: Solution-aware to product-aware. It names the product in the hook and lists ingredients by chemistry; it never establishes what the viewer thinks is wrong. "This is what happens to your skin when you use F-Balm every day for 30 days."

● Sophistication Stage: Stage 3 presented as Stage 4. It claims a mechanism ("bio-identical oils... rebuild the skin barrier") but no enemy, no new cause, no inverted objection. Differentiation is "100% natural compounds doing what the lab made skin care never could."

● Tone of Voice: The brand, in second person. Register: educational, ingredient-forward, mildly hype ("BEGGING"). The close is first-person plural: "we're so confident in the quality and the sourcing of our product."

AVATAR LAYER

● Primary Avatar: A woman whose skin is dry, red and lined, who wears makeup.

● Sub-Avatar 1: Crepey skin (hook 2): "what happens to crepey skin."
● Sub-Avatar 2: The husband buying for his wife (hook 3): "your wife's skin."
● Sub-Avatar 3: The line-resigned: "the lines you thought you'd just have to live with."

● Age (explicit + implicit): Explicit: Not addressed. Implicit: "for years," "lines," "crepey," "thousands of years."

● Gender (explicit + implicit): Explicit: "your wife's skin" (hook 3 addresses a man). Implicit: makeup.

● Daily Occurrences: "that tight feeling after you wash your face." "your makeup goes on properly instead of catching on every dry patch." "when you touch your face."

● Habits / Lifestyle Callouts: Wears makeup. "Covering up." Uses "lab made skin care."

PSYCHOLOGY LAYER

● Pain Points, Physical:
  1. "that tight feeling after you wash your face"
  2. "The redness"
  3. "catching on every dry patch"
  4. "the lines you thought you'd just have to live with"
  5. "fine lines, roughness and elasticity"
  6. "greasy" (by negation)

● Pain Points, Emotional:
  1. "you're not fighting your skin anymore"
  2. "lines you thought you'd just have to live with" (resignation)

● Pain Points, Social / Relational:
  1. "people start to notice, you start getting compliments"

● Pain Points, Identity:
  1. "stop covering up and start glowing"

● False Beliefs: Not addressed. The only candidate is "lines you thought you'd just have to live with," which is resignation, not a wrong cause.

● Felt Solutions Tried: "the lab made skin care" (one mention, week two). Nothing else named.

● New Mechanism Introduced: "missing bioavailable nutrients." "a natural version of retinol from beef tallow. Non synthetic vitamin E... Squalene oil and boswellic acids." "bio-identical oils in the tallow and olive oil start to rebuild the skin barrier." "boswellic acid in the frankincense visibly improving fine lines." No cause named for why the nutrients went missing.

PROMISE & OFFER LAYER

● Core Promise: "Your skin looks softer, feels stronger, and finally holds its own moisture through the day."

● Results Timeline: "From the very first night... In the first few days... By the end of week one... After 2 weeks... By week three... After one whole month."

● Proof Elements: Not addressed. No review count, no witness, no before and after, no origin, no maker.

● Offer Structure: "try the F-balm for 30 days and if for any reason you don't like it let us know and we'll give you a full refund." No price, no discount, no urgency, no scarcity.

● CTA Copy + Positioning: No explicit CTA. The guarantee sentence is the last line and contains no link, no verb.

On the persuasion equation: PROMISE × (PROOF × CURIOSITY) / CONSTRAINTS. The loser's proof term is zero, so the product is zero regardless of the promise. Curiosity is one word ("Watch this"). Both winners open a loop in the hook ("what drops off," "gonna blow your mind") and pay it with a visible event.

Speaker: both winners have a narrator with a stance (the omniscient guide, the blunt peer). The loser is the brand, and it says so at the end ("we're so confident"). A "what happens to you" format read by the company is an ad; read by a narrator who has watched it happen, it's a report.

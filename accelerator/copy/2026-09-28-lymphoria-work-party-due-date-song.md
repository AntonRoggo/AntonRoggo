# Lymphoria: "When are you due?" work-party song, adapted from the Resilia parasite script

Filed 2026-09-28. Tenth completion with the library. Source: the Resilia (oregano oil and black seed oil) song script: Carol asks Lisa when she is due at a work party, the bathroom overhear, the doctor ("labs are fine, over 40, eat less move more"), Rachel in the parking lot after ten years ("you want the real answer / duh / it was never my fault"), the parasite mechanism, two compounds, "Resilia, two softgels every morning," the second party ("that is Mark's wife? / everyone keeps asking who you are / let them ask," Carol's "still not pregnant"), the convert ("we met last year, what happened / keep it between us"), the offer, "thank me in 28 days." Target: Black woman, lymphatic drainage, weight loss; the hook as a reenactment, then the song.

Format: the hook is acted (two lines each). Then the song, single voice (Lisa), other people's lines reported, the way the bank's songs are cut. Cast: LISA (46), JANET (Kevin's coworker, a friend of the couple who hasn't seen Lisa since last year's party), ANGELA (the old friend, the guide), KEVIN (Lisa's husband). Operator ruling 2026-09-28: it is Kevin's company party. The people there see her once a year, so the mistake comes from someone who knows her and hasn't seen her in a while, and six weeks later the same crowd sees the change. They can still tell it is her; it is an improvement, not a non-recognition. Kevin's beat stays where the source put it, at the second party. Renamed from the source (Carol, Rachel and Mark are all in the register two or three times over).

Mechanism: the drain, with the coworker script's cause in a woman's life (after forty it slows, no pump, twenty years at a desk holding it together shuts it the rest of the way). The source's absolution ("it was never my fault") is kept and is the operator's rule.

## Story logic pass (workflows/story-ad-logic.md)

Chain: the reenactment (the ask, "I'm not pregnant," the dress, "I need some air") → the bathroom overhear → the call → the doctor, "eat less and move more" → the parking lot, Angela, ten years, "better, actually, how" → "the real answer / duh" → "never my fault" → the drain, no pump, after forty, the desk → holds every pound, bloated, doctors don't test → four herbs, the dose, "that's it" → six weeks, Kevin's party again → "that's Kevin's wife? she looks so good," "let them ask" → Janet, "still not pregnant" → the woman from last year, "keep it between us," the recap, the offer, "thank me in six weeks."

What it caught and what I did:
1. **Whose party.** Kevin's company's, both times (operator ruling). The crowd sees her once a year, which is why Janet, a friend of the couple, can be a year behind and congratulate her, and why the same crowd six weeks later registers the change. The company's calendar gives the second party without a reason being invented.
2. **Recognition.** Nobody fails to know her. "That's Kevin's wife? She looks so good" is recognition plus the change, and Kevin's "everyone keeps asking what you did" replaces the source's "who you are." Walmart ruling holds. Kevin's beat is where the source had it; the source gives him nothing at the first party and neither does this.
3. **Checklist 4, the guide.** Angela is living proof after ten years ("better, actually") and had the same thing ("stuck in that same weight circle"). "Do you want the real answer? / Duh" is the locker room's check. Holds.
4. **The accountability rule.** "It was never my fault" is the source's line and it is the rule word for word. The doctor's "you're over forty, it is completely normal" is the wrong authority. The cause is the drain and the desk, not her plate. "I eat clean, I walk every day" is on the record before Angela.
5. **Checklist 5, the parasite logic replaced cleanly.** The source's chain was: they live in your gut, steal nutrients, your body panics and holds every pound, doctors don't test. The drain's chain: it has no pump, it shuts, the waste sits and hardens, your body holds every pound, doctors don't test. Same shape, no gap.
6. **Arithmetic.** Forty-six (the doctor's "over forty"), ten years since Angela, twenty years at the desk, six weeks between parties, "we met last year." Holds. No pound number anywhere; the source had none.
7. **Checklist 3, hooks.** All three are the reenactment and end on "I need some air," then the song. V3 adds her age, paid off by the doctor's "over forty."
8. **The offer.** The source's own: 70 percent off the first bottle, 30-day guarantee, sells out constantly. Thirty days is the source's number; the last four sheets said sixty. Still the account's open number.

Watch:
- Register is light: names only. The ICP is in the faces and the party. If you want one line of it, Angela's "Duh" is already there and "girl" fits after it.
- "Twenty years of holding it together at that desk" is Lisa's cause in Angela's mouth; Angela would know that about her after ten years apart only if it is her own story too ("I was stuck in that same weight circle"). It reads as Angela describing both of them. If it bothers you, "twenty years of holding it together" without the desk.
- The reenactment is two lines each, so the actors' cut is short and the song carries the rest, which is what the operator asked for.

**WHY IT WORKS**

*   **The wound:** the bar won't click in front of the line, and the one person she least wants watching is her grandson.
*   **The hooks:** all three are the same afternoon from three angles. V1 is the scene, V2 is her own line ("Let me suck in, hon"), and V3 is Wyatt's eyes.
*   **The guide:** Bev was on the same route and was heavier. She explains it walking that route, so the cause (we stopped moving) is right there on screen.
*   **The clock:** a real event with the same ride and the same attendant, the state fair six weeks out.
*   **The flip:** "I want to ride with you, Nana" comes back as "I want to ride with you, baby." The bar that wouldn't click clicks. The attendant who sent her off says "There you go, ma'am."

**SUGGESTIONS**

*   **Six weeks instead of a year.** The song went to next year's fair. The drama guidelines want a six-week clock with the same people, so I made the first fair the county fair and the payoff the state fair, with the same traveling ride. To keep the full year, drop the title cards and use seasons. It loses the weekly witnesses.
*   **Bev's coffee became a walk.** The song had coffee at Bev's kitchen table. The guidelines say the helper explains while moving, and walking the old route makes "we stopped moving" visible. The kitchen still works if you'd rather keep it.
*   **"Let me suck in, hon" in V2.** It's the ego hit, in her own voice. If it feels too much, swap it for her pulling the coral blouse down over the bar.

# Brand Voice: the living rubric

The rules Claude grades my copy against, banked one reaction at a time per [[standards-ladder]]. The corrected rules are the most valuable ones. Every entry is two beats: the raw reaction, said once, then the permanent rule.

## The log (banked, dated, permanent)

- 2026-08-21 Reaction: "Never use em dashes." Banked as: no em dashes anywhere, not in copy, not in notes, not in chat. Use a comma, a period, or a colon. Also enforced in the Script's Rules that bite.

## Voice rules
Adopted 2026-08-28 from The Standards Ladder, operator-approved. Unbank any of these the moment one annoys you.
- No vague pronouns. Name the actual noun. If a cold reader could misread "it," "this," or "that," name the thing.
- Show it, don't summarize it. Never name a quality and expect the reader to buy it. Stage the evidence and let them land on the conclusion themselves.
- Hedge the biggest claims. A naked superlative reads as hype and the guard goes up. A small hedge on the same claim makes it believable. The voice stays at a 10, only the claim gets the hedge.
- Break up the triplets. Three parallel items in a row is a strong AI tell. Doublet is the default; a survivor earns its own line.

Adopted 2026-08-28 from Mario's full rulebook, operator-directed ("add Mario's voice into my rubric"). The register rules (street-smart, profanity) are the most Mario-specific; unbank any the moment it stops sounding like you.
- One sentence per line. Shatter the paragraph. Do not cluster. A wall of grey makes the eye quit before it reads a word.
- Serve the reader, not yourself. A line that admires its own cleverness is dead weight. Swap it for what the reader actually gets.
- Street-smart is the register. Grade for real, not articulate.
- Do not neuter the profanity. It is texture, not vulgarity. Never scrub it clean.
- Lead with the human, not the machine. The mechanism is body proof, never the hook.
- Never leave the WHY vague. Cash every claim out into a concrete reader payoff.
- Kill the canned signpost. No "and here's the magic." Let the line land on its own.
- The punch-cap: the single emotional-payoff word of a beat gets CAPS and underline. One per beat, not scattered.

## The log (banked, dated, permanent) — continued

- 2026-08-28 Reaction: "You're going for the mechanism before the consequences." Banked as: every hook must open on the human consequence (what the reader sees, feels, pays, notices). The science, the biology, the ingredient explanation: all of that is body proof. If the first sentence of a hook is a lesson instead of a felt experience, rewrite it. This is the "lead with the human" rule enforced at the hook level, not just as a general principle.
- 2026-08-29 Reaction: "This is super vague. Sounds like a lie." (on "my husband said something for the first time in years") Banked as: every story beat needs a specific scene. A place, a real quote, a physical detail, a time of day. "He said something" is a summary, and summaries sound fake. "He looked at me across the kitchen table and said 'what's different about you'" is a scene, and scenes get believed. If the line could appear in any ad for any product, it is too vague. Rewrite until it belongs to ONE person in ONE moment.

# Lifestyle Legends: editing breakdowns of the reference reels

I downloaded 7 of the top story-about-a-famous-person reels from Sandcastles and went through each one. For every reel I measured:
- every hard cut (scene detection)
- a word-level transcript, for speaking speed and the exact timing of each beat
- a timestamped frame every ~2 s, for layout, captions and graphics
- loudness, and how loud the background bed sits under the voice

Instagram blocked one reference, "Let's Talk About It"; its section comes from Sandcastles' own analysis.

**The common thread**
- **There's never silence.** A music or ambience bed sits 7–10 dB under the voice the whole time.
- **The picture changes about every 1.5–4.5 s.** Even the one-take reels keep the top image moving.
- **The narrator is rarely the picture.** Only the guessing reel keeps the storyteller on screen the whole time.

## A. The Origin Story

### Lens on Legends: Dua Lipa (main reference)
https://www.instagram.com/reel/DYul8wohxIL/ · 735K views · 259× the channel average

**Measured:** 122 s, 286 words, **2.4 words per second**, the slowest of all seven. Every line gets room to land. There are 42 cuts, about one per sentence, and the narrator never appears.

**Beat map**

| Time | Beat | What's said | Picture |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.0 | **Hook**: age plus a bold act | "At 15 years old, she moved to London alone." | Illustrated girl with a suitcase in the London rain. A small boxed name tag, **DUA LIPA**, sits on frame 1 only. |
| 4.3 | Stakes | "Her parents begged her to come home." | Crying in a red phone box |
| 6.5 | Rejections, as fragments | "Modeling agencies said she was too big… voice wasn't special enough." | Portfolio, a closed door |
| 12.3 | **The one-liner beat** | "She stayed anyway." | Held shot |
| 13.6 | Origin | "Dua Lipa was born in London to Albanian parents from Kosovo… between worlds." | Childhood living room, family table |
| 26.2 | Turning point (back to the hook) | "When she was 13… moved back to Kosovo… At 15… moving back to London alone." | Kosovo street, rain on a window, packing a suitcase |
| 39.2 | The grind, as a list | "Worked as a waitress… Too tall, too curvy, wrong look, wrong sound." | Café, a brutalist London street |
| 51.8 | Persistence | "Kept writing, kept recording… rejected her again and again." | Mic on a pile of books, laptop screen full of DECLINED |
| 63.5 | **The break** | "Then finally, at 18, Warner Music signed her." | Studio with the WB logo |
| 68–89 | **The payoff flurry** | "New Rules… hit after hit… Three Grammy Awards. More than 50 million records sold." | **16 cuts in 5.3 s** (81.4–86.7 s), Grammy statue, stadium |
| 89.5 | **The detail nobody tells** | "She went back for something she thought she had lost… taught herself Albanian." | Recording booth, Kosovo hills, hugging her grandmother |
| 103.4 | **Callback to the hook** | "…when she boarded that plane alone at 15." | The airport and plane again |
| 108.0 | Moral | "She built everything, then went back for herself." | A silhouette in a spotlight |
| 111.8 | **Branded outro**, about 10 s | "Follow Lens on Legends for more untold stories…" | Animated gold "LENS ON LEGENDS" logo |

**The edit**
- **Visuals:** AI-illustrated scenes in a Pixar style, each gently animated (rain, steam, parallax), with one new scene per sentence.
- **Captions:** heavy uppercase, only 1–3 words at a time, about 80% down the frame, with **one keyword per caption in bright green**.
- **Audio:** voice over a music bed about 10 dB lower, mastered to -14 LUFS with a very even level (LRA 2.6).

### Jon Favreau (winning.therapy)
https://www.instagram.com/reel/DDSdkJGoVRs/ · 5.8M views · 556×

**The version Instagram served me is a text post.**
- A tweet-style card (avatar, name, verified tick) holds the whole story in about 140 words, with the key sentence in bold.
- Under it plays a clip of Favreau in his own film, with music only.
- It's 8 s long, but nobody can read 140 words in 8 s, so people pause and rewatch. Watch time ends up longer than the video.

Sandcastles' analysis of the voiced version: 26 s, three acts. "In 1993, Jon Favreau landed a small but memorable role in *Rudy*, thinking it would be his big break. But…" → stuck → wrote *Swingers* → career.

**What to take:** any Origin Story can be reposted as a text post with zero filming. The 556× is the best ratio of reach to effort in the whole data set.

### Alan Bond (glasshouse.aus)
https://www.instagram.com/reel/DdpupJ_BinY/ · 58K views · 67×

**Measured:** 169 s, 2.5 words per second, and only one hard cut. Everything animates onto a black "dossier" page instead:
- small monospace section headers ("THE TITLE", "FREMANTLE", "THE COLLAPSE")
- archive photos in thin frames with tiny captions
- **years and dollar figures set huge** ("$1,800,000,000")
- a red stamp reading "IT NEVER HAPPENED."

**What to take:** numbers can be the picture. When the story hits a figure, put the figure on screen big.

### Let's Talk About It (sydney.yancy.ugc), from Sandcastles' analysis
https://www.instagram.com/reel/DaWEza9y-Dg/ · 6.0M views · **3,241×** · 10% engagement

**The format**
- **Picture:** the creator over a collage of the subject (greenscreen), about 2 min long.
- **Hook:** "Jordan Howlett, more popularly known as Jordan the Stallion. He's 28 years old and easily one of the most recognizable content creators on the planet."
- **Personal stake:** "Last week I learned his story and I can't stop thinking about it."
- **Series line, at 12–20 s:** "This is Let's Talk About It, a series where I share the unique stories of people who inspire me."
- **Story:** origin → setback → pivot → proof → the lesson for you.

**What to take:** say the series name out loud, and say why the story matters to *you*.

### Rules for our Origin Story
1. **The first sentence is the most surprising fact, with an age or a year**, said in under 4 s.
2. **The series line comes by 8 s.**
3. **Use short declarative sentences** (Lens averages about 7 words), fragments for lists of rejections, and a lone one-liner after a hard beat ("She stayed anyway.").
4. **Put up a new picture every sentence** (2.5–4 s), with a **flurry** of quick panels over the payoff numbers.
5. **Name tag on frame 1.** In our editor, that's the first pill label.
6. **"The part nobody tells you"** comes at about 80% of the runtime.
7. **The moral calls back to the hook image.**
8. **An 8–10 s branded outro** with a spoken follow CTA and a tease of the next legend.
9. **Deliver slowly**, about 2.4–2.5 words per second, under a music bed.

## B. Guess the Legend

### Guess the footballer (jackgrimse), main reference
https://www.instagram.com/reel/DdjfzUURsA3/ · 97K views · 12×

**Measured:** 62.5 s, 191 words, **3.1 words per second with zero pauses over half a second**. The whole thing is one breathless present-tense run-on. There are 13 hard cuts, and the top image changes on every clue.

**Layout** (the base for our split screen)
- A thin crumpled-paper strip across the top.
- A **top panel (~42%)**: a collage of the clue, with club badges, emoji stickers (😢), a big green arrow pointing at the pixelated player, and stock-chart graphics for relegation and promotion.
- A **blue pill** at the bottom of the panel, in bold italic capitals, paraphrasing the clue ("EARNED A SCHOLARSHIP AT MANCHESTER CITY").
- **Captions** in uppercase bold italic white with a black stroke, sitting on the seam.
- The **presenter** talking to camera in the bottom half, on a themed set (shelves of football memorabilia, a half-and-half club shirt).
- **The player's face is pixelated in every photo** until the reveal.

**Beat map**

| Time | Beat |
|---|---|
| 0.0 | "Can you imagine experiencing this footballer's rollercoaster career?" with the pill **GUESS THE FOOTBALLER 🤔** |
| 4–55 | Clues in date order, one image and one pill per clue, with no pauses |
| **56.1** | The reveal: "By now, you probably know that this is the story of… **Jamie Tartt**" (59.5 s), at **90% of the runtime** |
| 60.2 | "Comment below when you figured it out." Full-screen clips of the character play out to the end. |

**The twist:** the subject turns out to be a fictional TV character (Ted Lasso), so the reveal recasts everything before it. Ours is a real famous name, and the payoff is the same "oh!" moment.

### Rules for Guess the Legend
1. **Present tense, no pauses, one run-on sentence.**
2. **One clue = one picture = one pill.**
3. **Pixelate faces and any give-away titles** until the reveal.
4. **Reveal at 85–90% of the runtime**, then show the same photo unpixelated.
5. **The CTA is "comment when you got it"**, not "follow". Comments are the whole point of this format.
6. 45–60 s.

## C. In Their Footsteps

### Stevenson Trail (heleneinbetween), main reference
https://www.tiktok.com/@heleneinbetween/video/7646771334520769805 · 578K views · 65× · **11% engagement**

**Measured:** 99 s, 325 words, 3.3 words per second, and **67 cuts, a new shot every 1.5 s**, the fastest edit of all seven. She's on camera about 25% of the time.

**Beat map**

| Time | Beat | Picture |
|---|---|---|
| 0–6 | Selfie hook in a gorge: "This is totally normal in Europe and doesn't even exist in the USA." | **Big white serif title with a shadow**: "Walking 150+ miles solo on France's Stevenson Trail" |
| 7–23 | How it works: no tent, bag transfer, village inns | Fast B-roll, including the street sign **"Rue Robert-Louis Stevenson"** at 13.5 s |
| 23–58 | The senses: bakeries, poppies, medieval villages, five-course dinners | 1–2 s shots of food, landscape and her walking away from camera |
| 61–67 | Back on camera: "I loved long-distance walking so much…" | Selfie, beaming |
| **85–90** | **The legend's landmark**: "…the same village where Robert Louis Stevenson ended his own journey in 1878. Standing there, I couldn't believe what I'd done." | She stands at his memorial fountain in Saint-Jean-du-Gard |
| 92–99 | Her own transformation: "Two years ago I'd never call myself a hiker… lost 60 pounds… That's the power of choosing yourself." | Selfie, arms up |

**The edit**
- **Captions:** small white bold text with a shadow, about 73% down, two lines.
- **Audio:** natural sound (-21.5 LUFS), with ambience under the voice and no heavy mastering.
- **The key move:** Stevenson is the *reason* for the trip, not the subject. She names him early, then pays him off at his landmark near the end.

### Hever Castle (history_alice)
https://www.tiktok.com/@history_alice/video/7672324997637082390 · 471K views · 13× · **10% engagement**

**Measured:** 105 s, 2.9 words per second, 30 cuts (3.4 s).

**The room does the hooking.** It opens on the empty bedchamber with "This is one of the most important rooms in Tudor English history." She doesn't appear until **19.7 s**.

**The edit**
- **Evidence shots:** Anne Boleyn's portrait (24 s), Henry VIII's portrait (54 s), and the real Book of Hours in its case (41–50 s).
- **Gentle reenactment:** she looks out of the leaded window (33 s and 85–90 s) while she says Anne "would have been reading in here".
- **Captions:** TikTok-native, small white text on black boxes, two lines, about 70% down.
- **Audio:** natural, -24 LUFS.

### Harry DuBois (sethmansfield)
https://www.instagram.com/reel/DcEVwdNxIA5/ · 34K views · 14×

**Measured:** 73 s, 3.3 words per second, 26 cuts (2.7 s).

**Hook:** a stack of accomplishments, "He saved shipwreck survivors, farmed pineapples, had 600 beehives, and has one of the oldest houses still standing in Palm Beach County", under a **big title** for 5 s: "Most Interesting Man in Jupiter".

**The visual toolkit**
- an animated old map with a red dashed route (7.6 s)
- **AI-illustrated scenes** for moments nobody photographed (rowing into a storm, 10.6 s)
- archive photos with a **handwritten arrow** ("Harry Dubois", 16.6 s)
- an infographic map reading **"600+ HIVES"**
- portraits stamped with the year ("1898")
- then-vs-now drone shots of the house

**Structure**
- The creator appears only at 59–62 s, walking out of the house: "at DuBois Park in Jupiter".
- It closes on a **next-week tease** over a drone sunset: "Next week, we uncover the man who bet his fortune on an empty island and never got to see it built."

### Rules for In Their Footsteps
1. **Open in the real place under a big title** (5–6 s) that states the mission.
2. **The hook is about the experience, and the legend is the reason**: "because of a book", "where Cousteau first put his face in the sea", "one rule for a day".
3. **B-roll carries the video:** a new shot every 1.5–3 s. You're on camera about 25% of the time: at the hook, at the emotional beats, and at the end.
4. **Show the evidence:** the plaque, the street sign, the museum, the object in its case.
5. **End at the legend's landmark**, then say what doing it changed for you.
6. **Tease the next episode.**
7. **Keep the natural sound.** The two best performers sat at -21 and -24 LUFS, because Instagram turns everything to the same level anyway.

## What this means for the editor (`edit_legend.py`)

**Already built**
- The split screen from the guessing reel: top panel, blue pill, captions on the seam, talking head underneath.
- Full-screen B-roll and full-screen you.
- Pixelation, with the clean photo shown at the reveal.
- Highlighted keywords and numbers.
- The branded end card.
- Ducked music.

**Added from this breakdown:** the **opening title** for In Their Footsteps (`"title"` in a plan), the big white text over the first seconds, as the Stevenson and DuBois reels use.

**Not automated:** animated maps, hand-drawn arrows, stickers and AI-illustrated scenes. Bake them into the image or clip you use as a panel. Illustrated scenes, the Lens on Legends look, can be generated for moments with no photo, but don't make photoreal fakes of real people.

**Two easy formats on top of the three**
- **Text post (Favreau, 556×):** reuse any Origin Story script as a tweet-style card over a clip. Zero filming.
- **Payoff flurry (Lens):** in a plan, anchor 3–5 panels to consecutive words in the payoff line ("Three… Oscar… fifty…") to get the rapid-cut moment.

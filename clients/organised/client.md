# Organised

UK "ancestral nutrition" DTC brand (CV calls it "£10M+"). Anton was its in-house Creative Director from Aug 2024 to May 2026 (sole copywriter, two video editors, everything except media buying). He came back as an agency client on 2026-10-05 and is writing and briefing Meta ads again. Founder: **Niall** (named as "Person A (Niall / Founder)" in the Timeline of Doom brief, and he has his own "Niall Kiddle - Feedback Loops" doc, so likely Niall Kiddle (unconfirmed)).

## What they sell

- **One product, one SKU**: the Organised pouch, a powdered whole-food blend you whisk into food or drink. The Product Doc says the team treats this as "a non-negotiable that they will never launch new products or flavours".
- **Ingredients** ("8 whole food ingredients, zero artificial ingredients", per the review analysis): beef protein, collagen (hydrolysed bovine peptides), bovine colostrum, 5 grass-fed / pasture-raised beef organs (liver, heart, kidney, lung, spleen; freeze-dried), raw (Manuka) honey, dates and maple syrup. Ad line: "absolutely nothing synthetic".
- **Positioning**: replaces "three categories at once: multivitamin + collagen + colostrum/immunity". Built for the whole family: kids, pregnant or breastfeeding mums, picky eaters (including ARFID cases from reviews).
- **Usage**: one scoop a day in milk, a smoothie, pancake batter, yoghurt, or savoury food (ragus, broths, soups). Taste claim: "no smell" and it "tastes just like the sweet milk at the bottom of a cereal bowl". "One pouch lasts a month."
- **Offer**: a 100-day risk-free money-back guarantee, "even if the pouch is completely empty". Oct 2026 briefs add "a free whisk and a bottle" with the first order. Price per pouch or subscription: unknown.
- **Social proof figures used in ads**: "Over 100,000 families" or "100,000 people" (May 2026 briefs), "Over 200,000 families" (Timeline of Doom, Oct 2026). Anton's CV says "197,000+ customers". These need checking before use.

## Who buys

- **Core avatar: a parent who wants their kids to be healthy.** The core-avatars doc (Mar 2026) says "6 of your top 14 scripts target this avatar". It calls the motivation "survival-level".
- **Sub-avatars from briefs and research**:
  - parent of a picky eater ("child falling below weight", "feels frustrated or guilty", "tired mum")
  - parent of young kids who eat mostly processed food and show low-grade symptoms ("tiredness, frequent colds, dark circles"; "feels guilty but stuck")
  - mum of a child with behavioural or brain-development concerns ("autism mum")
  - women with low iron or ferritin (hair shedding, split nails, pale skin, tiredness)
  - pregnant and breastfeeding mums
  - gut-health buyers
- **Demographics** (ChatGPT-generated avatar sheet, Jan 2026, so treat as unconfirmed): aged 30–55, about 85% female, UK first with the US as an expansion target, household income £60k–150k+.
- **What buyers say** (review analysis of 362 Trustpilot reviews, Mar 2026): taste is the strongest reason they choose and stay, at 141 mentions (39.0%). Price comes up even among happy customers: "The only reason I rate 4 stars is because it is quite expensive".

## What the ads say

These angles and mechanisms have run or are in briefs:
- **Founder story / family.** The founder cooked for his four picky younger brothers (ages 3, 5, 7, 10) and sneaked organs into their food. This is the account's proven frame. The best-known hook is "I cooked a meal for my four picky-eater little brothers to trick them into eating one of the world's healthiest foods".
- **Anti-synthetic reframe.** Synthetic or non-heme iron has "around a 5% absorption rate". The rest "passes straight through" or "feeds bad bacteria", causing constipation and tummy aches. The fix is "a blood-building system": organs (heme iron, which "absorbs 7 times better") plus colostrum. The May 2026 version also cited "spleen = 72% heme iron, liver = Nobel Prize 1934".
- **External villain / not your fault.** The company behind Marlboro bought Kraft and General Foods and engineered a "bliss point" into kids' food (Tobacco / Bliss Point ad).
- **Ancestral.** "Our great-grandparents worked this out a hundred years ago: they fed children organs."
- **Whole food vs isolate.** "Whole foods have up to 26,000 compounds versus about 20 in an isolated supplement" (Hong Kong).
- **Deficiency timeline.** At age 3 a deficiency looks like "a fussy eater". By 5–7 it shows up at school. By 10 "It becomes who they are". Opens on "56% of children are missing a key nutrient" (Timeline of Doom, Oct 2026).
- **Symptom ID.** "This is iron deficiency" over hair clumps, split nails and a pale face (Oct 2026).
- **Founder apology / "we lied"** (reverse-psychology bottom-of-funnel format): "We need to publicly apologise to everyone who bought Organised for their gut, because we lied to you" (BOF Founder Brief, Oct 2026).
- **Proof devices**: real customer messages and reviews on screen, e.g. "like going from Mr. Hyde to Dr. Jekyll", "Sarah said her ferritin levels went from 17 to 30", and the Kaya, autism-mum and Laura reviews.
- **Every ad ends on** the sneak-it-in usage demo, the ingredient stack, social proof, and the 100-day guarantee, with "Tap the link below to try your first pouch".

## Voice and rules

- Tone: "Serious, empathetic and conversational; direct eye contact" (Timeline of Doom brief). Talk "like you're chatting with a friend".
- Lift guilt off the parent. The best angles hand them an external villain ("not your fault"). A plain "your kids eat junk" message makes parents defensive.
- Use UK spelling and words: Organised, mum, colour, yoghurt, "every penny back".
- Keep it anti-synthetic and pro-whole-food. Never call the product a supplement pill. Lead with "one scoop", "whisk it in" and "it disappears completely".
- Show real proof. Use original customer review screenshots with names and highlighted passages, not paraphrases. Mark second-hand results as "use with care" (W1 notes).
- Brief format (Oct 2026): 1 script + 3 hooks per brief, filmed 9:16 for Facebook and Instagram ads and the brand IG. Script and B-roll go in separate files. Each line gets pop-up image direction. Deadline is 5 days after brief.
- Claims to check before running (unconfirmed substantiation): "56% of children are missing a key nutrient", "~5% absorption" of synthetic iron, "feeds bad bacteria like E. coli and salmonella", colostrum "raised iron levels better than iron tablets" in studies, "7 times better" heme absorption, and the customer counts. No source covers UK ASA/CAP health-claim rules.

- **Noble Origins "All-in-One Beef Protein"** (US): beef protein isolate plus "only 250 mg of organs+colostrum per serving", flavoured with stevia.

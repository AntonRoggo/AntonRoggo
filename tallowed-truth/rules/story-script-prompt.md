# Prompt: turn a customer story into a teleprompter script

Use this to write a third-party age-reaction ("someone noticed her") ad script from one real customer story.
Paste everything below the line, then the story (her review or message, word for word, with her name,
age and the date). It works in StefanBrain, the company Claude project, or any chat.

---

Write a teleprompter script for a Meta video ad for F-Balm by The Tallowed Truth, built from the real
customer story below.

## Who reads it and how

- Spoken by Ben, the founder, straight to camera, passing the story on like a message he just got
  ("We got a review the other day from...", "This lady reached out to us..."). For a creator, the same
  script works with "they" in place of "we" for the product lines.
- Teleprompter style: only the words that are read out, one short sentence or phrase per line, written
  the way people talk. Blank line between beats. Cues go in [brackets] on their own line, and only these:
  [HOLD THE TIN UP] at the bridge line and [END ON THE TIN] at the end.
- 75 to 130 seconds read aloud (about 200 to 340 words). Short beats, no wind-up.

## The shape: three acts, ten beats (from our 16 winning story scripts)

Act 1, the story (first line to about 37%):
1. **Open on the payoff.** The first line says the end of the story: who noticed, what they said, and/or
   her age. ("She got ID'd at 73 years old." "This lady shocked her esthetician when she was 60.")
   Her age is a number. Never open on the setup.
2. **Where it came from.** One line: a review, a message, an email. ("We got a review the other day from
   this lady.")
3. **The scene, then the reaction quoted.** A few lines with small real details from her words, then the
   other person's words as a direct quote. Never sum it up as "she got a compliment".
4. **Her details**, only the ones she gave: her age as a punchline after the reaction, how long she used
   it, what she stopped using.
   Finish the story before the product comes in. Never go back to the story after the bridge.

Act 2, why F-Balm works (about 37% to 80%):
5. **Bridge in one line** that points back to her: "This is the product she was talking about." [HOLD THE TIN UP]
6. **Why ours works: how we make it.** In-house, small batch, Canadian grass-fed tallow, triple purified,
   not bleached or deodorized, not made in China. Five ingredients: tallow, organic cold-pressed olive oil,
   Canadian beeswax, Ontario raw honey, frankincense. Sourcing and making, not skin science.
7. **What it replaces.** One tin against Botox, a $300 ten-step routine, half her products. A pea-sized
   amount on damp skin, morning or night.
8. **Reviews as proof.** "Over 20,000 five-star reviews. Read them yourself, don't take my word for it."
   Naming the doubt helps ("this sounds crazy", "no guarantees").

Act 3, the close (last 15 to 20%):
9. **Money-back guarantee tied to her story.** "Use the whole tin. If [her payoff] doesn't happen for you,
   we'll give you a full refund." 30-day money-back guarantee. Say "risk-free" or "no downside", never "free".
10. **Link.** "Hit the link below." End on the tin.

## Rules

- Her words, her age and what happened stay exactly as she wrote them. Don't add details she didn't give
  (a place, a name, a number). Quote the reaction only as she reported it.
- Results are her story, not a promise to the viewer: "you might get compliments", "no guarantees".
- No medical claims (heal, cure, treat, eczema, acne).
- Facts (checked on the product page, Oct 29 2026): one 2 oz tin is $49 (under $50), lasts two to three
  months used daily; 30-day money-back guarantee; F-Balm has over 21,900 five-star Judge.me reviews, so
  "over 20,000 five-star reviews". Don't quote a sale, bundle or discount.
- Also write the hook text box, shown on screen for the first 3 to 5 seconds, following `rules/tos-hook-rules.md`:
  3 to 7 words, under 35 characters, a third party plus her exact age, ending in a colon, and not the same words
  as the spoken first line ("Esthetician shocked by 64 year old's skin:").

## Before you hand it over, check

- First line gives the payoff. Her age is a number.
- One line on where the story came from.
- The reaction is a direct quote in the other person's words.
- The story is finished before the product comes in.
- At least two of: how we make it, reviews, the guarantee. The guarantee comes after the reviews.
- No detail, number or claim that isn't in her story or the facts above.

# Creative learnings

What we've learned from testing TTT ads. Each point says where it came from. Add to this after every
weekly "what's working" call: one short, general rule per learning, not a note about one ad.

## How to test

- **One change per ad.** Keep the words of the script the same and change only one thing: delivery,
  order, setting, prop or who says it. Then the result can be read. (Ads Arcadia call, 2026-10-23)
- **Delivery, not message.** Don't rewrite a review to make it spicier; make the delivery better.
  (Ads Arcadia call, 2026-10-23)

## What works

- **Props win.** Any ad with a real prop beats the same ad without one, e.g. holding a real photo of
  the grandmother being talked about. A real photo is proof, so use it. (Ads Arcadia call, 2026-10-23)
- **Show the product early.** In a review-based ad there's no point hiding the tin; lead with it.
  Test: grandma's photo on the left, the tin on the right, in the first frame. (Ads Arcadia call, 2026-10-23)
- **Ordinary stories are fine.** These stories aren't dramatic. Win on delivery. (Ads Arcadia call, 2026-10-23)
- **Lead with the strangest true detail.** E.g. "My grandma put beef fat on her face every night."
  as the hook. (Ads Arcadia call, 2026-10-23, test 4)
- **Pop the proof in the first 2 seconds.** The person or photo the hook is about pops up on screen,
  with burned-in captions. (F-Balm briefs, editor note)
- **Third-party age reactions win. Hook on the payoff.** Stories where someone else reacted to her skin or
  her age (a stranger guessed her age, she got carded, a dermatologist, esthetician, doctor or plastic surgeon
  asked what she uses) carried the week. Open on the payoff: who noticed, what they said, or her age. 15 of 16
  winners open this way; never open on the setup. Put her review on screen as she's quoted. Ready-made
  stories with hooks and screenshots: `reviews/stories/age-reactions.md`. (Anton, week of 2026-10-26)

## What the account says (last 30 days to 2026-10-27, see `ads/whats-working.md`)

- **Ben on camera is the most efficient big spender.** Founder ads: CA$40 per purchase vs CA$57 for
  creator ads, on 26% of spend. Best: "come clean" founder confession about bad (Chinese) tallow,
  CA$36 on CA$44K.
- **Review-story hooks scale.** The biggest creator ads are customer reviews told to camera:
  "Esthetician shocked", "Carded at 73", "Nicest skin ever seen", "Noticed at 64". Pull the review
  from `testimonials/screenshots.csv` / `reviews/stories/authority.md` and pop it on screen.
- **Cheapest proven angles:** "Stop buying tallow" (CA$27), "$900 French creams" (CA$29),
  "Founder 4" (CA$29), static before/afters (CA$29-35), "under eyes" (CA$34). Iterate these.
- **Smoker's lines is expensive to scale** (CA$65-66 for two creators). Use it as a secondary story,
  not the main hook, until a version beats CA$49.

## What the comments say (see `comments/`)

- **Top doubts:** greasy, smells like cow, price, "doesn't work / no difference", breakouts. Answer
  one inside the script (e.g. "I thought it'd be greasy... you only need a pea-sized bit on damp skin").
- **"Too fast to be true" backfires.** Viewers call out instant results ("her mom just applied it and
  the daughter already sees a difference? Come on"). Show a timeline (weeks) instead of a same-day
  miracle, and use a pea-sized amount on camera.
- **Show the routine right.** People keep explaining "damp face, pea-sized, warm it in your fingers"
  to each other. Show that on camera; it heads off the greasy complaint.
- **People ask for before/afters.** It's one of the most common requests under the ads.

## Filming rules every brief repeats

- Screen-free filming: creators never film phones, tablets or computer screens. Editors add reviews,
  screenshots, messages, photos and websites as pop-ups in the edit.
- Film the script and the B-roll as separate files. 9:16, HDR off, clean lens, room around you.
- Super casual and conversational, like talking to a friend. Not word for word; same message.
